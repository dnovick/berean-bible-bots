### III. Grammatical Commentary

**1 Kings 8:55**

**וַיַּעְמֹד** — The Pathach preformative vowel appears in the Qal Imperfect with Type 2, I-Guttural verbs (16.2, 16.3.2). This same preformative vowel also appears in the Hiphil Imperfect (26.5). The stem vowel will distinguish between the Qal and Hiphil (26.6.1). In the Qal, the stem vowel is normally o-class or a-class. In the Hiphil, the stem vowel is normally i-class or e-class.

**וַיְבָרֶךְ** — The Shewa under the Imperfect preformative is diagnostic of the Piel stem. The guttural (ר) in second root position rejects the Daghesh Forte of the Piel and causes the expected Pathach (short a) under the first root consonant to lengthen to Qamets (long a). This is called compensatory lengthening (31.9–10).

**קוֹל גָּדוֹל** — The expression קוֹל גָּדוֹל is an unmarked adverbial modifier, best translated as "with a loud voice" or "in a loud voice." Unmarked modifiers of this type frequently occur at the end of a clause and the preposition added to the English translation must be supplied from context.

**1 Kings 8:56**

**בָּרוּךְ יְהוָה** — The divine name functions as the subject, and the Participle functions as the predicate adjective, "Yahweh is blessed" or "Blessed be Yahweh."

**לְעַמּוֹ** — There are three parts to this prepositional phrase: the preposition לְ, the object of the preposition עַם, and a 3ms pronominal suffix, "to his people." The Daghesh Forte in the מ is occasioned by the fact that the noun עַם was originally a Geminate noun (4.8.4). When nouns of this type are pluralized, the consonant that originally appeared twice will now be written once with Daghesh Forte. The Daghesh Forte represents the lost Geminate consonant.

**דְּבָרוֹ הַטּוֹב** — The attributive adjective הַטּוֹב modifies the nominal construction דְּבָרוֹ (his word). Attributive adjectives normally follow the nouns they modify and agree in gender, number, and definiteness (7.5.1). In this case, the 3ms pronominal suffix (וֹ) renders the masculine singular noun דָּבָר definite (10.2.3), "his good word."

**1 Kings 8:57**

**יְהִי** — When an Imperfect verb (without the Waw Consecutive) appears as the first word in its clause, it is normally volitional (23.6.3), "May Yahweh our God be." Remember that with III-ה, Biconsonantal, and Hiphil verbs the spelling of the volitional Imperfect 3ms may be a shortened form of the verb, just like the form of the consecutive Imperfect (17.3.2, 23.12.2). With the verb היה (to be), the regular (long) form is יִהְיֶה and the short form is יְהִי or וַיְהִי (with Waw Consecutive).

**אֲבֹתֵינוּ** — The masculine noun אָב (father) takes a feminine plural ending as in אָבוֹת (4.7.2). In this case, the plural ending is defectively spelled (4.7.5) before the lcp (Type 2) pronominal suffix, "our fathers."

**יַעַזְבֵנוּ** — Once again, the Pathach preformative vowel appears in the Qal Imperfect with Type 2, I-Guttural verbs (16.2, 16.3.2). See the note for וַיַּעְמֹד in 1 Kgs 8:55.

**יִטְּשֵׁנוּ** — The verbal root for this form is נטשׁ (to forsake). The נ has assimilated into the second consonant of the verbal root and is represented by Daghesh Forte (16.9.1). This verb also appears with a 1cp pronominal suffix (ֵנוּ), "he will [not] forsake us."

**1 Kings 8:58**

**לְהַטּוֹת** — The verbal root נטה is doubly weak, both I-נ and III-ה. The נ has assimilated into the second consonant of the verbal root and is represented by Daghesh Forte. With the Infinitive Construct, III-ה verbs end in וֹת (20.4). The preposition לְ appears regularly with the Infinitive Construct and the הַ prefix is diagnostic of the Hiphil stem. The expression לְהַטּוֹת לְבָבֵנוּ "to stretch our heart" may also be translated as "to incline" or "to turn" our heart.

**לָלֶכֶת** — The verbal root is הלך, but this verb inflects like a I-י verb. In the Infinitive Construct, I-י verbs drop the י in first root position, add ת to the end of the verb, and follow the Segholate pattern of spelling (לֶ֫כֶת). The preposition לְ appears regularly with the Infinitive Construct, "to walk."

**צִוָּה** — The Hireq under the first root consonant and the Daghesh Forte in the second root consonant are the two diagnostic features of the Piel Perfect. In the absence of a Perfect sufformative, this form is 3ms from צוה. In this form, the consonant ו appears with the Daghesh Forte (וּ). Do not confuse this combination with the unchangeable long vowel Shureq (also spelled וּ).

# BBH Chapter 9 — Hebrew Pronominal Suffixes

*Source: Pratico & Van Pelt, Basics of Biblical Hebrew Grammar (BBH), Chapter 9*

---

## 9.1 Introduction (§9.1)

In the last chapter we studied the subjective independent personal pronouns (he, she, we). In this chapter we study pronouns that can be either **possessive** (his, her, our) or **objective** (him, her, us). In Hebrew, these pronouns appear as **suffixes** on nouns, prepositions, and the definite direct object marker — they are called **pronominal suffixes**.

- On nouns: possessive — "his book," "her wisdom"
- On prepositions or the direct object marker: objective — "to them," "for them," "them"

---

## 9.2 The Pronominal Suffixes (§9.2)

There are two sets of pronominal suffixes: **Type 1** and **Type 2**. Both types have the same possessive and objective translation values.

| PGN | Type 1 | Type 2 | Translation (Possessive/Objective) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3ms | וֹ | ָיו | his (its) / him (it) |
| 3fs | ָהּ | ֶ֫יהָ | her (its) / her (it) |
| 2ms | ךָ | ֶ֫יךָ | your / you |
| 2fs | ךְ | ַ֫יִךְ | your / you |
| 1cs | ִי | ַי | my / me |
| 3mp | הֶם | ֵיהֶם | their / them |
| 3fp | הֶן | ֵיהֶן | their / them |
| 2mp | כֶם | ֵיכֶם | your / you |
| 2fp | כֶן | ֵיכֶן | your / you |
| 1cp | נוּ | ֵ֫ינוּ | our / us |

---

## 9.3 Notes on the Pronominal Suffixes (§9.3)

1. With few exceptions, **Type 1 suffixes occur with singular nouns** and **Type 2 suffixes occur with plural nouns**.
2. All pronominal suffixes have person, gender, and number. Gender is distinguished in all second and third person forms. First person singular and plural forms are **common** (not inflected for gender).
3. The presentation moves from third person to second person to first person, singular then plural.
4. All Type 2 suffixes have **י** as part of their spelling — this distinguishes them from Type 1 suffixes.
5. The dot in the Type 1 3fs suffix (הּ) is called **Mappiq**. It is neither Daghesh Lene nor Daghesh Forte (both of which are impossible in ה). Mappiq frequently marks the 3fs suffix.
6. Several **alternate forms** of the Type 1 suffixes must be memorized:

| PGN | Type 1 | Alternate |
|---|---|---|
| 3ms | וֹ | הוּ |
| 3fs | ָהּ | הָ |
| 1cs | ִי | נִי |
| 3mp | הֶם | ָם |
| 3fp | הֶן | ָן |

---

## 9.4 Masculine Nouns with Pronominal Suffixes (§9.4)

Pronominal suffixes on nouns express possession. Singular nouns take Type 1 suffixes; plural nouns take Type 2 suffixes. Paradigm: סוּס (horse).

| PGN | Noun ms (Type 1) | Translation | Noun mp (Type 2) | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3ms | סוּסוֹ | his horse | סוּסָיו | his horses |
| 3fs | סוּסָהּ | her horse | סוּסֶ֫יהָ | her horses |
| 2ms | סוּסְךָ | your horse | סוּסֶ֫יךָ | your horses |
| 2fs | סוּסֵךְ | your horse | סוּסַ֫יִךְ | your horses |
| 1cs | סוּסִי | my horse | סוּסַי | my horses |
| 3mp | סוּסָם | their horse | סוּסֵיהֶם | their horses |
| 3fp | סוּסָן | their horse | סוּסֵיהֶן | their horses |
| 2mp | סוּסְכֶם | your horse | סוּסֵיכֶם | your horses |
| 2fp | סוּסְכֶן | your horse | סוּסֵיכֶן | your horses |
| 1cp | סוּסֵ֫נוּ | our horse | סוּסֵ֫ינוּ | our horses |

---

## 9.5 Notes on Masculine Nouns with Pronominal Suffixes (§9.5)

1. Singular nouns can take both singular and plural pronominal suffixes; plural nouns likewise. The distinction is the number of the pronominal suffix, not the noun.
2. When pronominal suffixes are added to masculine **plural** nouns, the masculine plural ending (ִים) is **dropped**. The noun is recognizable as plural only by the Type 2 suffix.
3. If a feminine noun takes a masculine plural ending (§4.7), it continues to act like a masculine noun when taking pronominal suffixes (e.g., עִיר → עָרִים → עָרֵיכֶם, your cities).
4. Note the important distinction between the 1cs suffixes on singular and plural nouns: סוּסִי (my horse, Hireq Yod) vs. סוּסַי (my horses, Pathach Yod).
5. Alternate endings appear in the forms with 3mp suffix (סוּסָם) and 3fp suffix (סוּסָן).
6. Masculine nouns ending in ֶה frequently drop this ending before pronominal suffixes: שָׂדֶה → שָׂדְךָ (your field), שָׂדִי (my field). Recognize that the ֶה ending has been dropped when locating these nouns in a lexicon.

---

## 9.6 Feminine Nouns with Pronominal Suffixes (§9.6)

Feminine nouns also take Type 1 suffixes in the singular and Type 2 in the plural. Paradigm: תּוֹרָה (law).

| PGN | Noun fs (Type 1) | Translation | Noun fp (Type 2) | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3ms | תּוֹרָתוֹ | his law | תּוֹרוֹתָיו | his laws |
| 3fs | תּוֹרָתָהּ | her law | תּוֹרוֹתֶ֫יהָ | her laws |
| 2ms | תּוֹרָֽתְךָ | your law | תּוֹרוֹתֶ֫יךָ | your laws |
| 2fs | תּוֹרָתֵךְ | your law | תּוֹרוֹתַ֫יִךְ | your laws |
| 1cs | תּוֹרָתִי | my law | תּוֹרוֹתַי | my laws |
| 3mp | תּוֹרָתָם | their law | תּוֹרוֹתֵיהֶם | their laws |
| 3fp | תּוֹרָתָן | their law | תּוֹרוֹתֵיהֶן | their laws |
| 2mp | תּוֹרַתְכֶם | your law | תּוֹרוֹתֵיכֶם | your laws |
| 2fp | תּוֹרַתְכֶן | your law | תּוֹרוֹתֵיכֶן | your laws |
| 1cp | תּוֹרָתֵ֫נוּ | our law | תּוֹרוֹתֵ֫ינוּ | our laws |

---

## 9.7 Notes on Feminine Nouns with Pronominal Suffixes (§9.7)

1. When a feminine singular noun ending in ָה (e.g., תּוֹרָה) receives a pronominal suffix, the **ה is replaced by ת**: תּוֹרָה → תּוֹרָת. The ת appears in every form of the feminine singular paradigm. Failure to recognize this ת as indicating a feminine singular noun ending in ָה will prevent identification of its lexical form.
2. Unlike masculine plural nouns, **feminine plural nouns retain their plural ending** (וֹת). Both the ending and the Type 2 suffix confirm the plural.

---

## 9.8 Vowel Changes in Nouns with Pronominal Suffixes (§9.8)

Most nouns experience vowel changes when pronominal suffixes are added:

| Lexical Form | → | Noun with Suffix | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| דָּבָר | → | דְּבָרִי | my word |
| דָּבָר | → | דִּבְרֵיהֶן | their (fp) words |
| עֶ֫בֶד | → | עַבְדּוֹ | his servant |
| בַּ֫יִת | → | בֵּיתְךָ | your (ms) house |
| מָ֫וֶת | → | מוֹתוֹ | his death |

The basic **consonants** do not change; the pronominal suffixes remain constant. Master the suffix paradigm, maintain reasonable vocabulary recall, and use a standard lexicon for identification.

---

## 9.9 Monosyllabic Nouns with Pronominal Suffixes (§9.9)

Certain singular monosyllabic nouns add **ִי (Hireq Yod)** to their stem before a pronominal suffix. Distinguish carefully: with the singular noun, Hireq Yod appears throughout (Type 1); with the plural noun, the vowel varies and Hireq Yod is absent (Type 2). Paradigm: אָח (brother).

| PGN | Noun ms (Type 1) | Translation | Noun mp (Type 2) | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3ms | אָחִיו | his brother | אֶחָיו | his brothers |
| 3fs | אָחִ֫יהָ | her brother | אַחֶ֫יהָ | her brothers |
| 2ms | אָחִ֫יךָ | your brother | אַחֶ֫יךָ | your brothers |
| 2fs | אָחִ֫יךְ | your brother | אַחַ֫יִךְ | your brothers |
| 1cs | אָחִי | my brother | אַחַי | my brothers |
| 3mp | אֲחִיהֶם | their brother | אֲחֵיהֶם | their brothers |
| 3fp | אֲחִיהֶן | their brother | אֲחֵיהֶן | their brothers |
| 2mp | אֲחִיכֶם | your brother | אֲחֵיכֶם | your brothers |
| 2fp | אֲחִיכֶן | your brother | אֲחֵיכֶן | your brothers |
| 1cp | אָחִ֫ינוּ | our brother | אַחֵ֫ינוּ | our brothers |

## 9.10 Prepositions with Pronominal Suffixes (§9.10)

Prepositions also take pronominal suffixes. On prepositions, the suffixes are **objective** rather than possessive: "to him," "for her," "with me." Some prepositions take Type 1 and others Type 2 suffixes; the translation value is the same for both types.

**לְ (to, for) — Type 1 suffixes:**

| PGN | Form | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| 3ms | לוֹ | to him (it) |
| 3fs | לָהּ | to her (it) |
| 2ms | לְךָ | to you |
| 2fs | לָךְ | to you |
| 1cs | לִי | to me |
| 3mp | לָהֶם | to them |
| 3fp | לָהֶן | to them |
| 2mp | לָכֶם | to you |
| 2fp | לָכֶן | to you |
| 1cp | לָ֫נוּ | to us |

**עַל (on, upon) — Type 2 suffixes:**

| PGN | Form | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| 3ms | עָלָיו | on him (it) |
| 3fs | עָלֶ֫יהָ | on her (it) |
| 2ms | עָלֶ֫יךָ | on you |
| 2fs | עָלַ֫יִךְ | on you |
| 1cs | עָלַי | on me |
| 3mp | עֲלֵיהֶם | on them |
| 3fp | עֲלֵיהֶן | on them |
| 2mp | עֲלֵיכֶם | on you |
| 2fp | עֲלֵיכֶן | on you |
| 1cp | עָלֵ֫ינוּ | on us |

---

## 9.11 Notes on Prepositions with Pronominal Suffixes (§9.11)

1. Some variation in spelling of the pronominal suffixes will be observed with certain prepositions; focus on identifying person, gender, and number of the suffix.
2. Prepositions taking **Type 1** suffixes: לְ, בְּ, עִם, אֵת (with). Prepositions taking **Type 2** suffixes: עַל, אֶל, תַּ֫חַת, אַחֲרֵי. With regard to translation, a preposition's suffix preference is of little importance.

---

## 9.13 Notes on כְּ and מִן with Pronominal Suffixes (§9.13)

1. With **כְּ**, forms with singular and 1cp suffixes use the longer alternate spelling כָּמ֫וֹ. Plural suffixes use the shorter form.
2. With **מִן**, forms with singular and 1cp suffixes also use a longer alternate spelling. Note: the 3ms (מִמֶּ֫נּוּ) and 1cp (מִמֶּ֫נּוּ) forms are **identical**; context determines the correct reading.

---

## 9.14 The Definite Direct Object Marker and the Preposition אֵת with Pronominal Suffixes (§9.14)

Both the object marker (אֵת/אֶת־) and the preposition אֵת/אֶת־ (with) take Type 1 suffixes. Distinguish them by their vowels:

| PGN | Object Marker | Translation | Preposition | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3ms | אֹתוֹ | him (it) | אִתּוֹ | with him (it) |
| 3fs | אֹתָהּ | her (it) | אִתָּהּ | with her (it) |
| 2ms | אֹתְךָ | you | אִתְּךָ | with you |
| 2fs | אֹתָךְ | you | אִתָּךְ | with you |
| 1cs | אֹתִי | me | אִתִּי | with me |
| 3mp | אֹתָם | them | אִתָּם | with them |
| 3fp | אֹתָן | them | אִתָּן | with them |
| 2mp | אֶתְכֶם | you | אִתְּכֶם | with you |
| 2fp | אֶתְכֶן | you | אִתְּכֶן | with you |
| 1cp | אֹתָ֫נוּ | us | אִתָּ֫נוּ | with us |

**Key distinction:**
- Object marker: **Holem** after the initial consonant (אֹתִי), except Seghol in 2mp and 2fp (אֶתְכֶם).
- Preposition: **Hireq** under the initial consonant (אִתִּי) + Daghesh Forte in the תּ.

## 9.15 עִם and עַם with Pronominal Suffixes (§9.15)

The preposition עִם (with) and the noun עַם (people) with pronominal suffixes can be confused. The most significant difference is the vowel under the first consonant. Both take Daghesh Forte in מ when pronominal suffixes are added.

| PGN | Preposition עִם | Translation | Noun עַם | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3ms | עִמּוֹ | with him (it) | עַמּוֹ | his people |
| 3fs | עִמָּהּ | with her (it) | עַמָּהּ | her people |
| 2ms | עִמְּךָ | with you | עַמְּךָ | your people |
| 2fs | עִמָּךְ | with you | עַמֵּךְ | your people |
| 1cs | עִמִּי | with me | עַמִּי | my people |
| 3mp | עִמָּם | with them | עַמָּם | their people |
| 3fp | עִמָּן | with them | עַמָּן | their people |
| 2mp | עִמָּכֶם | with you | עַמְּכֶם | your people |
| 2fp | עִמָּכֶן | with you | עַמְּכֶן | your people |
| 1cp | עִמָּ֫נוּ | with us | עַמֵּ֫נוּ | our people |

**Key distinction:** Preposition = Hireq (עִמִּי); noun = Pathach (עַמִּי).

## 9.16 Summary (§9.16)

1. Pronominal suffixes are **possessive** on nouns and **objective** on prepositions.
2. All pronominal suffixes have person, gender, and number.
3. Two sets: **Type 1** (singular nouns, selected prepositions) and **Type 2** (plural nouns, selected prepositions). Type 2 suffixes always contain י.
4. The dot in the Type 1 3fs suffix is called **Mappiq** (הּ); it is not Daghesh.
5. Singular nouns can take both singular and plural pronominal suffixes; plural nouns likewise.
6. With masculine plural nouns, the plural ending (ִים) is **lost** when pronominal suffixes are added (סוּסַי, my horses). Feminine plural nouns **retain** the וֹת ending (תּוֹרוֹתַי, my laws).
7. When a feminine singular noun ending in ָה receives a pronominal suffix, the ה is replaced by ת (תּוֹרָתִי, my law).
8. When a noun ending in ֶה receives a pronominal suffix, the final ה simply drops off — not replaced by ת (שָׂדִי, my field).
9. Certain singular monosyllabic nouns add ִי (Hireq Yod) to their stem before a pronominal suffix (אָחִ֫יךָ, your brother).
10. Prepositions may take either Type 1 or Type 2 suffixes; the translation value is the same for both types.
11. The prepositions כְּ and מִן use longer alternate spellings in all singular and 1cp suffixed forms (כָּמ֫וֹךָ, מִמְּךָ).
12. The object marker אֵת/אֶת־ takes Type 1 suffixes; it is translated as a personal pronoun in the objective case. Distinguish from the preposition אֵת/אֶת־ (with) by vowel: object marker has Holem (אֹתִי); preposition has Hireq (אִתִּי) + Daghesh Forte in תּ.
13. Combined Type 1/Type 2/Alternate paradigm for reference:

| PGN | Type 1 | Type 1 Alternate | Type 2 | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3ms | וֹ | הוּ | ָיו | his (its) / him (it) |
| 3fs | ָהּ | הָ | ֶ֫יהָ | her (its) / her (it) |
| 2ms | ךָ | — | ֶ֫יךָ | your / you |
| 2fs | ךְ | — | ַ֫יִךְ | your / you |
| 1cs | ִי | נִי | ַי | my / me |
| 3mp | הֶם | ָם | ֵיהֶם | their / them |
| 3fp | הֶן | ָן | ֵיהֶן | their / them |
| 2mp | כֶם | — | ֵיכֶם | your / you |
| 2fp | כֶן | — | ֵיכֶן | your / you |
| 1cp | נוּ | — | ֵ֫ינוּ | our / us |

## 9.19 Additional Information: Resumptive Pronouns (§9.19)

A relative clause may contain a **resumptive pronoun** — a pronoun within the relative clause that refers back to the noun the clause modifies. In translation, it is best to omit the resumptive pronoun.

| Hebrew | Literal | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| הַמָּקוֹם אֲשֶׁר אַתָּה עוֹמֵד עָלָיו | the place which you are standing on (it) | Exod 3:5 |
| הַבָּתִּים אֲשֶׁר־יֹאכְלוּ אֹתוֹ בָּהֶם | the houses which they eat it in (them) | Exod 12:7 |
| וַיִּשְׁמְרֵנוּ בְּכָל־הַדֶּרֶךְ אֲשֶׁר הָלַכְנוּ בָהּ | he guarded us in all the way that we walked in (it) | Josh 24:17 |

## 9.20 Something You Should Know: Who is Like You? Part 2 (§9.20)

The prophet Micah's name — מִיכָה or מִיכָיְהוּ — is composed of מִי (who?), the preposition כְּ, and a shortened form of יהוה: "Who is like Yahweh?" The link between the prophet's name and his climactic declaration in Mic 7:18 is unmistakable.

> מִי־אֵל כָּמוֹךָ
> Who is a God like you? (Mic 7:18)

A simple grammatical construction: the interrogative pronoun מִי, the divine title אֵל, and the preposition כְּ with a 2ms pronominal suffix (כָּמוֹךָ). This rhetorical question expects no answer because no candidates can be identified.

God himself declares his uniqueness in Isa 44:6–7:

> כֹּה־אָמַר יְהוָה … אֲנִי רִאשׁוֹן וַאֲנִי אַחֲרוֹן וּמִבַּלְעָדַי אֵין אֱלֹהִים וּמִי־כָמוֹנִי יִקְרָא וְיַגִּידֶהָ
> Thus says Yahweh … "I am the first and I am the last; besides me there is no god. Who is like me? Let him proclaim it."

And in a litany of superlative constructions in Deut 10:17:

> יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵיכֶם הוּא אֱלֹהֵי הָאֱלֹהִים וַאֲדֹנֵי הָאֲדֹנִים הָאֵל הַגָּדֹל הַגִּבֹּר וְהַנּוֹרָא
> Yahweh your God is God of gods and Lord of lords, the great, the mighty, and the awesome God.

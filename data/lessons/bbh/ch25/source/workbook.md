### III. Grammatical Commentary

**1 Kings 8:22**

**וַיַּעֲמֹד** — The Pathach preformative vowel and the Hateph Pathach under the first root letter is the Type 2, I-Guttural weak verb pattern (§16.2–3).

**כַּפָּיו** — The feminine noun כַּף (with 3ms pronominal suffix) is a Geminate noun; the doubled consonant is represented by Daghesh Forte (§4.8.4).

**הַשָּׁמָיִם** — Translate "to the heavens" or "toward heaven." Adverbial modifiers of this type do not require a preposition or directional ה to indicate motion toward.

**1 Kings 8:23**

**יְהוָה** — The divine name and title יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל are vocative (direct address).

**הַהֹלְכִים** — Masculine plural attributive Participle. It agrees in gender, number, and definiteness with עֲבָדֶיךָ, which is definite because of the pronominal suffix (§10.2.3).

**לִבָּם** — The noun לֵב with 3mp pronominal suffix is a Geminate noun (Daghesh Forte in the בּ).

**1 Kings 8:24**

**אֲשֶׁר** — The relative pronoun introduces a clause attributing an action to Yahweh: "who has kept."

**אֵת אֲשֶׁר** — The definite direct object marker identifies the relative clause as the object of שָׁמַרְתָּ: "You have kept what you spoke."

**דִּבַּרְתָּ** — Piel Perfect 2ms from דבר (§30.2.3). The sufformative תָּ identifies Perfect 2ms.

**וַתְּדַבֵּר** — Piel Imperfect 2ms from דבר with Waw Consecutive.

**מִלֵּאתָ** — Piel Perfect 2ms from מלא.

**1 Kings 8:25**

**לֹּו** — The Daghesh in the ל preposition is a Conjunctive Daghesh, placed in an initial consonant to smooth pronunciation between words ending in an unaccented ָה or ֶה and a following word beginning with an accented syllable (§32.11).

**מִלְּפָנַי** — Four parts: מִן + לְ + masculine plural construct of פָּנֶה + 1cs pronominal suffix. Translate "from before me."

**רַק אִם** — This combination introduces a condition: "only if."

**1 Kings 8:26**

**יֵאָמֶן נָא** — The particle נָא (§18.10) identifies the volitional use (§23.7.3) of this Niphal Imperfect. Normally volitional verbs appear first in their clause, but here the adverb עַתָּה and the vocative אֱלֹהֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל precede the verb.

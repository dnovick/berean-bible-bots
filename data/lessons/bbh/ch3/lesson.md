# BBH Chapter 3 — Syllabification and Pronunciation

*Basics of Biblical Hebrew, Pratico & Van Pelt — Chapter 3*

---

## Two Rules of Syllabification (§3.2)

1. Every syllable must begin with **one consonant** and have **only one vowel**.
2. There are only **two types** of syllables: **open** (ends with a vowel) and **closed** (ends with a consonant).

| Type | Pattern | Ends with | Example | Syllabification |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Open** | CV | vowel | דָּ in דָּבָר | דָּ (vowel at end = open) |
| **Closed** | CVC | consonant | בָר in דָּבָר | בָר (resh at end = closed) |

---

## Syllable Accent (§3.3–3.4)

Hebrew words are usually accented on the **last syllable**. When a word is accented on a different syllable, an accent mark appears over that syllable (e.g., סֵ֫פֶר — accent on the first syllable).

This mark is a convention used by grammars such as BBH to help students locate the stress — it is not how accentuation works in the Masoretic Text itself.

| Position | Name | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Accented syllable | **Tonic** | The stressed syllable | בָר in דָּבָר |
| One before tonic | **Pretonic** | Immediately before accent | דָּ in דָּבָר |
| Two before tonic | **Propretonic** | Two syllables before accent | דְּ in דְּבָרִים |

Hebrew grammars also use a second, related set of terms for syllable position — this time counted from the **end of the word**, regardless of where the accent falls:

| Position | Name | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Last syllable | **Ultimate** (ultima) | The final syllable of the word | בָר in דָּבָר |
| Second-to-last syllable | **Penultimate** (penult) | One syllable before the ultima | דָּ in דָּבָר |
| Third-to-last syllable | **Antepenultimate** (antepenult) | Two syllables before the ultima | דְּ in דְּבָרִים |

Because Hebrew words are usually accented on the last syllable, the two sets of terms usually line up: ultima = tonic, penult = pretonic, antepenult = propretonic. But they are not the same thing — tonic/pretonic/propretonic are defined **relative to the accent**, while ultimate/penultimate/antepenultimate are defined **relative to the end of the word**. When the accent falls elsewhere, the two systems diverge: in סֵ֫פֶר (two syllables, accented on the first), the accented syllable is the **penult**, not the ultima — so here the tonic syllable and the penultimate syllable are the same syllable, while the ultima (פֶר) is unaccented.

## Daghesh and Syllabification (§3.5)

The **Daghesh Forte** doubles the consonant and must be divided in syllabification. The **Daghesh Lene** (in begadkephat letters) does **not** affect syllabification.

| Word | Syllabification | Note |
|---|---|---|
| חֻקָּה | חֻקְ׀קָה | Daghesh Forte in ק doubles it |
| יַבָּשָׁה | יַבְּ׀בָּ׀שָׁה | Daghesh Forte in ב doubles it |
| תְּפִלָּה | תְּ׀פִלְ׀לָה | Daghesh Forte in ל doubles it |

**Identifying Daghesh in begadkephat letters (ב ג ד כ פ ת):**

1. **Forte** if preceded by a vowel (e.g., ת in אַתָּה — preceded by Pathach)
2. **Lene** if preceded by a consonant (e.g., כּ in מַלְכָּה — preceded by ל with Silent Shewa)
3. **Lene** at the beginning of a word (unless the previous word ends in a vowel)

## The Shewa and Syllabification (§3.6)

| Type | Sound | Syllable Position |
|---|---|---|
| **Vocal Shewa** | Ultra-short (like *a* in *amuse*) | Opens an open syllable |
| **Silent Shewa** | No sound | Closes a syllable (follows a short vowel) |

**Rules for identifying the Shewa:**

1. **Silent** if the previous vowel is short (e.g., מַלְכָּה — Shewa under ל follows short Pathach)
   - First of two adjacent Shewas is silent (e.g., מִשְׁ in מִשְׁפְּטֵי)
   - A Shewa at the end of a word is silent
2. **Vocal** in all other circumstances:
   - Initial Shewa is always vocal (e.g., בְּרָכָה)
   - Second of two adjacent Shewas is vocal (e.g., פְּ in מִשְׁפְּטֵי)
   - Shewa under a consonant with Daghesh Forte is vocal (e.g., הַמְּלָכִים)
   - Shewa after an unaccented long vowel is vocal (e.g., כֹּתְבִים)

> Gutturals (א ה ח ע) generally avoid Vocal Shewa, often replacing it with a Ḥateph vowel (e.g., אֲ, הֱ, חֲ). However, they may take a Silent Shewa when they close a syllable (e.g., שָׁמַ֫עְתָּ).

## Qamets vs. Qamets Hatuf (§3.7)

The **Qamets** (בָּ, long *ā*) and **Qamets Hatuf** (בָּ, short *ŏ*) are written identically. Context determines which vowel is present.

| Condition | Vowel | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| Open syllable | Qamets | long *ā* |
| Closed, **accented** syllable | Qamets | long *ā* |
| Closed, **unaccented** syllable | Qamets Hatuf | short *ŏ* |
| Any syllable with **Metheg** (<span style="font-size:1.35em;font-family:'Arial Hebrew',Arial,sans-serif">בָֽ</span>) to the left | Qamets | long *ā* |

> The most common Qamets Hatuf: כָּל ("all of") when unaccented before maqqef.

## Special Cases

### Furtive Pathach (§3.8)

When a word ends in **ח** or **ע**, a Pathach may appear beneath it and must be pronounced **before** the guttural. The Furtive Pathach is not counted in syllabification.

| Word | Syllabification | Note |
|---|---|---|
| בֹּרֵחַ | בֹּ׀רֵחַ | Furtive Pathach before final ח |
| רוּחַ | רוּחַ | Monosyllabic |
| נֹטֵעַ | נֹ׀טֵעַ | Furtive Pathach before final ע |

### Quiescent א (§3.9)

When **א** appears without a vowel, it is quiescent (silent) and is **not counted** as a consonant in syllabification.

> חַטָּאת is divided as חַטְ׀טָאת (the quiescent א does not close a syllable).

### Hebrew Diphthongs (§3.10)

Two vowel-consonant clusters function as a **single unit** and make the syllable **closed**.

| Type | Pattern | Example | Syllabification |
|---|---|---|---|
| AY diphthong | ַיִ | שָׁמַ֫יִם | שָׁ׀מַ֫יִם |
| AW diphthong | ָוֶ | מָ֫וֶת | מָ֫וֶת (monosyllabic) |

## Summary Rules (§3.11)

1. Every syllable must begin with a consonant and have only one vowel.
2. There are only two syllable types: open (ends in a vowel) and closed (ends in a consonant).
3. A Daghesh Forte doubles the consonant and must be divided in syllabification.
4. A Daghesh in a begadkephat is Lene if preceded by a consonant; Forte if preceded by a vowel.
5. A Daghesh Forte in a begadkephat doubles the **hard** sound, not the soft sound.
6. The gutturals (א ה ח ע) and ר cannot take Daghesh Lene or Daghesh Forte.
7. A Shewa is **silent** if the previous vowel is short; **vocal** in all other circumstances.
8. Gutturals generally avoid Vocal Shewa, often taking a Ḥateph vowel instead; they may take a Silent Shewa when closing a syllable.
9. Unlike the gutturals, ר **can** take Vocal Shewa.
10. The reduced vowels (בֲּ בֱּ בֳּ) always occur in open syllables and are never silent.
11. The Metheg (<span style="font-size:1.35em;font-family:'Arial Hebrew',Arial,sans-serif">בָֽ</span>) occurs with Qamets (not Qamets Hatuf) to distinguish the two.
12. Furtive Pathach is not a full vowel and is not counted in syllabification.
13. When א occurs without a vowel, it is quiescent.
14. Syllables containing the diphthong (ַיִ) are closed because they end with a consonant.

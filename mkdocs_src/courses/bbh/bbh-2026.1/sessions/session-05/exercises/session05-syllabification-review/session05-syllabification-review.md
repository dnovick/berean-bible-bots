## Notes on Selected Items

**Word 1 — בַּיִת:** The Yod here functions as a consonant ("house"), not as a mater lectionis. It receives a Hireq vowel, which begins the second syllable. Compare with מִי where Yod is quiescent (mater for Hireq Yod).

**Word 5 — אֱמֶת:** The Alef takes a Hateph Seghol (composite shewa) because Alef is a guttural — gutturals cannot take a simple vocal shewa. The syllable אֱ is still open: a composite shewa under a guttural functions as a reduced vowel, not a syllable-closer.

**Word 12 — פְּנֵי:** The initial Pe bears a vocal shewa because it is in word-initial position — every Hebrew word must begin with a full vowel sound. The shewa here is vocal (not silent), opening the first syllable פְּ. The second syllable ends in Tsere-Yod, a mater lectionis (the Yod is quiescent). This is the construct form of פָּנִים ("face of").

**Word 13 — מָקוֹם:** The Holem-Waw in the second syllable is a mater lectionis (long O); the Waw itself does not begin a new syllable but extends the vowel. The Mem closes the syllable, making syl.2 closed (C).

**Word 14 — בְּרָכָה:** Three open syllables. The initial shewa is vocal (word-initial position). The final He has no vowel and no mappiq, making it a quiescent mater lectionis (Qamets He), not a consonant. The stress falls on the final syllable.

**Word 15 — יְהוּדָה:** Three open syllables. The Yod bears a vocal shewa (word-initial). The Holem-Waw (הוּ) is a mater lectionis for the long U vowel. The final He is quiescent (Qamets He), as in בְּרָכָה above.

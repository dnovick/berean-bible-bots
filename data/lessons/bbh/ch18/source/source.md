# BBH Chapter 18 — Qal Imperative

*Source: Pratico & Van Pelt, Basics of Biblical Hebrew Grammar (BBH), Chapter 18*

---

## 18.1 Introduction (§18.1)

The Imperative is used primarily to express direct commands. It can also be used to grant permission or to communicate a request. The Hebrew Imperative occurs only in the second person, singular and plural. For example, "(You) hate evil, and (you) love good!" (Amos 5:15).

---

## 18.2 Qal Imperative Paradigm: Strong Verbs (§18.2)

The Imperative conjugation occurs exclusively in the second person, giving four forms: masculine singular and plural, feminine singular and plural. These forms are derived from their corresponding Imperfect forms — the Imperative is an Imperfect without the preformative. The following paradigm must be memorized:

| | Imperative | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| **2ms** | קְטֹל | (you) kill! |
| **2fs** | קִטְלִי | (you) kill! |
| **2mp** | קִטְלוּ | (you) kill! |
| **2fp** | קְטֹ֫לְנָה | (you) kill! |

---

## 18.3 Imperative Paradigm Notes (§18.3)

**Note 1 — Derivation from the Imperfect.** Remove the Imperfect preformative (consonant and vowel) from the four second person Imperfect forms and you have the basic Imperative paradigm:

| | Imperfect Form | | Imperative Form |
|---|---|---|---|
| **2ms** | תִּקְטֹל | ➣ | קְטֹל |
| **2fs** | תִּקְטְלִי | ➣ | קִטְלִי |
| **2mp** | תִּקְטְלוּ | ➣ | קִטְלוּ |
| **2fp** | תִּקְטֹ֫לְנָה | ➣ | קְטֹ֫לְנָה |

**Note 2 — Rule of Shewa.** Both the 2fs and 2mp forms undergo a minor change: two Vocal Shewas cannot stand together at the beginning of a word. Removing the preformative from תִּקְטְלוּ leaves קְטְלוּ; applying the Rule of Shewa (§4.11.1) yields קִטְלוּ.

**Note 3 — Alternate 2ms Form with ָה.** Frequently (approximately 300 times), the 2ms Imperative appears with a ָה ending. Note that the vowel in the first syllable is Qamets Hatuf, not Qamets. This alternate form does not translate differently than the regular 2ms form.

**Strong verb examples:**

| Lexical Form | Imperative 2ms | Alternate 2ms | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| קָטַל | קְטֹל | קָטְלָה | you (2ms) kill! |
| שָׁמַר | שְׁמֹר | שָׁמְרָה | you (2ms) keep! |
| שָׁמַע | שְׁמַע | שִׁמְעָה | you (2ms) listen! |

**Weak verb examples — three most common forms with ָה:**

| Lexical Form | Imperative 2ms | Alternate 2ms | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| הָלַךְ | לֵךְ | לְכָה | you (2ms) come (go)! |
| נָתַן | תֵּן | תְּנָה | you (2ms) give! |
| קוּם | קוּם | קוּמָה | you (2ms) arise! |

## 18.9 Negation of the Imperative (§18.9)

To produce a negative command, Hebrew does not negate an Imperative form. Prohibitions (negative commands) are expressed with the negative particles לֹא or אַל with the second person Imperfect. The particle לֹא before the Imperfect expresses an absolute and permanent prohibition. The particle אַל before an Imperfect expresses an immediate and specific prohibition.

---

## 18.10 The Particle נָא (§18.10)

Frequently (about 190 times), Imperatives are followed by the particle נָא, which may be translated as "please" or simply left untranslated. This particle may be connected to the Imperative with Maqqef. For example, both שְׁמֹר נָא and שְׁמָר־נָא occur and may be translated as either "observe!" or "please observe!" Note that when the Imperative is connected to the particle with Maqqef, there is a vowel change in the Imperative (Holem to Qamets Hatuf). The presence of נָא is often an indication of the Imperative (though it may signal other forms as well).

---

## 18.11 Translating the Imperative (§18.11)

When translating the Hebrew Imperative, it is not always necessary to use the second person pronoun. For example, שְׁמֹר may be translated as either "you observe!" or simply "observe!" English style normally prefers the shorter translation without the pronoun. Note, however, that the Imperative is always second person even if you choose not to use the second person pronoun.

> עִבְדוּ אֶת־יְהוָה
>
> "You (2mp) serve Yahweh!" or "Serve Yahweh!" (Ps 100:2)

---

## 18.12 Important Verbal Sequences with the Imperative (§18.12)

1. **Several Imperative verbs in succession.** They may be linked with the conjunction וְ. Context determines whether they are related consequentially or sequentially.

> אֶת־אָבִיךָ עֲלֵה וּקְבֹר
>
> Go up and bury your father (Gen 50:6).

> עֲשׂוּ וִחְיוּ זֹאת
>
> Do this and live (Gen 42:18).

2. **Imperative followed by a consecutive Perfect.** The Perfect verb may carry the full force of the preceding Imperative, indicating consecution of action.

> אֶל־עַבְדִּי לֵךְ וְאָמַרְתָּ
>
> Go and say to my servant (2 Sam 7:5).

> בָהָר לִפְנֵי יְהוָה צֵא וְעָמַדְתָּ וַיֹּאמֶר
>
> And he said, "Go out and stand on the mountain before Yahweh" (1 Kgs 19:11).

3. **Imperative followed by an Imperfect.** This creates a purpose or result clause. The Imperfect is marked with the conjunction וְ (not Waw Consecutive) and may be translated "so that."

> וְנִחְיֶה וְשִׁבְרוּ־לָנוּ מִשָּׁם רְדוּ־שָׁמָּה
>
> Go down there and buy grain for us from there so that we might live (Gen 42:2).

> אֶל־הַמֶּלֶךְ אֹתְךָ וְאֶשְׁלְחָה הֵנָּה בֹּא
>
> Come here so that I may send you to the king (2 Sam 14:32).

---

## 18.13 Summary (§18.13)

1. The Imperative is used primarily to express direct commands. It can also be used to grant permission or to communicate a request. The Hebrew Imperative occurs only in the second person, singular and plural.
2. The Qal Imperative paradigm must be memorized:

| | Imperative | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| **2ms** | קְטֹל | (you) kill! |
| **2fs** | קִטְלִי | (you) kill! |
| **2mp** | קִטְלוּ | (you) kill! |
| **2fp** | קְטֹ֫לְנָה | (you) kill! |

3. In the strong verb, the Qal Imperative forms are derived from the corresponding Imperfect forms: remove the Imperfect preformative from the four second person forms. Some vowel variation occurs in certain weak verb classes.
4. To produce a negative command, the Imperative is not negated. Prohibitions are expressed with the negative particles לֹא and אַל followed by the second person Imperfect.
5. Imperatives may be followed by the particle נָא, translated as "please" or left untranslated; it may be connected to the Imperative with Maqqef (־נָא).
6. Three important sequences involving the Imperative: (1) several Imperatives in succession, related consequentially or sequentially; (2) the Imperative followed by a consecutive Perfect indicating consecution of action; (3) an Imperative followed by an Imperfect creating a purpose or result clause.

## 18.15 Something You Should Memorize: Psalm 115:1 (§18.15)

> One of the great joys of learning Hebrew is the ability to memorize portions of the Bible in one of the original languages in which the Scriptures have come to us. The treasury is vast, but even the smallest of texts are more desirable than gold (Ps 19:10).
>
> Our first text for memorization comes from the opening lines of Ps 115. In this psalm, Yahweh is identified as the true source of blessing, the one in whom Israel must trust, and the maker of heaven and earth. The idols of the surrounding nations, on the other hand, are lifeless, man-made objects. For these reasons, the name of Yahweh is all glorious and the only satisfying object of true worship.
>
> לֹא לָנוּ יְהוָה לֹא לָנוּ כִּי־לְשִׁמְךָ תֵּן כָּבוֹד עַל־חַסְדְּךָ עַל־אֲמִתֶּךָ
>
> Not to us, Yahweh, not to us, but to your name give glory, because of your steadfast love, because of your faithfulness.
>
> In the first line of this verse, the negative particle לֹא followed by the preposition לְ with a 1cp pronominal suffix is repeated on either side of the divine name, "Not to us, Yahweh, not to us." The second line begins with כִּי. When כִּי follows a negative clause, as it does here, it may be translated adversatively as "but." The verb תֵּן is Qal Imperative 2ms from נתן, preceded by its indirect object (the preposition לְ prefixed to שֵׁם with a 2ms pronominal suffix). The last line consists of two prepositional phrases joined by Maqqef to their objects, both appearing with a 2ms pronominal suffix, providing the reasons that glory should be given to Yahweh's name.

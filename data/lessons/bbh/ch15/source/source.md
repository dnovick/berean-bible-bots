# BBH Chapter 15 — Qal Imperfect Strong Verbs

*Source: Pratico & Van Pelt, Basics of Biblical Hebrew Grammar (BBH), Chapter 15*

---

## 15.1 Introduction (§15.1)

The Imperfect conjugation is the second of only two main (finite) verbal conjugations in biblical Hebrew. This conjugation is also referred to as the prefix conjugation because of a special set of verbal prefixes (also called preformatives) that appear with the Imperfect verb to indicate person, gender, and number. Of the over 50,000 occurrences of the Qal stem in the Hebrew Bible, the Imperfect conjugation occurs almost 22,000 times, approximately 43 percent of Qal stem occurrences. Like the Perfect conjugation, the Imperfect is inflected to indicate person, gender, and number.

---

## 15.2 The Meaning of the Qal Imperfect (§15.2)

The first basic use of the Imperfect (or prefix) conjugation is to express an incomplete action translated by the English present tense (I study) or future tense (I will study). The action of the verb occurs either at the time of speaking or after the time of speaking. The Hebrew Imperfect is also used to denote habitual or customary action, whether in the past, present, or future (he prays regularly, he used to pray). The Imperfect may also be rendered by one of several modal values (would, could, should, may, might, can, etc.). These modal translation values are suggested by various contextual considerations. It must also be emphasized that, like the Perfect, the Hebrew Imperfect does not have tense (time of action) apart from context and issues of syntax. It also signifies verbal aspect. Verbal aspect in the Imperfect signifies an action for which, in the mind of the speaker or writer, the conclusion is not in view. To state it differently, aspect in the Imperfect denotes incomplete action, whether in the past, present, or future.

---

## 15.3 The Form of the Qal Imperfect (§15.3)

Recall that the Perfect conjugation is called the suffix conjugation because inflectional suffixes called sufformatives are attached to the verbal root. The Imperfect conjugation is called the prefix conjugation because inflectional prefixes called preformatives are added to all Imperfect verbal forms. These preformatives distinguish the Imperfect conjugation from all other conjugations. Half of the Imperfect verbal forms also take sufformatives. The Imperfect paradigm must be memorized with a view to both writing and oral recitation. The preformatives and sufformatives of the Qal Imperfect are the same for all Imperfect forms in the derived stems, strong and weak verbs.

| | **Preformative** | **Imperfect** | **Sufformative** | **Translation** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **3ms** | יִ | יִקְטֹל | — | he will kill |
| **3fs** | תִּ | תִּקְטֹל | — | she will kill |
| **2ms** | תִּ | תִּקְטֹל | — | you will kill |
| **2fs** | תִּ | תִּקְטְלִי | ִי | you will kill |
| **1cs** | אֶ | אֶקְטֹל | — | I will kill |
| **3mp** | יִ | יִקְטְלוּ | וּ | they will kill |
| **3fp** | תִּ | תִּקְטֹ֫לְנָה | נָה | they will kill |
| **2mp** | תִּ | תִּקְטְלוּ | וּ | you will kill |
| **2fp** | תִּ | תִּקְטֹ֫לְנָה | נָה | you will kill |
| **1cp** | נִ | נִקְטֹל | — | we will kill |

## 15.4 Notes on the Qal Imperfect Strong Verb Paradigm (§15.4)

1. Precise memorization of this paradigm is essential. All Imperfect verbs in the derived stems use these same preformatives and sufformatives.
2. The presentation of forms moves from third person to second to first person and from singular to plural.
3. In the Qal stem, only the Imperfect conjugation has a verbal preformative. In the Qal strong verb, all preformative consonants have the Hireq vowel, except the 1cs form, which has Seghol (אֶקְטֹל). Five of the Imperfect forms have sufformatives.
4. Two sets of forms are identical in the Imperfect paradigm; context must provide the proper identification:

| Form | Identifications |
|---|---|
| תִּקְטֹל | both 3fs and 2ms (she will kill / you will kill) |
| תִּקְטֹ֫לְנָה | both 3fp and 2fp (they will kill / you will kill) |

5. In the Perfect, the third person plural form קָֽטְלוּ is common in gender. In the Imperfect, the third person plural is inflected for gender: יִקְטְלוּ (3mp) and תִּקְטֹ֫לְנָה (3fp).
6. The Imperfect prefix תִּ is a begadkephat letter that normally has a Daghesh Lene.
7. The stem vowel is Holem except in forms with sufformatives consisting of a vowel (2fs, 3mp, 2mp), where the Holem is reduced to Vocal Shewa.
8. With some frequency (approximately 320 times), the 3mp and 2mp forms appear with a final ן as in יִקְטְלוּן (Nun Paragogicum), which does not affect meaning.
9. Diagnostic features of the Qal Imperfect 3ms strong verb: Hireq preformative vowel (יִ) + Holem stem vowel (יִקְטֹל).

## 15.6 Stative Verbs: Imperfect (§15.6)

Stative verbs are classified by their stem vowel in the Qal Perfect: Pathach (גָּדַל), Tsere (כָּבֵד), or Holem (קָטֹן). With the Imperfect inflection of stative verbs, however, the stem vowel is **Pathach** regardless of the stem vowel in the Perfect.

| | **Perfect** | | **Imperfect** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pathach-Stative | גָּדַל | → | יִגְדַּל |
| Tsere-Stative | כָּבֵד | → | יִכְבַּד |
| Holem-Stative | קָטֹן | → | יִקְטַן |

Apart from this change in stem vowel, the Imperfect conjugation of the stative verb follows the strong verb pattern. Note the assimilation of the third root consonant (נ) into the נ of the Imperfect sufformative in the 3fp and 2fp forms of קָטֹן (תִּקְטַ֫נָּה).

| | **גָּדַל** (Pathach-Stative) | **כָּבֵד** (Tsere-Stative) | **קָטֹן** (Holem-Stative) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **3ms** | יִגְדַּל | יִכְבַּד | יִקְטַן |
| **3fs** | תִּגְדַּל | תִּכְבַּד | תִּקְטַן |
| **2ms** | תִּגְדַּל | תִּכְבַּד | תִּקְטַן |
| **2fs** | תִּגְדְּלִי | תִּכְבְּדִי | תִּקְטְנִי |
| **1cs** | אֶגְדַּל | אֶכְבַּד | אֶקְטַן |
| **3mp** | יִגְדְּלוּ | יִכְבְּדוּ | יִקְטְנוּ |
| **3fp** | תִּגְדַּ֫לְנָה | תִּכְבַּ֫דְנָה | תִּקְטַ֫נָּה |

## 15.8 Negation of the Imperfect: The Particles לֹא and אַל (§15.8)

1. Like the Perfect, the Imperfect is negated with **לֹא**, placed immediately before the verb. In the Ten Commandments (Decalogue), לֹא plus the Imperfect is used for an **absolute or permanent prohibition**:

| Hebrew | Translation |
|---|---|
| לֹא תַעֲשֶׂה | you shall not make (Exod 20:4) |
| לֹא תִשָּׂא | you shall not lift up (Exod 20:7) |
| לֹא תִּרְצָח | you shall not kill (Exod 20:13) |
| לֹא תִּנְאָף | you shall not commit adultery (Exod 20:14) |
| לֹא תִּגְנֹב | you shall not steal (Exod 20:15) |
| לֹא תַעֲנֶה | you shall not answer (Exod 20:16) |
| לֹא תַחְמֹד | you shall not covet (Exod 20:17) |

2. The negative particle **אַל** before an Imperfect expresses an **immediate, specific, and non-durative prohibition** (often connected with Maqqef):

| Hebrew | Translation |
|---|---|
| אַל־תִּכְתֹּב בַּסֵּ֫פֶר | Do not write in the book! |
| אַל־תִּירָא | Do not fear! (Gen 15:1) |
| אַל־תִּשְׁמְעוּ אֶל־דִּבְרֵי נְבִיאֵיכֶם | Do not listen to the words of your prophets! (Jer 27:16) |

---

## 15.9 Summary (§15.9)

1. The Qal stem expresses simple action with an active voice. The Imperfect conjugation expresses an incomplete action, translated with the English present or future tense.
2. The Imperfect (prefix) conjugation takes a verbal preformative in every form. The preformatives are unique to the Imperfect in the Qal and derived stems.
3. Diagnostic features: Hireq preformative vowel (יִ) + Holem stem vowel → יִקְטֹל (Qal Imperfect 3ms strong verb).
4. With the Imperfect inflection of stative verbs, the stem vowel is Pathach (יִכְבַּד), not Holem (יִקְטֹל).
5. Imperfect verbs are negated with לֹא (absolute or permanent prohibition) and אַל (immediate and specific prohibition).
6. The use of the Imperfect described here represents only one of several possible uses. Additional uses are described in chapters 17 and 23.

## 15.11 Something You Should Know: God Has a Long Nose! (§15.11)

> Of the many wonders to be found in the Word of God, few surpass the revelation of the character of God. For example, we know that God is the creator of all things (Gen 1:1–2:3; Isa 45:6) and the redeemer of his people (Exod 14; Isa 43:1) and that by these actions he has demonstrated his immeasurable and unparalleled power. Another way we learn about the character of God is by certain divinely inspired descriptions of God, such as "majestic in holiness" or "awesome in glory" (Exod 15:11). Yet, perhaps the most remarkable way in which we learn about the character of God is when God describes himself. For example, in Scripture God has described himself as compassionate (רַחוּם in Exod 34:6), gracious (חַנּוּן in Exod 34:6), and holy (קָדוֹשׁ in Lev 11:45; 19:2). These are some of the most well-known attributes of God's character. However, you might be surprised to discover that God has also described himself as having a "long nose."
>
> This unique description of God's character is first found in Exod 34:6, when God came and stood with Moses to proclaim his name. We read in the text, "And he passed in front of Moses, proclaiming, 'The LORD, The LORD, the compassionate and gracious God, slow to anger, abounding in love and faithfulness'" (NIV). What is translated as "slow to anger" in English is the Hebrew construct chain אֶרֶךְ אַפַּיִם, literally "long of nose." This expression may also be translated as "patient" or "longsuffering" (KJV). In Hebrew, the idiom "long of nose" was used to describe someone who was patient or slow to anger (Prov 14:29). Conversely, a person "short of nose" (קְצַר־אַפַּיִם as in Prov 14:17) was considered to be impatient or quick-tempered, much like the modern English idioms "hothead" or "short fuse."
>
> The theological significance of God's long nose is not to be underestimated. Because God is patient, his people do not perish. Because God's nose is long, he has not treated us as we deserve. In the New Testament we are encouraged to "bear in mind that God's patience means salvation" (2 Pet 3:15; cf. 3:9). Reflecting on these realities, the psalmist has put it this way (Ps 103:8–10, NIV):
>
> The LORD is compassionate and gracious,
> slow to anger [long of nose], abounding in love.
> He will not always accuse,
> nor will he harbor his anger forever;
> he does not treat us as our sins deserve
> or repay us according to our iniquities.
>
> Miles V. Van Pelt
> Alan H. Belcher, Jr. Professor of Old Testament and Biblical Languages

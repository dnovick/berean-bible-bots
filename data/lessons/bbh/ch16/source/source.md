
Qal Imperfect Weak Verbs


    16.1      Introduction. Compared to weak verbs in the Perfect, the spelling of weak Imperfect verbs exhibits far more variation and irregularity. The addition of both preformatives and sufformatives to weak verbal roots increases the likelihood of spelling variation. Regardless of a verb’s weakness, however, the preformative consonants and the sufformatives of the Imperfect conjugation do not change. For this reason, it is necessary to have first memorized the Imperfect forms of the strong verb before working through this chapter. In this chapter, you will not be required to memorize weak verb paradigms. Rather, you must study and understand the types of changes that occur in each of the various weak verb classes. The types of changes encountered with Qal Imperfect verbs will also apply to the study of other verbal conjugations in subsequent chapters.
    16.2      I-Guttural Verbs. There are three types of I-Guttural weak verbs in the Imperfect. Each type is classified by its preformative vowel.     16.3      I-Guttural Notes.
         1.      Type 1 verbs exhibit a Seghol preformative vowel followed by a Hateph Seghol under the guttural in first root position (יֶחֱזַק). Note that in those forms having a sufformative consisting of a vowel (2fs, 3mp, 2mp), the vowel under the guttural is just Seghol in a closed syllable (תֶּחֶזְקוּ).
         2.      Type 2 verbs exhibit a Pathach preformative vowel and a Hateph Pathach under the guttural in first root position (יַעֲמֹד). One exception to this pattern is the 1cs form אֶעֱמֹד with Seghol under the preformative and Hateph Seghol under the guttural. Note also that in those forms having a sufformative consisting of a vowel (2fs, 3mp, 2mp), the vowel under the guttural is just Pathach in a closed syllable (תַּעַמְדוּ).
         3.      Type 3 verbs have א for the first root letter (אָמַר). While this pattern occurs with only six verbs, some of these verbs occur with significant frequency. In each case, the preformative vowel is Holem and the א is quiescent (יֹאמַר). Note the unusual 1cs form אֹמַר (I will say). You would expect the א to be written twice (אֹאמַר), once for the Imperfect preformative and once for the first consonant of the verbal root. Observe, however, that it is written only once (אֹמַר).
         4.      It is important to note that not all I-א verbs are inflected according to the pattern of Type 3 verbs as described above. There are several other I-א verbs that follow the Type 1 or the Type 2 pattern for I-Guttural verbs. Examples of I-א verbs that follow the Type 1 or Type 2 pattern include אָסַף (to gather), אָהַב (to love), and אָסַר (to tie, bind).

    16.4      II-Guttural, III-ע/ח, and III-א Verbs. Verbs in these weak categories differ from the strong verb primarily in the stem vowel. Strong verbs have a Holem stem vowel (יִקְטֹל), but these weak verbs prefer an a-class stem vowel in selected forms, Pathach (יִבְחַר) and Qamets (יִמְצָא).

    16.5      II-Guttural, III-ע/ח, and III-א Notes.
         1.      In the II-Guttural class, note the Hateph Pathach under the guttural in the 2fs (תִּבְחֲרִי), 3mp (יִבְחֲרוּ), and 2mp (תִּבְחֲרוּ) forms. The guttural in second root position cannot take the expected Vocal Shewa.
         2.      Like the Qal Imperfect stative verbs studied in the previous chapter, II-Guttural and III-ע/ח verbs take a Pathach stem vowel. This shift in stem vowel occurs because guttural consonants prefer a-type vowels. Note that the vocalization of the Qal Imperfect stative (15.6) is identical to the vocalization of the Qal Imperfect III-ע/ח verb.
         3.      In the III-א class, the stem vowel is Qamets (יִמְצָא) in those forms without a sufformative. Those forms with sufformatives that consist of a vowel (2fs, 3mp, and 2mp) are identical to their corresponding strong verb forms. The twin 3fp and 2fp forms (תִּמְצֶ֫אנָה) exhibit an unexpected Seghol stem vowel. This variation is unusual but should pose no difficulty for the identification of these two forms.

    16.7      I-י Notes.
         1.      In every form of the Type 1 paradigm, the initial י root consonant has dropped off and the Imperfect preformative vowel is Tsere. Note that the י in the 3ms (יֵשֵׁב) and 3mp (יֵשְׁבוּ) forms is the Imperfect preformative and not the first consonant of the verbal root. You should also observe that the stem vowel is Tsere in all forms without a sufformative. Verbs of this type can be difficult to recognize because the initial root consonant is lost, leaving only the second and third consonants of the verbal root. It is important to recognize the Tsere preformative vowel (יֵשֵׁב) as diagnostic of this class of I-י verbs. Recognizing this feature will help you to recover the missing verbal root in first position.
         2.      The verb הָלַךְ (to walk) inflects just like the Type 1 verb above with a loss of the initial root consonant. This verb occurs frequently and so it is important to be able to recognize these forms.
         3.      In the Type 2 paradigm, the initial י of the verbal root is seemingly preserved as the vowel letter Hireq Yod (יִירַשׁ). The stem vowel is Pathach except in those forms that have a sufformative consisting of a vowel. Identification of the verbal root in this class should not be as difficult as with Type 1 verbs because of the Hireq Yod preformative vowel.

    16.9      I-נ Notes.
         1.      With I-נ verbs, the initial root consonant (נ) has assimilated into the second consonant of the verbal root and is represented by a Daghesh Forte. In other words, יִנְפֹּל has become יִפֹּל with the assimilation of the נ. The presence of this Daghesh Forte will enable you to identify the verbal root as I-נ.
         2.      The verb לָקַח (to take) inflects just like a I-נ verb with assimilation of the first root consonant (ל) into the second root consonant (ק) as Daghesh Forte (יִקַּח). Observe, however, that the Daghesh Forte is given up in the 2fs, 3mp, and 2mp forms due to the rule of sqnmlwy (5.5). This verb occurs frequently and so it is important to be able to recognize its Imperfect forms.
         3.      In the above doubly weak paradigm (I-נ and II-Guttural), you will notice that the נ which is the first root consonant does not assimilate into the second root consonant. The expected assimilation does not occur because the second root consonant is a guttural and, therefore, rejects the assimilation of the נ because it cannot take a Daghesh Forte.

    16.11      III-ה Notes.
         1.      Imperfect forms without a sufformative have an ֶה ending. The final ה in these forms is not, however, the original third root consonant but rather a vowel letter. While this final ה is not the original consonant, it does make identification of the verbal root easier in these forms. Even in the derived stems, the ֶה ending will be diagnostic of most III-ה Imperfect forms.
         2.      Imperfect forms with a sufformative will not take the ֶה ending (יִבְנוּ). This is an important feature to recognize. When you encounter such a form, it will exhibit only two of the three original root consonants. In order to determine the lexical form, therefore, you will need to reconstruct the original root consonants based upon your understanding of how III-ה verbal roots inflect.
         3.      In the twin 3fp and 2fp forms, the stem vowel is Seghol Yod (תִּבְנֶ֫ינָה). This long stem vowel should remind you of the Hireq Yod stem vowel found in the Perfect conjugation of this weak verb class (compare the Perfect 2ms form בָּנִ֫יתָ).

    16.13      Geminate Notes.
         1.      There are three types of Geminate verbs, each of which is distinguished by its preformative vowel in most forms. Type 1 has a Qamets preformative vowel (יָסֹב). Type 2 has a Hireq preformative vowel (יִסֹּב). Type 3 has a Tsere preformative vowel (יֵתַם). Note the irregular and unexpected Daghesh Forte in the first root consonant of the Type 2 pattern (יִסֹּב). Other minor variations also exist.
         2.      In every form of the Geminate paradigms above, only one of the Geminate consonants remains. In those forms with a sufformative, the presence of a Daghesh Forte in the remaining Geminate consonant should help you to identify the verbal root. In those forms without a sufformative, there is no Daghesh Forte in the Geminate consonant. This will make identification of the verbal root more difficult, as you will simply have to recognize the Geminate class as a possibility.

    16.15      Doubly Weak Notes.
         1.      In the three III-ה paradigms (עָשָׂה, רָאָה, and הָיָה), note the ֶה ending, except in those forms with sufformatives. The ֶה ending is diagnostic of III-ה Imperfect verbs in both the Qal and derived stems.
         2.      The verb עָשָׂה is both III-ה and I-Guttural. Note the vowel pattern of Pathach under the Imperfect preformative and Hateph Pathach under the guttural in first root position. This is the expected pattern in the Type 2 class of I-Guttural verbs (16.2). Even the Seghol/Hateph Seghol pattern is preserved in the 1cs form (אֶעֱשֶׂה).
         3.      Study the Imperfect forms of הָיָה with care. You will observe that this doubly weak verb inflects exactly like the Imperfect of בָּנָה (16.10). Careful study is required, however, because the presence of the י in second root position creates certain forms that appear to be irregular in their inflection such as תִּהְיִי (2fs), יִהְיוּ (3mp), תִּהְיוּ (2mp), and even תִּהְיֶ֫ינָה (3fp and 2fp). In fact, these forms are inflected exactly like their counterparts in the בָּנָה paradigm above.
         4.      In the Imperfect of נָשָׂא, most forms preserve a Daghesh Forte in the second consonant of the verbal root as in יִשָּׂא (3ms). This Daghesh Forte represents the assimilated נ that is the first root consonant. Observe, however, that the Daghesh Forte is given up in the 2fs, 3mp, and 2mp forms (5.5).

    16.16      Reconstructing Verbal Roots. As you know by now, certain weak verbs may lose one or more of their original root consonants. For example, in the above paradigm of נָפַל (to fall), the Imperfect 3ms form יִפֹּל (he will fall) exhibits the loss of the first root consonant. This type of loss can make identification of the full verbal root a difficult task. How, therefore, do you identify a verbal root when one or more of the original root consonants is missing? The first step is to know your options. With the Imperfect, there are four triconsonantal weak verb classes that appear with only two root letters when inflected in the Qal Imperfect: I-י, I-נ, III-ה, and Geminate. Second, you must memorize those clues or diagnostic features that distinguish each weak verb class in order to properly reconstruct the verbal root for parsing and translation.
         1.      With I-י verbs that are Type 1, the initial root consonant drops off in every form. For example, the Imperfect 3ms form of יָשַׁב is יֵשֵׁב. The initial י in יֵשֵׁב is the 3ms preformative, not the root consonant. As such, this weak verbal form preserves only two of the three original root consonants. If you are unfamiliar with the verbal root, how do you know what has dropped off? Is this verb Geminate (שָׁבַב), III-ה (שָׁבָה), I-י (יָשַׁב), or I-נ (נָשַׁב)? In this case, the key to identifying the verbal root is the Tsere preformative vowel (יֵשֵׁב). The Tsere preformative vowel is the diagnostic feature for the I-י Imperfect conjugation in the Qal stem (16.6). By knowing this, you will be able to correctly reconstruct the verbal root without having to look up numerous other verbal root options in a lexicon.
         2.      Most I-נ verbs (and לָקַח) assimilate their first root consonant into the second root consonant with a resulting Daghesh Forte. For example, the Imperfect 3ms form of נָפַל (to fall) is יִפֹּל (he will fall). In this case, the Daghesh Forte in the second root consonant (יִפֹּל) will identify the I-נ weak verbal root (16.8).
         3.      With III-ה verbal roots, the loss of the final ה root consonant can make identification difficult. In those forms without a sufformative, a final ה vowel letter is present (יִבְנֶה). The presence of this vowel letter makes reconstructing the original verbal root much easier. In the remaining forms of the Imperfect having a sufformative, the final ה is altogether absent. For example, the Imperfect 3mp form of בָּנָה (to build) is יִבְנוּ (they will build). Note the absence of the final root consonant. In this case, it is helpful to use a process of elimination: (1) there is no Daghesh Forte in the ב of this form and so it is not I-נ; (2) the preformative vowel is not Tsere and so it is not I-י; and (3) it could be Geminate but the preformative vowel of a Geminate verb is usually Qamets or Tsere. The only other option is III-ה (16.10). This might seem like a complicated process at first, but it is better than trying to look up numerous, possibly nonexistent, forms in a Hebrew lexicon.
         4.      With Geminate verbs, only one Geminate consonant remains in the Imperfect conjugation. Some Geminate forms of the Imperfect exhibit a Daghesh Forte in the remaining Geminate consonant (יָסֹ֫בּוּ). In such instances, this clue should enable you to identify the weak verbal form as Geminate (16.12). In those forms without the Daghesh Forte, you will want to use the same process of elimination as with III-ה verbs, though it is more difficult with Geminate verbs because they exhibit considerable variation. It will be helpful to remember that Qal Imperfect Geminate verbs occur only 163 times in the Hebrew Bible, while III-ה verbs occur over 3,500 times.

    16.18      Biconsonantal Notes.
         1.      In all forms (except 3fp and 2fp) and in every class, the Imperfect preformative vowel is Qamets (יָקוּם and יָשִׂים and יָבוֹא).
         2.      The stem vowel of every form is determined by the Biconsonantal sub-class: Shureq (יָקוּם), Hireq Yod (יָשִׂים), or Holem Waw (יָבוֹא).
         3.      It should be noted that some mixing of the diagnostic stem vowel does occur. For example, יָשִׂים is sometimes spelled יָשׂוּם (Exod 4:11). It should also be noted that the stem vowel may be written defectively. For example, יָקוּם may appear as יָקֻם (Gen 27:31).

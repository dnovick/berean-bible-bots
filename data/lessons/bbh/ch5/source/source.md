# BBH Chapter 5 — Definite Article and Conjunction וְ

*Source: Pratico & Van Pelt, Basics of Biblical Hebrew Grammar (BBH), Chapter 5*

---

## 5.1 Introduction (§5.1)

Hebrew nouns are either **definite** or **indefinite**. There is no indefinite article in Hebrew; a noun without the definite article is indefinite. Three types of nouns are definite: (1) nouns with the definite article, (2) proper nouns, and (3) nouns with possessive pronouns.

---

## 5.2 Form of the Hebrew Definite Article (§5.2)

A noun is made definite by prefixing **הַ** (He + Pathach) plus a **Daghesh Forte** in the first consonant of the noun.

| Indefinite | Translation | Definite | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| מִזְבֵּחַ | an altar | הַמִּזְבֵּחַ | the altar |
| סוּס | a horse | הַסּוּס | the horse |
| מָקוֹם | a place | הַמָּקוֹם | the place |
| לֵב | a heart | הַלֵּב | the heart |

---

## 5.3 The Article Prefixed to Begadkephat Consonants (§5.3)

When the definite article is added to a word with an initial begadkephat consonant, the Daghesh Lene becomes a Daghesh Forte.

| Indefinite | Translation | Definite | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| בַּ֫יִת | a house | הַבַּ֫יִת | the house |
| דֶּ֫רֶךְ | a road | הַדֶּ֫רֶךְ | the road |
| תּוֹרָה | a law | הַתּוֹרָה | the law |

---

## 5.4 The Article Prefixed to Guttural Consonants (§5.4)

Gutturals (and ר) reject the Daghesh Forte. Three outcomes result:

### 1 — Compensatory Lengthening (with א, ע, ר)

The Pathach of the article lengthens to Qamets (הָ).

| Indefinite | Translation | Definite | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| אִישׁ | a man | הָאִישׁ | the man |
| אִשָּׁה | a woman | הָאִשָּׁה | the woman |
| רֹאשׁ | a head | הָרֹאשׁ | the head |
| עִיר | a city | הָעִיר | the city |

### 2 — Virtual Doubling (with ה, ח)

The Daghesh Forte is rejected but the Pathach does **not** lengthen.

| Indefinite | Translation | Definite | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| הֵיכָל | a palace | הַהֵיכָל | the palace |
| חוֹמָה | a wall | הַחוֹמָה | the wall |

### 3 — Irregular Seghol Vowel

Before nouns beginning with unaccented הָ, חָ, or עָ, the article appears with Seghol (הֶ) and no Daghesh Forte.

| Indefinite | Translation | Definite | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| חָכָם | a wise man | הֶחָכָם | the wise man |
| עָנָן | a cloud | הֶעָנָן | the cloud |

---

## 5.5 The Article Prefixed to יְ and מְ (§5.5)

Words beginning with יְ or מְ usually give up the Daghesh Forte without lengthening the Pathach.

| Indefinite | Translation | Definite | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| יְלָדִים | boys | הַיְלָדִים | the boys |
| מְרַגְּלִים | spies | הַמְרַגְּלִים | the spies |

> The loss of the Daghesh Forte is governed by the rule of **sqnmlwy** (ס צ שׁ שׂ ק נ מ ל ו י): when one of these consonants carries Daghesh Forte + Vocal Shewa, the Daghesh Forte frequently drops out.

---

## 5.6 Vowel Changes with the Definite Article (§5.6)

A small group of nouns change their first vowel to Qamets when the article is prefixed.

| Indefinite | Definite | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| אֶ֫רֶץ | הָאָ֫רֶץ | the earth |
| עַם | הָעָם | the people |
| גַּן | הַגָּן | the garden |
| הַר | הָהָר | the mountain |
| אֲרוֹן | הָאָרוֹן | the ark |

---

## 5.7 The Conjunction וְ (§5.7)

The conjunction **וְ** (and, but, also, even) is the most frequent word in the Hebrew Bible (50,000+ occurrences). It is always prefixed to the following word.

### Spelling Variations

| Condition | Form | Example | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Before most consonants | וְ | וְאִישׁ | and a man |
| Before ב, מ, or פ ("bump" rule) | וּ | וּמֶ֫לֶךְ | and a king |
| Before consonant with Vocal Shewa | וּ | וּנְעָרִים | and young men |
| Before הָ, חָ, or עָ (Hateph vowel) — Hateph Pathach | וַ | וַאֲנָשִׁים | and men |
| Before הָ, חָ, or עָ (Hateph vowel) — Hateph Seghol | וֶ | וֶאֱמֶת | and truth |
| Before הָ, חָ, or עָ (Hateph vowel) — Hateph Qamets | וָ | וָחֳלִי | and sickness |
| Before monosyllabic or initially accented words | וָ | וָצֹאן | and sheep |
| Before יְ syllable | וִי | וִיהוּדָה | and Judah |

> **Key:** When אֱלֹהִים occurs with the conjunction, the quiescing of א produces וֵאלֹהִים (see §4.11.4).

> Regardless of the vowel under ו, a word-initial ו is the conjunction over 99% of the time.

---

## 5.8 Summary (§5.8)

1. There is no indefinite article in Hebrew. Nouns without the article are indefinite.
2. The definite article = הַ + Daghesh Forte in the first consonant of the noun (הַמֶּ֫לֶךְ).
3. Article variations with initial gutturals/ר:
   - א, ע, ר → compensatory lengthening: Pathach → Qamets (הָ)
   - ה, ח → virtual doubling: Daghesh Forte rejected; Pathach unchanged
   - Before unaccented הָ/חָ/עָ → irregular Seghol vowel (הֶ), no Daghesh Forte
4. Words beginning with יְ or מְ may lose the Daghesh Forte (sqnmlwy rule).
5. The conjunction וְ (and, but, also, even) is always prefixed; its vowel varies by context (see table above).

## 5.10 Additional Information: Special Uses of the Definite Article (§5.10)

1. **Demonstrative Adjective.** The article may function as "this" or "that": אָנֹכִי מְצַוְּךָ הַיּוֹם — "I am commanding you **this** day."
2. **Vocative.** The article marks direct address: הַמֶּ֫לֶךְ — "O king."
3. **Superlative.** The article may indicate the superlative of adjectives: הַטּוֹב — "the best."
4. **Possessive Pronoun.** The article may function as a possessive: דָּוִד הַכִּנּוֹר — "David … **his** harp."

---

## 5.11 Additional Information: Indefinite Article (§5.11)

Hebrew has no indefinite article. The words for "one" — אֶחָד (ms) and אַחַת (fs) — are sometimes used to mark an indefinite noun:

| Hebrew | Literal | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| אִישׁ אֶחָד | one man | a man / a certain man |
| אִשָּׁה אַחַת | one woman | a woman / a certain woman |

# BBH Chapter 24 — The Niphal Stem: Strong Verbs

*Source: Pratico & Van Pelt, Basics of Biblical Hebrew Grammar (BBH), Chapter 24*

---

## 24.1 Introduction (§24.1)

Having studied the Qal stem, you are now equipped to identify over 69 percent of all verbal forms in the Hebrew Bible. It is now time to begin studying the derived stems and their conjugations. Review the introduction to verbal stems in §12.5–10. The diagnostic approach to the derived stems assumes you have memorized all of the strong verb paradigms for the Qal stem.

---

## 24.2 The Meaning of the Niphal Stem (§24.2)

1. **Passive.** The Niphal stem is commonly used to express simple action with a passive voice. For example, שָׁמַע in the Qal is "he heard," while נִשְׁמַע in the Niphal may be translated "he was heard."

2. **Reflexive.** The Niphal may also express simple action with a reflexive voice: "he heard himself."

3. **Reciprocal.** The Niphal may carry a reciprocal meaning: נִשְׁמְעוּ — "they heard one another."

4. **Simple Active.** Some Niphal verbs are translated with an active voice, especially when the root is rare or unattested in the Qal.

| Qal | Niphal | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| לָחַם | נִלְחַם | he fought |
| שָׁבַע | נִשְׁבַּע | he swore (an oath) |
| שָׁאַר | נִשְׁאַר | he survived |

5. There are a few other Niphal categories, but these are infrequent. Any standard lexicon will provide the meaning of a verbal root in the Niphal stem.

---

## 24.3 The Conjugations of the Niphal Stem (§24.3)

In the Niphal paradigms, a נ is added to the verbal root in every form — this is the נ of the Niphal. In the Perfect, Participle, and one form of the Infinitive Absolute, this נ appears as the consonant itself. In the Imperfect, Imperative, Infinitive Construct, and the other Infinitive Absolute form, the נ assimilates into the first consonant of the verbal root and becomes a Daghesh Forte.

The following Niphal paradigms use the strong verb קָטַל (to kill) for illustration, with Qal paradigms for comparison. Memorization of these paradigms is not required — memorize only the diagnostic features for each conjugation.

---

## 24.4 Niphal Perfect (§24.4)

The Niphal Perfect is formed with the Niphal stem prefix (נִ), the verbal root, and the Perfect sufformatives. Translate the Niphal Perfect passively: "he was killed."

| **3ms** | קָטַל | נִקְטַל | he was killed |

| **2ms** | קָטַ֫לְתָּ | נִקְטַ֫לְתָּ | you were killed |
| **2fs** | קָטַלְתְּ | נִקְטַלְתְּ | you were killed |
| **1cs** | קָטַ֫לְתִּי | נִקְטַ֫לְתִּי | I was killed |
| **3cp** | קָֽטְלוּ | נִקְטְלוּ | they were killed |
| **2mp** | קְטַלְתֶּם | נִקְטַלְתֶּם | you were killed |

---

## 24.5 Niphal Perfect Diagnostics (§24.5)

1. **נִ prefix in every form.** This distinguishes the Niphal Perfect from the Qal and from all other derived stems.

2. **Pathach stem vowel** in all forms except 3fs (נִקְטְלָה) and 3cp (נִקְטְלוּ), which have a Vocal Shewa because their sufformatives begin with or consist of a vowel.

**Niphal Perfect diagnostic form: נִקְטַל**

---

## 24.6 Niphal Imperfect (§24.6)

The Niphal Imperfect is formed with the Imperfect preformatives, the verbal root with assimilated נ (Daghesh Forte in the first root consonant), and the Imperfect sufformatives. Translate the Niphal Imperfect passively: "he will be killed."

| | **Qal** | **Niphal** | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **3ms** | יִקְטֹל | יִקָּטֵל | he will be killed |
| **3fs** | תִּקְטֹל | תִּקָּטֵל | she will be killed |
| **2ms** | תִּקְטֹל | תִּקָּטֵל | you will be killed |
| **2fs** | תִּקְטְלִי | תִּקָּֽטְלִי | you will be killed |
| **1cs** | אֶקְטֹל | אֶקָּטֵל | I will be killed |
| **3mp** | יִקְטְלוּ | יִקָּֽטְלוּ | they will be killed |
| **3fp** | תִּקְטֹ֫לְנָה | תִּקָּטַ֫לְנָה | they will be killed |
| **2mp** | תִּקְטְלוּ | תִּקָּֽטְלוּ | you will be killed |
| **2fp** | תִּקְטֹ֫לְנָה | תִּקָּטַ֫לְנָה | you will be killed |
| **1cp** | נִקְטֹל | נִקָּטֵל | we will be killed |

---

## 24.7 Niphal Imperfect Diagnostics (§24.7)

1. **Hireq preformative vowel** in all forms (יִקָּטֵל), except the 1cs (אֶקָּטֵל), which has Seghol like the Qal.

2. **Daghesh Forte in the first consonant of the verbal root** (יִקָּטֵל) — the assimilated נ of the Niphal. This also occurs in the Imperative, Infinitive Construct, and one form of the Infinitive Absolute.

3. **Qamets under the first consonant of the verbal root** in every form (יִקָּטֵל). The stem vowel is not diagnostic.

4. Do not confuse the Qal Imperfect 1cp (נִקְטֹל) with the Niphal Imperfect 1cp (נִקָּטֵל).

**Niphal Imperfect diagnostic form: יִקָּטֵל**

---

## 24.8 Niphal Imperative (§24.8)

The Niphal Imperative is formed with a הִ prefix, the verbal root with assimilated נ (Daghesh Forte), and the Imperative sufformatives. Translate the Niphal Imperative passively: "be killed!"

| | **Qal** | **Niphal** | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **2ms** | קְטֹל | הִקָּטֵל | be killed! |
| **2fs** | קִטְלִי | הִקָּֽטְלִי | be killed! |
| **2mp** | קִטְלוּ | הִקָּֽטְלוּ | be killed! |
| **2fp** | קְטֹ֫לְנָה | הִקָּטַ֫לְנָה | be killed! |

---

## 24.9 Niphal Infinitive Construct (§24.9)

The masculine singular Imperative (הִקָּטֵל) and the Infinitive Construct form (הִקָּטֵל) are identical.

---

## 24.10 Niphal Imperative and Infinitive Construct Diagnostics (§24.10)

1. **הִ prefix** in every form of the Imperative and in the Infinitive Construct.

2. **Daghesh Forte in the first consonant of the verbal root** — the assimilated נ of the Niphal.

3. **Qamets under the first consonant of the verbal root** in every form.

4. The stem vowel is variable in the Imperative but is not diagnostic.

**Niphal Imperative and Infinitive Construct diagnostic form: הִקָּטֵל**

---

## 24.11 Niphal Infinitive Absolute (§24.11)

There are two forms for the Niphal Infinitive Absolute. The first preserves the נ of the Niphal; the second has the הִ prefix with the assimilated נ.

| **Qal** | **Niphal — Form 1** | **Niphal — Form 2** |
|---|---|---|
| קָטוֹל | נִקְטוֹל | הִקָּטוֹל |

---

## 24.12 Niphal Infinitive Absolute Diagnostics (§24.12)

1. The Niphal Infinitive Absolute occurs only 37 times in the Hebrew Bible. Both forms appear with equal frequency.

2. Both forms preserve the Holem Waw stem vowel (like the Qal Infinitive Absolute). The Holem Waw may be written defectively as Holem (הִקָּטֹל), or the stem vowel may be Tsere (הִקָּטֵל), making it identical in form to the 2ms Imperative and Infinitive Construct. Context — especially when the Infinitive Absolute appears with another verb from the same root — will normally enable identification.

**Niphal Infinitive Absolute diagnostic forms: נִקְטוֹל / הִקָּטוֹל**

---

## 24.13 Niphal Participle (§24.13)

The Niphal Participle is formed with the Niphal prefix (נִ), the verbal root, and the same inflectional endings as the Qal Participle. Participles are verbal adjectives and inflect for gender and number but not for person.

| | **Qal** | **Niphal** | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ms** | קֹטֵל | נִקְטָל | being killed |
| **fs** | קֹטֶ֫לֶת | נִקְטֶ֫לֶת | being killed |
| **mp** | קֹטְלִים | נִקְטָלִים | being killed |
| **fp** | קֹטְלוֹת | נִקְטָלוֹת | being killed |

---

## 24.14 Niphal Participle Diagnostics (§24.14)

1. **נִ prefix** in all Participle forms.

2. **Qamets stem vowel** in all forms (נִקְטָל), except the feminine singular (נִקְטֶ֫לֶת).

3. Note that the only difference between the Niphal Perfect 3ms (נִקְטַל) and the Participle ms (נִקְטָל) is the stem vowel: Pathach in the Perfect, Qamets in the Participle.

**Niphal Participle diagnostic form: נִקְטָל**

---

## 24.15 Summary (§24.15)

1. The Niphal stem expresses simple action with passive or reflexive voice. It may also express reciprocal action or, for certain verbs, the active voice.

2. The נ stem prefix is added to every form. In the Perfect, Participle, and one form of the Infinitive Absolute (נִקְטוֹל), the נ appears as a consonant. In the Imperfect, Imperative, Infinitive Construct, and the other Infinitive Absolute form (הִקָּטוֹל), the נ assimilates into the first root consonant and becomes a Daghesh Forte.

3. Memorize the diagnostic features of spelling, not the full paradigms:

**The Niphal Stem: Strong Verb Diagnostic Chart**

| Conjugation | Diagnostic Form | Key Features |
|---|---|---|
| **Perfect** | נִקְטַל | נִ prefix; Pathach stem vowel |
| **Imperfect** | יִקָּטֵל | Hireq preformative; Daghesh Forte + Qamets in R1 |
| **Imperative** | הִקָּטֵל | הִ prefix; Daghesh Forte + Qamets in R1 |
| **Infinitive Construct** | הִקָּטֵל | Identical to Imperative 2ms |
| **Infinitive Absolute** | נִקְטוֹל / הִקָּטוֹל | Two alternate forms |
| **Participle** | נִקְטָל | נִ prefix; Qamets stem vowel |

## 24.17 Additional Information: Disjunctive Waw (§24.17)

Hebrew clauses are frequently connected by the conjunction וְ. There are two basic uses: **conjunctive** (linking clauses sequentially, temporally, or logically, often with a verbal form) and **disjunctive** (prefixed to a non-verbal form, introducing a break or interruption in the narrative). Four major uses of the disjunctive Waw:

1. **Parenthetical.** Interrupts the narrative to provide explanatory information.

> וְהֵם לֹא יָדְעוּ כִּי שֹׁמֵעַ יוֹסֵף כִּי הַמֵּלִיץ בֵּינֹתָם
>
> Now they did not know that Joseph was understanding (them) because there was an interpreter between them (Gen 42:23).

2. **Circumstantial.** Introduces circumstantial information relating to the main action.

> וַיְהִי כְּהַיּוֹם הַזֶּה וַיָּבֹא הַבַּיְתָה לַעֲשׂוֹת מְלַאכְתּוֹ וְאֵין אִישׁ מֵאַנְשֵׁי הַבַּיִת שָׁם בַּבָּיִת
>
> (And) one day, he went into the house to do his work and none of the household servants was there in the house (Gen 39:11).

3. **Contrastive.** Introduces a contrastive idea; translated "but."

> וַיִּשַׁע יְהוָה אֶל־הֶבֶל וְאֶל־מִנְחָתוֹ וְאֶל־קַיִן וְאֶל־מִנְחָתוֹ לֹא שָׁעָה
>
> Yahweh looked favorably on Abel and his offering, but on Cain and his offering he did not look favorably (Gen 4:4b–5a).

4. **Introductory.** Begins a new narrative or introduces a new idea.

> וְהַנָּחָשׁ הָיָה עָרוּם מִכֹּל חַיַּת הַשָּׂדֶה אֲשֶׁר עָשָׂה יְהוָה אֱלֹהִים
>
> Now the serpent was more crafty than any of the creatures that Yahweh God had made (Gen 3:1).

## 24.18 Something You Should Know: The Covenant Name of God, Part 1 (§24.18)

> God is called by many names throughout the Hebrew Bible. אֱלֹהִים (God) occurs 2,602 times; אֵל (God), 236 times. The name used most commonly is יהוה ("Yahweh" or "LORD") — the tetragrammaton — occurring 6,828 times (19.1% of all proper names in the Hebrew Bible). The Old Testament is, first and foremost, about the words, acts, and character of the one known as יהוה.
>
> Exodus 3:13–15 is the first essential text for understanding the revelation and meaning of יהוה. In Exod 3:13, Moses asks:
>
> הִנֵּה אָנֹכִי בָא אֶל־בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל וְאָמַרְתִּי לָהֶם אֱלֹהֵי אֲבוֹתֵיכֶם שְׁלָחַנִי אֲלֵיכֶם וְאָמְרוּ־לִי מַה־שְּׁמוֹ מָה אֹמַר אֲלֵהֶם׃
>
> Suppose I go to the people of Israel and I say to them, "The God of your fathers has sent me to you," and they ask me, "What is his name?" Then what shall I say to them?
>
> The question מַה־שְּׁמוֹ is supremely theological. The noun שֵׁם (name) communicates personhood, character, reputation, and authority. In Exod 3:14, God identifies himself:
>
> וַיֹּאמֶר אֱלֹהִים אֶל־מֹשֶׁה אֶהְיֶה אֲשֶׁר אֶהְיֶה וַיֹּאמֶר כֹּה תֹאמַר לִבְנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל אֶהְיֶה שְׁלָחַנִי אֲלֵיכֶם
>
> God said to Moses, "I AM who I AM." And he said, "Say this to the people of Israel, 'I AM has sent me to you.' "
>
> The two identical verbs אֶהְיֶה אֲשֶׁר אֶהְיֶה are Qal Imperfect 1cs from היה with the relative pronoun between them: "I AM who/what I AM" or "I WILL BE who/what I WILL BE." In 3:15, the divine self-revelation culminates:
>
> וַיֹּאמֶר עוֹד אֱלֹהִים אֶל־מֹשֶׁה כֹּה־תֹאמַר אֶל־בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵי אֲבֹתֵיכֶם אֱלֹהֵי אַבְרָהָם אֱלֹהֵי יִצְחָק וְאלֹהֵי יַעֲקֹב שְׁלָחַנִי אֲלֵיכֶם זֶה־שְּׁמִי לְעֹלָם וְזֶה זִכְרִי לְדֹר דֹּר
>
> God also said to Moses, "Say this to the people of Israel, 'Yahweh, the God of your fathers, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob, has sent me to you. This is my name forever, and in this way, I am to be remembered throughout all generations.' "
>
> The use of היה in Exod 3:13–15 implies more than mere ontological being. Yahweh is not only the God who is; he is also the God who acts. The exodus is to the Old Testament what the incarnation is to the New.

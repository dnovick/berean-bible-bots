# BBH Chapter 7 — Hebrew Adjectives

*Source: Pratico & Van Pelt, Basics of Biblical Hebrew Grammar (BBH), Chapter 7*

---

## 7.1 Introduction (§7.1)

Hebrew adjectives function just like English adjectives — they modify, describe, characterize, or classify nouns. Hebrew adjectives inflect (change form) to indicate gender and number. They are inflected in four forms: masculine singular, masculine plural, feminine singular, feminine plural.

---

## 7.2 Inflection of Adjectives (§7.2)

The inflectional endings for adjectives are the **same endings** already learned for nouns.

**Paradigm of טוֹב (good):**

| | Masculine | Feminine |
|---|---|---|
| **Singular** | טוֹב | טוֹבָה |
| **Plural** | טוֹבִים | טוֹבוֹת |

---

## 7.3 Notes on Inflection (§7.3)

1. Hebrew adjectives **agree in gender and number** with the nouns they modify. The gender of an adjective does not change its meaning.
2. Adjectives that are **plural in form** are **singular in translation value** (unless used substantively, §7.5.3). The plural form reflects agreement with the noun, not a plural meaning.

---

## 7.4 Basic Patterns of Adjectival Inflection (§7.4)

### Pattern 1 — No Change

Monosyllabic adjectives like טוֹב do not change when inflected.

| | Masculine | Feminine |
|---|---|---|
| **Singular** | טוֹב | טוֹבָה |
| **Plural** | טוֹבִים | טוֹבוֹת |

### Pattern 2 — Propretonic Reduction

Adjectives with a changeable long vowel in the pretonic syllable undergo propretonic reduction when inflectional endings are added.

**a. Pattern of גָּדוֹל (great):**

| | Masculine | Feminine |
|---|---|---|
| **Singular** | גָּדוֹל | גְּדוֹלָה |
| **Plural** | גְּדוֹלִים | גְּדוֹלוֹת |

**b. Pattern of זָקֵן (old):**

| | Masculine | Feminine |
|---|---|---|
| **Singular** | זָקֵן | זְקֵנָה |
| **Plural** | זְקֵנִים | זְקֵנוֹת |

**c. Pattern of יָשָׁר (upright):**

| | Masculine | Feminine |
|---|---|---|
| **Singular** | יָשָׁר | יְשָׁרָה |
| **Plural** | יְשָׁרִים | יְשָׁרוֹת |

**d. Initial guttural — Hateph Pathach instead of Vocal Shewa:**

**Pattern of חָכָם (wise):**

| | Masculine | Feminine |
|---|---|---|
| **Singular** | חָכָם | חֲכָמָה |
| **Plural** | חֲכָמִים | חֲכָמוֹת |

### Pattern 3 — Adjectives with ֶה Ending

The ֶה ending is dropped before inflectional endings are added.

**Pattern of קָשֶׁה (difficult):**

| | Masculine | Feminine |
|---|---|---|
| **Singular** | קָשֶׁה | קָשָׁה |
| **Plural** | קָשִׁים | קָשׁוֹת |

### Pattern 4 — Geminate Adjectives

Like Geminate nouns, Geminate adjectives take a Daghesh Forte in the second consonant with inflectional endings.

**Pattern of רַב (many):**

| | Masculine | Feminine |
|---|---|---|
| **Singular** | רַב | רַבָּה |
| **Plural** | רַבִּים | רַבּוֹת |

**Geminate adjectives with a guttural** (e.g., רַע evil) cannot take the Daghesh Forte — compensatory lengthening occurs instead:

| | Masculine | Feminine |
|---|---|---|
| **Singular** | רַע | רָעָה |
| **Plural** | רָעִים | רָעוֹת |

## 7.5 The Use of Adjectives (§7.5)

### 1 — Attributive Use

Directly modifies a noun. **Rules:**
- Follows the noun it modifies
- Agrees in gender, number, and **definiteness**

| Hebrew | Translation |
|---|---|
| אִישׁ טוֹב | a good man |
| הָאִישׁ הַטּוֹב | the good man |
| אִשָּׁה טוֹבָה | a good woman |
| הָאִשָּׁה הַטּוֹבָה | the good woman |
| הָאֲנָשִׁים הַטּוֹבִים | the good men |
| הַנָּשִׁים הַטּוֹבוֹת | the good women |

### 2 — Predicative Use

Asserts something about a noun (like "the man **is** good"). **Rules:**
- Agrees in gender and number but **not definiteness**
- Never takes the definite article
- May precede or follow the noun

| Hebrew | Translation |
|---|---|
| טוֹב הָאִישׁ / הָאִישׁ טוֹב | The man is good. |
| טוֹבָה הָאִשָּׁה / הָאִשָּׁה טוֹבָה | The woman is good. |
| טוֹבִים הָאֲנָשִׁים / הָאֲנָשִׁים טוֹבִים | The men are good. |
| טוֹבוֹת הַנָּשִׁים / הַנָּשִׁים טוֹבוֹת | The women are good. |

> **Ambiguity:** אָב זָקֵן can be either "an old father" (attributive) or "a father is old" (predicative). Context determines the usage.

> Proper nouns are definite and may appear with predicative adjectives: קָדוֹשׁ קָדוֹשׁ קָדוֹשׁ יְהוָה — "Holy, holy, holy is Yahweh" (Isa 6:3).

### 3 — Substantive Use

Used independently as a noun — no noun for the adjective to modify.

| Hebrew | Translation |
|---|---|
| חָכָם | a wise one / a wise man |
| הֶחָכָם | the wise one / the wise man |
| בֵּין טוֹב וּבֵין רָע | between good and evil (Lev 27:12) |
| עִם הַיָּשָׁר | with the one who is upright (Mic 2:7) |

---

## 7.6 Lexical Form (§7.6)

The **lexical form** of a Hebrew adjective is always the **masculine singular** form.

---

## 7.7 The Directional Ending (§7.7)

The directional ending **ָה** (always unaccented) is added to a word to express **motion toward** someone or something. It is equivalent to a prepositional phrase with אֶל־.

| Base Form | Translation | With Directional Ending | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| בַּ֫יִת | house | הַבַּ֫יְתָה | to(ward) the house |
| עִיר | city | הָעִ֫ירָה | to(ward) the city |
| שָׁמַ֫יִם | heaven | הַשָּׁמַ֫יְמָה | heavenward |
| מִצְרַ֫יִם | Egypt | מִצְרַ֫יְמָה | to(ward) Egypt |
| הַר | mountain | הָהָ֫רָה | to(ward) the mountain |
| שָׁם | there | שָׁ֫מָּה | to that place |
| יָם | sea, west | יָ֫מָּה | westward |

**Distinguishing the three ָה endings:**

| Ending | Type | Accented? | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| ָה | Feminine singular noun | Yes | תּוֹרָה |
| ָה | Feminine singular adjective | Yes | גְּדוֹלָה |
| ָה | Directional ending | **No** | אַ֫רְצָה |
| ָהּ | 3fs pronominal suffix (has Mappiq) | — | לָהּ |

---

## 7.8 Summary (§7.8)

1. Hebrew adjectives inflect in four forms (ms, mp, fs, fp) using the same endings as nouns.
2. Adjectives agree with the nouns they modify in gender and number; gender does not affect translation value.
3. Plural adjectives are singular in translation value.
4. Know the four patterns of adjectival inflection (§7.4): no change, propretonic reduction, ֶה-ending, and Geminate.
5. Three adjectival uses: attributive (follows noun; agrees in gender, number, definiteness), predicative (no article; agrees in gender and number only), substantive (used as a noun).
6. Lexical form = masculine singular.
7. The directional ending ָה (unaccented) expresses motion toward; translated "to" or "toward."

## 7.10 Something You Should Know: The Feast of Cover-over (§7.10)

> The first metaphor we meet with in the Bible likens the Creator-Spirit to a bird hovering over the deep-and-darkness (Gen 1:2).
>
> —M. G. Kline

In Exod 12, the verb פָּסַח has traditionally been translated "pass over." But lexical evidence from both Hebrew and Egyptian indicates פָּסַח means "to hover" or "to cover." In Isa 31:5, פָּסַח parallels עוּף (hover) and גָּנַן (cover):

> As birds hovering (עוּף), so Yahweh of hosts will cover (גָּנַן) Jerusalem; covering (גָּנַן), he will deliver it, hovering (פָּסַח), he will save it.

In 1 Kgs 18:21 Elijah uses the same verb: "How long will you hover (פָּסַח) between two branches?" And in 1 Kgs 18:26 the prophets of Baal flutter (פָּסַח) around their altar.

The Passover night was therefore not a night of God passing by the Israelite homes, but of God **protectively hovering over his people** as a shielding presence — "Cover-over" would be more accurate.

> The wonder of grace deepens when we recall that by reason of his personal Presence hovering (פָּסַח) over the Israelite houses, the Lord himself was their shielding shelter (פֶּ֫סַח). The lamb is the פֶּ֫סַח and the Lord is the פֶּ֫סַח. Both are true because the Lord becomes the lamb.
>
> —Jonathan G. Kline, Senior Editor, Hendrickson Publishers; Lecturer on Hebrew Bible, Harvard University

# BBH Chapter 13 — Qal Perfect Strong Verbs

*Source: Pratico & Van Pelt, Basics of Biblical Hebrew Grammar (BBH), Chapter 13*

---

## 13.1 Introduction (§13.1)

Over two-thirds of the approximately 72,000 verbs in the Hebrew Bible occur in the Qal stem (~50,000). In this grammar, paradigm memorization is required only for the Qal stem. Derived stems are identified through **diagnostic indicators** rather than paradigm memorization.

---

## 13.2 The Qal Stem (§13.2)

The Qal is the **simple, basic verbal stem** — active voice, unnuanced action. The simple Qal action is further divided:

1. **Transitive** — can take a direct object: "the prophet wrote the book."
2. **Intransitive** — cannot take a direct object: "the king perished in the battle."
3. **Stative** — describes a state of being: "the priest is old" (expressed by a single stative verb in Hebrew, e.g., כָּבֵד "to be heavy").

---

## 13.3 The Perfect Conjugation (§13.3)

The Perfect (also called the **suffix conjugation**) is the first of two main finite conjugations in biblical Hebrew. It expresses:

- **Completed action** → translated by English past tense (he studied), present perfect (he has studied), past perfect (he had studied), or future perfect (he will have studied)
- **State of being** → often translated with English present tense (he is wise)
- **Verbs of perception or attitude** → translated with English present tense (he knows, he loves)

The Perfect signifies **aspect** — action viewed as concluded — not tense (time) apart from context.

---

## 13.4 Introduction to Qal Perfect Strong Verbs (§13.4)

The Qal Perfect paradigm **must be memorized** for both writing and oral recitation. The Perfect sufformatives (inflectional endings) are **identical** across all verbal stems (Qal, Niphal, Hiphil, Hophal, Piel, Pual, Hithpael).

---

## 13.5 The Qal Perfect Paradigm of the Strong Verb (§13.5)

The model strong verb is קָטַל ("to kill").

| PGN | Sufformative | Perfect Form | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3ms | — | קָטַל | he killed |
| 3fs | ָה | קָֽטְלָה | she killed |
| 2ms | תָּ | קָטַ֫לְתָּ | you killed |
| 2fs | תְּ | קָטַלְתְּ | you killed |
| 1cs | תִּי | קָטַ֫לְתִּי | I killed |
| 3cp | וּ | קָֽטְלוּ | they killed |
| 2mp | תֶּם | קְטַלְתֶּם | you killed |
| 2fp | תֶּן | קְטַלְתֶּן | you killed |
| 1cp | נוּ | קָטַ֫לְנוּ | we killed |

**Common Qal Perfect 3ms vowel pattern:**

The 3ms form (lexical form) carries Qamets under the first root consonant and Pathach under the second.

---

## 13.6 Notes on the Qal Perfect Paradigm (§13.6)

1. The 3ms form (קָטַל) is the **lexical form** and the only Perfect form without a sufformative.
2. Three forms are **common** (not inflected for gender): 1cs (קָטַ֫לְתִּי), 1cp (קָטַ֫לְנוּ), and 3cp (קָֽטְלוּ).
3. The 3fs (קָֽטְלָה) and 3cp (קָֽטְלוּ) carry **Metheg** to mark Qamets, distinguishing it from Qamets Hatuf.

---

## 13.8 Verbal Roots Ending in ת (§13.8)

When a root ending in ת receives a sufformative beginning with ת, the two consonants merge into one ת with **Daghesh Forte** (תת → תּ). The verb כָּרַת (to cut) illustrates:

| PGN | Form | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| 2ms | כָּרַ֫תָּ | you cut |
| 2fs | כָּרַתְּ | you cut |
| 1cs | כָּרַ֫תִּי | I cut |
| 2mp | כְּרַתֶּם | you cut |
| 2fp | כְּרַתֶּן | you cut |

---

## 13.9 Verbal Roots Ending in נ (§13.9)

When a root ending in נ receives the 1cp sufformative (נוּ), the two Nuns merge into one נּ (Daghesh Forte). Example: שָׁכַן (to dwell) → 1cp שָׁכַ֫נּוּ.

The final נ may also assimilate into the initial ת of other sufformatives: נת → תּ. Example: נָתַן (to give) → 1cs נָתַ֫תִּי.

---

## 13.10 Stative Verbs: Perfect (§13.10)

Stative verbs describe a **state of being** (he is old, she is wise) rather than an action. They are classified by their **stem vowel** (the vowel under the second root consonant):

| Stative Type | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Pathach-Stative | גָּדַל, חָכַם | to be(come) great; to be wise |
| Tsere-Stative | כָּבֵד, זָקֵן | to be heavy; to be old |
| Holem-Stative | קָטֹן, יָכֹל | to be small; to be able |

**Full Paradigm Comparison:**

| PGN | גָּדַל (Pathach) | כָּבֵד (Tsere) | קָטֹן (Holem) | קָטַל (Strong) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3ms | גָּדַל | כָּבֵד | קָטֹן | קָטַל |
| 3fs | גָּֽדְלָה | כָּֽבְדָה | קָֽטְנָה | קָֽטְלָה |
| 2ms | גָּדַ֫לְתָּ | כָּבַ֫דְתָּ | קָטֹ֫נְתָּ | קָטַ֫לְתָּ |
| 2fs | גָּדַלְתְּ | כָּבַדְתְּ | קָטֹנְתְּ | קָטַלְתְּ |
| 1cs | גָּדַ֫לְתִּי | כָּבַ֫דְתִּי | קָטֹ֫נְתִּי | קָטַ֫לְתִּי |
| 3cp | גָּֽדְלוּ | כָּֽבְדוּ | קָֽטְנוּ | קָֽטְלוּ |
| 2mp | גְּדַלְתֶּם | כְּבַדְתֶּם | קְטָנְתֶּם | קְטַלְתֶּם |
| 2fp | גְּדַלְתֶּן | כְּבַדְתֶּן | קְטָנְתֶּן | קְטַלְתֶּן |
| 1cp | גָּדַ֫לְנוּ | כָּבַ֫דְנוּ | קָטֹ֫נּוּ | קָטַ֫לְנוּ |

### Notes

1. The Pathach-Stative (גָּדַל) is spelled identically to the regular strong verb.
2. The Tsere-Stative (כָּבֵד) has the Tsere stem vowel **only in the 3ms**; all other forms follow the strong verb pattern.
3. The Holem-Stative (קָטֹן) has an o-class vowel (Holem or Qamets Hatuf) throughout, except in the 3fs and 3cp. The 1cp (קָטֹ֫נּוּ) shows assimilation of the root נ into the sufformative נ.
4. Stative verbs and adjectives share identical forms in the 3ms:

| Form | As Stative Verb | As Adjective (ms) |
|---|---|---|
| כָּבֵד | to be heavy | heavy |
| מָלֵא | to be full | full |
| טָמֵא | to be unclean | unclean |
| רָעֵב | to be hungry | hungry |

## 13.11 Parsing (§13.11)

Parsing identifies: stem, conjugation, person, gender, number, and lexical form (or verbal root).

| Form | Parse | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| זָכַ֫רְתִּי | Qal Perfect 1cs from זָכַר | I remembered |
| שָׁמַ֫רְתְּ | Qal Perfect 2fs from שָׁמַר | you observed |

---

## 13.12 The Negative Particle לֹא (§13.12)

Perfect (and Imperfect) verbs are negated with **לֹא** (also spelled לוֹא), always placed **immediately before the verb**.

| Hebrew | Translation |
|---|---|
| שְׁמַרְתֶּם אֶת־הַתּוֹרוֹת לֹא | You did not observe the laws. |
| זָכַ֫רְתָּ אֶת־הַבְּרִית לֹא | You did not remember the covenant. |

---

## 13.13 Summary (§13.13)

1. The Qal stem expresses simple action with active voice: transitive, intransitive, or stative.
2. The Perfect conjugation expresses completed action (past, present perfect, past perfect, or future perfect) and states of being.
3. The Perfect is the "suffix conjugation" — sufformatives indicate person, gender, and number.
4. Memorize the Qal Perfect paradigm of the strong verb and the Perfect sufformatives.
5. When a root ending in ת or נ receives a sufformative beginning with the same consonant, they merge into one consonant with Daghesh Forte.
6. Stative verbs are classified by stem vowel: Pathach-Stative (like the strong verb), Tsere-Stative (Tsere only in 3ms), Holem-Stative (o-class throughout except 3fs and 3cp).
7. Perfect and Imperfect verbs are negated with לֹא, placed immediately before the verb.

## 13.15 Additional Information: The Particle הִנֵּה (§13.15)

הִנֵּה (also הֵן, הֶן־) is commonly translated "behold." It occurs over 1,000 times and can stand alone or take Type 1 pronominal suffixes.

| Person | Form |
|---|---|
| 1cs | הִנֵּ֫נִי / הִנְנִי |
| 1cp | הִנֶּ֫נּוּ / הִנְנוּ |
| 2ms | הִנְּךָ |
| 2mp | הִנְּכֶם |
| 2fs | הִנָּךְ |
| 3ms | הִנּוֹ |
| 3mp | הִנָּם |

**Three common uses:**

1. **Demonstrative interjection** — stresses importance:

> וַיַּרְא אֱלֹהִים אֶת־כָּל־אֲשֶׁר עָשָׂה וְהִנֵּה־טוֹב מְאֹד
> And God saw all that he had made, and behold, (it was) very good. (Gen 1:31)

2. **Predicator of existence** — indicates the presence of someone/something with urgency:

> הִנֵּנִי וַיֹּאמֶר
> And he said, "I am here." (Gen 22:1)

3. **Introduces a situation** on which a subsequent statement is based:

> הִנֵּה שָׁמַעְתִּי בְקֹלְכֶם
> Behold, I have obeyed (heard) your voice … (1 Sam 12:1)

## 13.16 Something You Should Know: Biblical Hebrew to Modern Hebrew (§13.16)

> Biblical Hebrew had its beginnings in an early Canaanite language (eighteenth–seventeenth centuries BC). The spoken language persisted until the fall of Jerusalem in 587/6 BC. Rabbinic Hebrew developed as a spoken language in the second century BC, limited mainly to religious teaching. Modern Hebrew was not merely a natural development but an intentional reboot based on Biblical Hebrew, kick-started by Ben-Yehuda's 1879 publication and fully consolidated as Israel's principal language after 1948.
>
> Keep on delving into this rich language of the Hebrew Bible. By learning it you are participating in a long history of the language.
>
> —Nancy Erickson, Executive Editor, Zondervan Academic

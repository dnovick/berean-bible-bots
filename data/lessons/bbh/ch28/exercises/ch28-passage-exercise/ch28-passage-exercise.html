Reflection Questions
  1. Verbs 5, 7, 9, 11 all use the formula מוֹת יוּמַת. What two verb forms make up this formula? One is the Qal Infinitive Absolute (מוֹת) and one is the Hophal Imperfect (יוּמַת). What intensifying effect does the cognate absolute construction produce, and why does the law use the Hophal rather than the Qal יָמוּת?
  2. In Passage B, verbs 3 (נִתָּן, Niphal) and 4 (מֻכִּים, Hophal) are both passive. What does the difference in stem tell you about the kind of passive? What Hiphil causative does the Hophal of נכה passivize?
  3. Passage D (Gen 39:1) contains both a Hophal (הוּרַד, verb 12) and a Hiphil (הוֹרִדֻהוּ, verb 14) from the same root ירד. What is the difference in meaning between "Joseph was brought down" (Hophal) and "the Ishmaelites had brought him down" (Hiphil), and why does the narrator use both in the same verse?
  4. Passage E (Exo 40:17–18) also uses both stems of קום — הוּקַם (Hophal, verb 15) and וַיָּקֶם (Hiphil, verb 16). What grammatical feature distinguishes them? (Hint: prefix vowel and root behavior in biconsonantal roots.)

# BBH Chapter 30 — The Piel Stem: Strong Verbs

*Source: Pratico & Van Pelt, Basics of Biblical Hebrew Grammar (BBH), Chapter 30*

---

## 30.1 Introduction

In this chapter, we will study the fourth of the derived stems, the Piel. As in your study of the Niphal through Hophal stems, paradigm memorization is not required for the Piel stem. Rather, you will study certain diagnostic points of spelling that are unique to the Piel stem. The most important diagnostic features include: (1) Vocal Shewa under all Imperfect preformatives and Participle prefixes; (2) Pathach under the first root consonant of every conjugation except the Perfect; and (3) Daghesh Forte in the second root consonant of every form in every conjugation. These and a few other diagnostic points of spelling will make the Piel stem one of the easiest verbal stems in the language to recognize.

---

## 30.2 The Meaning of the Piel Stem

### 1. Intensive

The Piel stem may be used to express an intensive type of action with an active voice. In other words, the simple action of the Qal stem will take on some type of intensive nuance in the Piel stem. For example, the verb שָׁבַר means "he broke" in the Qal stem. The Piel form is שִׁבֵּר and may be translated "he smashed" or "he shattered." This intensive type of action, however, is only one of several different types of "intensive" action denoted by the Piel stem.

### 2. Factitive

The factitive use of the Piel makes an intransitive Qal verb transitive. In other words, if a Qal verb is intransitive (cannot take a direct object), it will become transitive (can take a direct object) in the Piel stem. The following examples illustrate how an intransitive Qal verb becomes transitive in the Piel.

| Qal (Intransitive) | | Piel (Factitive) |
|---|---|---|
| קָדַשׁ — to be holy | → | קִדַּשׁ — to sanctify (something) |
| אָבַד — to perish | → | אִבַּד — to destroy (something) |
| טָמֵא — to be unclean | → | טִמֵּא — to defile (something) |

### 3. Denominative

Verbs that are derived from a noun or adjective are often inflected in the Piel stem, not the Qal. When this occurs, the type of verbal action is simple and not intensive.

| Noun | | Piel (Denominative) |
|---|---|---|
| דָּבָר — word | → | דִּבֶּר — to speak |
| כֹּהֵן — priest | → | כִּהֵן — to serve as priest |
| עָפָר — dust | → | עִפַּר — to throw dirt |

### 4. Iterative

The iterative action of the Piel expresses the nuance of repeated action. This use occurs primarily with verbs that express physical movement, effort, or voice projection. This type of action is often difficult to translate into English.

| Qal | | Piel (Iterative) |
|---|---|---|
| הָלַךְ — to go, walk | → | הִלֵּךְ — to pace, walk around |
| צָעַק — to cry | → | צֵעֵק — to weep over and over, keep crying |

### 5. Simple Action

Some verbs in the Piel stem are translated just like verbs in the Qal. This is frequently the case with verbal roots that are common in the Piel stem but are not attested (or rarely attested) in the Qal. The following examples illustrate this usage.

| Qal | | Piel (Simple Action) |
|---|---|---|
| צָוָה — not attested | → | צִוָּה — to command |
| בָּרַךְ — to bless | → | בֵּרֵךְ — to bless |

## 30.3 Piel Perfect

In the Piel Perfect, the diagnostic features are a Hireq under the first root consonant and a Daghesh Forte in the second root consonant.

| PGN | Qal | Piel | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3ms | קָטַל | קִטֵּל | he slaughtered |
| 3fs | קָֽטְלָה | קִטְּלָה | she slaughtered |
| 2ms | קָטַ֫לְתָּ | קִטַּ֫לְתָּ | you slaughtered |
| 2fs | קָטַלְתְּ | קִטַּלְתְּ | you slaughtered |
| 1cs | קָטַ֫לְתִּי | קִטַּ֫לְתִּי | I slaughtered |
| 3cp | קָֽטְלוּ | קִטְּלוּ | they slaughtered |
| 2mp | קְטַלְתֶּם | קִטַּלְתֶּם | you slaughtered |
| 2fp | קְטַלְתֶּן | קִטַּלְתֶּן | you slaughtered |
| 1cp | קָטַ֫לְנוּ | קִטַּ֫לְנוּ | we slaughtered |

---

## 30.4 Piel Perfect Diagnostics

1. Hireq under the first consonant of the verbal root (קִטֵּל).
2. Daghesh Forte in the second consonant of the verbal root (קִטֵּל).
3. The stem vowel is variable between Tsere (קִטֵּל), Seghol (דּבֶּר), and Pathach (שִׁלַּח), except in the 3fs and 3cp, where the expected Vocal Shewa is present. Note that the Tsere stem vowel occurs only in the 3ms.

**Piel Perfect Strong Verb:** קִטֵּל

---

## 30.5 Piel Imperfect

In the Piel Imperfect, the distinguishing features include: Vocal Shewa under the preformative (Hateph Pathach in the 1cs), Pathach under the first root consonant, and Daghesh Forte in the second root consonant.

| PGN | Qal | Piel | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3ms | יִקְטֹל | יְקַטֵּל | he will slaughter |
| 3fs | תִּקְטֹל | תְּקַטֵּל | she will slaughter |
| 2ms | תִּקְטֹל | תְּקַטֵּל | you will slaughter |
| 2fs | תִּקְטְלִי | תְּקַטְּלִי | you will slaughter |
| 1cs | אֶקְטֹל | אֲקַטֵּל | I will slaughter |
| 3mp | יִקְטְלוּ | יְקַטְּלוּ | they will slaughter |
| 3fp | תִּקְטֹ֫לְנָה | תְּקַטֵּ֫לְנָה | they will slaughter |
| 2mp | תִּקְטְלוּ | תְּקַטְּלוּ | you will slaughter |
| 2fp | תִּקְטֹ֫לְנָה | תְּקַטֵּ֫לְנָה | you will slaughter |
| 1cp | נִקְטֹל | נְקַטֵּל | we will slaughter |

---

## 30.6 Piel Imperfect Diagnostics

1. Vocal Shewa under the Imperfect preformatives (יְקַטֵּל), but Hateph Pathach in the 1cs (אֲקַטֵּל) since gutturals cannot take Vocal Shewa.
2. Pathach under the first consonant of the verbal root (יְקַטֵּל). This is the most important diagnostic feature since it occurs in every Piel conjugation except the Perfect. Note that the stem vowels are not considered to be diagnostic, though it is helpful to observe the presence of Tsere throughout the conjugations of the Piel (see summary chart in 30.13).
3. Daghesh Forte in the second consonant of the verbal root (יְקַטֵּל).

**Piel Imperfect Strong Verb:** יְקַטֵּל

---

## 30.7 Piel Imperative, Infinitive Construct, and Infinitive Absolute Diagnostics

These forms may be studied together because their diagnostic points of spelling are the same.

1. Pathach under the first consonant of the verbal root (קַטֵּל).
2. Daghesh Forte in the second consonant of the verbal root (קַטֵּל).

**Piel Imperative / Infinitive Construct / Infinitive Absolute Strong Verb:** קַטֵּל

---

## 30.8 Piel Imperative

It is instructive to note the similarity between the Imperative forms and the corresponding forms of the Imperfect. As in the Qal, all four forms of the Piel Imperative are derivable from their corresponding Imperfect forms by simply removing the Imperfect preformatives.

| PGN | Piel Imperfect | Piel Imperative | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2ms | תְּקַטֵּל | קַטֵּל | (you) slaughter! |
| 2fs | תְּקַטְּלִי | קַטְּלִי | (you) slaughter! |
| 2mp | תְּקַטְּלוּ | קַטְּלוּ | (you) slaughter! |
| 2fp | תְּקַטֵּ֫לְנָה | קַטֵּ֫לְנָה | (you) slaughter! |

---

## 30.9 Piel Infinitive Construct

The basic form of the Piel Infinitive Construct is identical to the 2ms form of the Piel Imperative. The following chart compares the Qal and Piel Infinitive Construct forms.

| Qal | Piel | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| קְטֹל | קַטֵּל | to slaughter, slaughtering |

---

## 30.10 Piel Infinitive Absolute

The Piel Infinitive Absolute of קָטַל is קַטֵּל. The Qal and Niphal forms of the Infinitive Absolute normally exhibit an o-class stem vowel, Holem or Holem Waw. In the Piel, Hiphil, and Hithpael stems, however, an e-class stem vowel is present, usually Tsere (but sometimes Tsere Yod or Seghol).

---

## 30.11 Piel Participle

The Piel Participle is formed with a מ prefix and has diagnostics similar to those of the Piel Imperfect. Remember that Participles are verbal adjectives and inflect for gender and number but not person.

| Gender/Number | Qal | Piel | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| ms | קֹטֵל | מְקַטֵּל | slaughtering |
| fs | קֹטֶ֫לֶת | מְקַטֶּ֫לֶת | slaughtering |
| mp | קֹטְלִים | מְקַטְּלִים | slaughtering |
| fp | קֹטְלוֹת | מְקַטְּלוֹת | slaughtering |

---

## 30.12 Piel Participle Diagnostics

1. מְ prefix. In the Piel and Pual Participles, this prefix appears with Vocal Shewa (מְקַטֵּל). It is important to remember that the מ prefix appears with the Participles of the Hiphil, Hophal, Piel, and Pual stems.
2. Pathach under the first consonant of the verbal root (מְקַטֵּל).
3. Daghesh Forte in the second consonant of the verbal root (מְקַטֵּל).

**Piel Participle Strong Verb:** מְקַטֵּל

---

## 30.13 Summary

1. The most important diagnostic features for the strong verb of the Piel stem include: (1) Vocal Shewa under all Imperfect preformatives and Participle prefixes; (2) Pathach under the first root consonant of every conjugation except the Perfect; and (3) Daghesh Forte in the second root consonant of every form in every conjugation. Though not considered to be diagnostic, it is helpful to note the Tsere stem vowel throughout the conjugations.
2. Looking ahead, note that the Daghesh Forte in the second consonant of the verbal root will also characterize the strong verb inflections of the Pual and Hithpael stems.
3. Note the distinctive מ prefix in the forms of the Piel Participle. This same prefix also occurs with the Participles of the Hiphil, Hophal, and Pual stems.

## 30.15 Additional Information: Loss of Daghesh Forte

There are a number of circumstances in which an expected Daghesh Forte in a Piel verb is lost. This loss of the Daghesh Forte is governed by the rule of sqnmlwy (5.5). This mnemonic device identifies all "s" type consonants in Hebrew (ס, צ, שׂ, and שׁ), plus the corresponding letters q (ק), n (נ), m (מ), l (ל), w (ו), and y (י). When one of these letters appears with a Daghesh Forte and a Vocal Shewa, the Daghesh Forte will frequently drop out. This phenomenon is especially common in the Piel stem, as the following examples illustrate.

| Form | Parsing | Why Daghesh Forte Is Lost |
|---|---|---|
| הַמְדַבֵּר | Piel Participle ms with definite article | Expected Daghesh Forte of the definite article is lost because the syllable begins with מְ |
| וַיְדַבֵּר | Piel Imperfect 3ms with Waw Consecutive | Expected Daghesh Forte of the Waw Consecutive is lost because the syllable begins with יְ |
| מִלְאוּ | Piel Perfect 3cp | Expected Daghesh Forte in the second root consonant is lost because ל occurs with Vocal Shewa (לְ) |
| וַיְקַנְאוּ | Piel Imperfect 3mp with Waw Consecutive | Daghesh of Waw Consecutive lost (syllable begins with יְ); additionally, Daghesh Forte in second root consonant absent because נ may give up Daghesh Forte when occurring with Vocal Shewa (נְ) |

## 30.16 Something You Should Know: The Imperative Summons to Praise

Of the many names and descriptive titles by which God is called in the Hebrew Bible, only one is his personal name. He is called יהוה (commonly translated as "LORD" or rendered as "Yahweh"). The divine name may also appear as a shortened independent form, as in Exod 15:2: עָזִּי וְזִמְרָת יָהּ (Yahweh is my strength and song). The form יָהּ is the shortened form of the tetragrammaton. The name is also attested in shortened forms that are commonly used in the formation of personal names such as יְהוֹנָתָן (Jonathan, "Yahweh has given"), יְהוֹשָׁפָט (Jehoshaphat, "Yahweh has judged"), יוֹאֵל (Joel, "Yahweh is God") or יְהוֹרָם (Jehoram, "Yahweh is exalted").

The shortened independent form occurs primarily in poetry and is most common in the spelling of "Hallelujah," as in Ps 135:3, כִּי־טוֹב יְהוָה הַלְלוּ־יָהּ (Praise Yahweh for Yahweh is good!). The form הַלְלוּ is a Piel Imperative 2mp verb from הלל which means "to praise." As an Imperative form, it translates "you praise" or simply "praise." You recognize the shortened form of the divine name (יָהּ) attached to the Imperative with Maqqef. You now understand the etymology of "Hallelujah." It is the Imperative summons to praise. The call to praise may also appear without a Maqqef, as in the first verse of Ps 106, הַלְלוּיָהּ (Praise Yahweh!). This form is the same as that in Ps 135:3.

As you might expect, the most frequent use of the verbal root הלל in expression of praise to God is in the book of Psalms, especially in the so-called Hallelujah Psalms that are found in several groups (Pss 104–106; 111–113; 115–117; 135; 146–150). Here, the Imperative summons to praise may appear as an integral part of the psalm (135:3), or it may serve as a liturgical introduction and/or conclusion with either of the spellings detailed above. It is significant to note that most of these occurrences are plural, emphasizing (though not uniquely) the congregational and communal dimension of praise. Praise can manifest itself in speaking (Jer 31:7), singing (Ps 69:30), and with dancing and musical instruments (Ps 149:3). However offered, corporately or individually, praise is a vital response to God. The book of Psalms concludes:

> Let everything that breathes praise Yahweh.
> הַלְלוּ־יָהּ (Ps 150:6)

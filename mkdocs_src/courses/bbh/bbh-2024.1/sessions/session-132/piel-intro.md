## Statistics

The Piel is the **D-stem** of Biblical Hebrew — diagnostically marked by a dagesh forte in R2 — and is the most common derived stem in the language. With approximately **5,474 tokens** across the Hebrew OT it appears roughly **18 times per 1,000 Hebrew words**, more than ten times the frequency of the Hophal.

### Top Piel Roots

Ten roots account for a disproportionate share of all Piel occurrences. The single verb דִּבֶּר alone represents roughly **19%** of all Piel tokens — because speaking in Biblical Hebrew defaults to the Piel form (the Qal of דבר is rare and largely poetic).

### Key Observations

1. **The root דבר (speak) dominates.** With ~1,035 tokens — roughly 1 in 5 Piel occurrences — דִּבֶּר is the default verb for speaking in Biblical Hebrew. When a narrative says "he spoke," it is almost always a Piel. The Qal of דבר is rare and largely confined to poetry.

2. **Psalms leads in raw count (677) but not in density (34.85/1k, 3rd overall).** The Psalter's heavy Piel use reflects the praise vocabulary — הלל, ספר, שבח, שיר all default to Piel. The book most used for prayer and worship relies heavily on the stem students are learning today.

3. **Nahum tops the density chart (46.68/1k) — nearly 3× the OT average.** A short book of only 557 words, Nahum's vivid judgment rhetoric (scatter, devour, destroy, cover, cut off) drives intense Piel concentration. The same is true of Lamentations (37.28/1k) and Malachi (35.71/1k), both covenant-confrontation texts.

4. **Leviticus is the most Piel-dense Torah book (27.25/1k).** Its ritual and purity legislation generates repeated Piel verbs: command (צוה), consecrate (קדש), atone (כפר), fill (מלא). Deuteronomy (17.56/1k) covers much of the same law in direct address, reducing the Piel density significantly.

5. **1 Chronicles is the lightest-density book of significant length (8.56/1k).** Nine chapters of genealogy (chapters 1–9) with virtually no verbal action drag the denominator up while the Piel count stays low — a reminder that word-count denominators include all parts of speech, not just verbs.

6. **The Perfect is the most common Piel conjugation (25%).** Together, Perfect + Imperfect + Weqatal account for 57% of all Piel tokens. A student who can recognize the Piel Perfect, Imperfect, and Weqatal can read the great majority of Piel occurrences encountered in OT text.

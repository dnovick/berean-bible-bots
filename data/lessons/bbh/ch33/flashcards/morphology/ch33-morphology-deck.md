# Chapter 33 — Pual Weak Morphology Deck

> **Scope note:** The Pual is studied in only three conjugations — Perfect, Imperfect, and Participle. The Pual has no Imperative (passive stems cannot take imperatival forms). This deck covers diagnostic-highlights coverage across four weak classes. For full paradigm practice, use the paradigm drill exercise.

28 cards covering 4 weak classes in Pual conjugations.

**Roots / Classes:**
- **מצא** (III-א) — to be found
- **גלה** (III-ה) — to be uncovered, disclosed
- **נחם** (II-Guttural, virtual doubling) — to be comforted, consoled
- **ברך** (II-Guttural, compensatory lengthening) — to be blessed

---

## Key Diagnostic Summary

| Card Front | Expected Back |
|---|---|
| Which Pual weak classes deviate significantly from the strong verb? | Only II-Guttural — the guttural in R2 rejects Dagesh Forte; III-א and III-ה preserve all Pual diagnostics |
| Pual II-Guttural virtual doubling: what changes? | No visible Dagesh in R2 (guttural); Patach (or Hateph Patach) under R2; Qibbuts under R1 is preserved |
| Pual II-Guttural compensatory lengthening (ר): what changes? | Qibbuts under R1 lengthens to Holem; no Dagesh in ר |
| How to distinguish Pual Participle from Hophal Participle? | Pual: מְ + Qibbuts under R1 + Dagesh in R2; Hophal: מוּ or מֻ prefix, NO Dagesh in R2 |
| Pual III-ה Perfect 3ms vs. Piel III-ה Perfect 3ms? | Pual: גֻּלָּה (Qibbuts under R1); Piel: גִּלָּה (Hireq under R1) |

Chapter 29 — "Spot the Hophal Weak" Passage Exercise

Stem: Hophal (הֻפְעַל) — Weak Verbs  |  Passages: Gen 39 + 43 (Joseph) · Exo 40 + Num 9 (Tabernacle) · Gen 38 + 43 + Exo 5 + 14 (Scattered) · Gen 4 + 22 + Exile context (Weak class coverage)

Instructions: For each numbered verb, fill in: (1) Is it Hophal? (Yes/No) (2) Conjugation (3) PGN (4) Root. Four verbs in this exercise are not Hophal — drawn from Qal and Hiphil already studied. Click ▶ Answer to reveal the key for that row.

Key diagnostic: Hophal prefix vowel is always u-class: הוּ- or הֻ- (Perfect), יוּ-/יֻ- (Imperfect), מוּ- (Participle). For I-guttural roots, the Perfect prefix is הֶ- (Seghol) instead of הֻ-. Compare: Hiphil prefix = i-class (הִ-/הֵ-/הָ-). If you see וּ, ֻ, or an unexpected הֶ- under the prefix consonant, suspect Hophal.

Passage D — Weak-Class Coverage: III-ה · I-גuttural · I-נ Wayyiqtol · I-נ/Geminate

Reflection Questions

  1. Verbs 6 (הוּקַם) and 9 (יוּקַם) and verb 17 (יֻקָּם) all show a consonantal sequence that includes ק and מ. Why are #6 and #9 from root קום while #17 is from root נקם? What is the morphological difference in the prefix that tells you which root is in view?
  2. Verb 3 (הַשָּׁב) and verb 10 (הוּשַׁב) both come from root שׁוב. One is Hophal and one is Qal. Explain what feature of each form tells you the stem, and why the dagesh in שׁ of הַשָּׁב does not indicate Hophal.
  3. Verb 14 (הֻגַּד) and verb 16 (וַיֻּגַּד) come from the same root (נגד) and both are Hophal. What is the conjugation difference between them, and what does the וַ prefix on verb 16 signal about narrative function?
  4. Verb 18 (הֶעֱמַד) has Seghol under the prefix (הֶ-) rather than Qibbuts (הֻ-). Why does the I-guttural class produce this prefix change in the Perfect, and what happens in the Imperfect (יֻעֲמַד)?

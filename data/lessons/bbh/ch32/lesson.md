# BBH Chapter 32 — Pual Strong Verbs (פֻּעַל)

*Basics of Biblical Hebrew, Pratico & Van Pelt, Chapter 32*

---

## 1. Function (§32.2)

The Pual (פֻּעַל) is the **passive of the Piel** stem. Because the Piel may be intensive, factitive, denominative, or iterative, the Pual expresses each of these with passive voice.

| Function | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Passive intensive | Undergoes the intensive action of the Piel | שֻׁבַּר — "he was shattered" ← Piel שִׁבֵּר "he shattered" |
| Passive factitive | Caused to become a state by another's action | יֻלַּד — "he was born/begotten" ← Piel יִלֵּד "she gave birth to" |
| Passive denominative | Derived from a noun, now passivized | |
| Passive iterative | Repeated action now passivized | |

> **Key diagnostic:** The Pual is identified by two co-occurring markers: **(1) Qibbuts (ֻ) under R1** and **(2) Daghesh Forte in R2**. The Piel's characteristic Daghesh Forte in R2 is retained; the vowel under R1 shifts from i-class (Piel: Hireq or Patach) to u-class (Pual: Qibbuts).

> **Note — when R2 = ר:** The letter ר rejects Daghesh Forte. When R2 is ר, the missing dagesh triggers compensatory lengthening: Qibbuts under R1 lengthens to **Holem (ֹ)**. For example: מְבֹרָךְ ("blessed," Pual Participle of ברך) shows Holem under מְבֹ rather than the expected Qibbuts, and ר has no dagesh.

---

## 2. Scope (§32.1)

BBH studies only three Pual conjugations — Perfect, Imperfect, and Participle — because other forms are infrequent in the OT.

---

## 3. Pual Perfect (§32.3–32.4)

The Pual Perfect is listed below with the Piel Perfect for comparison. The vowel under R1 (Qibbuts vs. Hireq) is the decisive difference.

| PGN | Piel | Pual |
|---|---|---|
| 3ms | קִטֵּל | קֻטַּל |
| 3fs | קִטְּלָה | קֻטְּלָה |
| 2ms | קִטַּ֫לְתָּ | קֻטַּ֫לְתָּ |
| 2fs | קִטַּלְתְּ | קֻטַּלְתְּ |
| 1cs | קִטַּ֫לְתִּי | קֻטַּ֫לְתִּי |
| 3cp | קִטְּלוּ | קֻטְּלוּ |
| 2mp | קִטַּלְתֶּם | קֻטַּלְתֶּם |
| 2fp | קִטַּלְתֶּן | קֻטַּלְתֶּן |
| 1cp | קִטַּ֫לְנוּ | קֻטַּ֫לְנוּ |

**Diagnostics:**
1. **Qibbuts under R1** in every form — this is the key distinguisher from the Piel (which has Hireq under R1).
2. **Daghesh Forte in R2** throughout, as in the Piel.
3. Stem vowel is Patach (except 3fs and 3cp where it reduces before the suffix).

> **Strong verb summary form:** קֻטַּל

> **Strong verb summary form:** יְקֻטַּל

## 7. Pual vs. Hophal — Distinguishing Two Passive Stems

Both the Pual and Hophal are passive stems with Participles beginning with מ and a u-class vowel. The differences are systematic:

| Feature | Pual | Hophal |
|---|---|---|
| Passive of | **Piel** | **Hiphil** |
| Universal marker | **Qibbuts under R1** + Daghesh in R2 | **u-class vowel under the prefix consonant** |
| Perfect | No prefix + Qibbuts on R1 | **הוּ** or **הֻ** prefix |
| Imperfect | **יְ** (shewa) prefix + Qibbuts under R1 | **יוּ** or **יֻ** prefix |
| Participle | **מְ** + Qibbuts under R1 + Daghesh in R2 | **מוּ** or **מֻ** — no daghesh in R2 |
| Daghesh in R2? | **Yes** | No (unless root assimilation) |
| Example Perfect | קֻטַּל | הוּמַת |
| Example Imperfect | יְקֻטַּל | יוּמַת |
| Example Participle | מְקֻטָּל | מוּמָת |

> **Quick test:** The Daghesh Forte in R2 is the decisive separator — it is present in the Pual (inherited from the Piel) and absent in the Hophal.

## 8. Conjunctive Daghesh (§32.11)

A **Conjunctive Daghesh** is a Daghesh Forte placed in the initial consonant of a word to connect it to the preceding word and smooth pronunciation. It typically occurs when the preceding word ends in an unaccented ֶה or ָה and the following word begins with an accented syllable. Examples:

- וְזֶה־שְּׁמוֹ — "and this is his name" (the Daghesh in שׁ is conjunctive)
- לְכָה־נָּא — "come now" (the Daghesh in נ is conjunctive)

# BBH Chapter 28 — Hophal Strong Verbs

*Source: Pratico & Van Pelt, Basics of Biblical Hebrew Grammar (BBH), Chapter 28*

---

## 28.1 Introduction (§28.1)

The Hophal stem occurs less frequently than any other derived stem. Because certain forms occur with such infrequency, only the **Perfect, Imperfect, and Participle** are studied here.

The stem name "Hophal" (הָפְעַל) preserves certain diagnostic features: the הָ prefix with Qamets Hatuf and the Pathach stem vowel. This prefix vowel and stem vowel combination distinguishes the Hophal from most Hiphil forms. In modern Hebrew, the stem is called "Huphal," reflecting the frequent u-class vowel.

---

## 28.2 The Meaning of the Hophal Stem (§28.2)

The Hophal is the **passive of the Hiphil**. It expresses causative action with a passive voice.

| Hiphil | Translation | Hophal | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| הִמְלִיךְ | he made (someone) king | הָמְלַךְ | he was made king |

The Hophal may also be factitive or denominative.

---

## 28.3 Hophal Perfect (§28.3)

The Hophal Perfect may occur with either a **Qibbuts** (u-class) or **Qamets Hatuf** (o-class) prefix vowel; Qamets Hatuf is more frequent in the strong verb. Two paradigms are given, each with a different prefix vowel. Translate passively with a form of "to be."

| | **Hophal u-class** | **Hophal o-class** | **Translation** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **3ms** | הֻקְטַל | הָקְטַל | he was made to kill |
| **3fs** | הֻקְטְלָה | הָקְטְלָה | she was made to kill |
| **2ms** | הֻקְטַ֫לְתָּ | הָקְטַ֫לְתָּ | you were made to kill |
| **2fs** | הֻקְטַלְתְּ | הָקְטַלְתְּ | you were made to kill |
| **1cs** | הֻקְטַ֫לְתִּי | הָקְטַ֫לְתִּי | I was made to kill |
| **3cp** | הֻקְטְלוּ | הָקְטְלוּ | they were made to kill |
| **2mp** | הֻקְטַלְתֶּם | הָקְטַלְתֶּם | you were made to kill |
| **2fp** | הֻקְטַלְתֶּן | הָקְטַלְתֶּן | you were made to kill |
| **1cp** | הֻקְטַ֫לְנוּ | הָקְטַ֫לְנוּ | we were made to kill |

---

## 28.4 Hophal Perfect Diagnostics (§28.4)

1. **הֻ** (u-class) or **הָ** (o-class) prefix in every form. The Shewa after the short prefix vowel is silent in every form of both paradigms.
2. **Pathach stem vowel** in both classes, except in the 3fs and 3cp forms.

**Diagnostic forms:** הֻקְטַל / הָקְטַל

---

## 28.5 Hophal Imperfect (§28.5)

The Hophal Imperfect is formed with Imperfect preformatives, the verbal root with Pathach stem vowel, and Imperfect sufformatives. The preformative vowel is variable between Qibbuts and Qamets Hatuf. Translate passively with a form of "to be."

| | **Hophal u-class** | **Hophal o-class** | **Translation** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **3ms** | יֻקְטַל | יָקְטַל | he will be made to kill |
| **3fs** | תֻּקְטַל | תָּקְטַל | she will be made to kill |
| **2ms** | תֻּקְטַל | תָּקְטַל | you will be made to kill |
| **2fs** | תֻּקְטְלִי | תָּקְטְלִי | you will be made to kill |
| **1cs** | אֻקְטַל | אָקְטַל | I will be made to kill |
| **3mp** | יֻקְטְלוּ | יָקְטְלוּ | they will be made to kill |
| **3fp** | תֻּקְטַ֫לְנָה | תָּקְטַ֫לְנָה | they will be made to kill |
| **2mp** | תֻּקְטְלוּ | תָּקְטְלוּ | you will be made to kill |
| **2fp** | תֻּקְטַ֫לְנָה | תָּקְטַ֫לְנָה | you will be made to kill |
| **1cp** | נֻקְטַל | נָקְטַל | we will be made to kill |

---

## 28.6 Hophal Imperfect Diagnostics (§28.6)

1. **Qibbuts** (יֻקְטַל) or **Qamets Hatuf** (יָקְטַל) preformative vowel in every form of their respective paradigms.
2. **Pathach stem vowel** in both classes, except in those forms with sufformatives that consist of a vowel (2fs, 3mp, 2mp).

**Diagnostic forms:** יֻקְטַל / יָקְטַל

---

## 28.7 Hophal Participle (§28.7)

The Hophal Participle is formed with the Participle prefix מ, the verbal root with Qamets stem vowel (except in the fs), and the inflectional endings of the Participle. Both u-class and o-class prefix vowels are attested. Translate passively.

| | **Hophal u-class** | **Hophal o-class** | **Translation** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ms** | מֻקְטָל | מָקְטָל | being made to kill |
| **fs** | מֻקְטֶ֫לֶת | מָקְטֶ֫לֶת | being made to kill |
| **mp** | מֻקְטָלִים | מָקְטָלִים | being made to kill |
| **fp** | מֻקְטָלוֹת | מָקְטָלוֹת | being made to kill |

---

## 28.8 Hophal Participle Diagnostics (§28.8)

1. **מֻ or מָ prefix.** Note that the Hiphil, Hophal, Piel, and Pual Participles all have a מ prefix. The Hiphil strong verb and most weak verbs have Pathach under the prefix (מַ).
2. **Qamets stem vowel** in every form except the feminine singular (מֻקְטֶ֫לֶת).

**Diagnostic forms:** מֻקְטָל / מָקְטָל

---

## 28.9 Summary (§28.9)

1. The Hophal stem expresses causative action with a **passive voice**. In most occurrences, it is the passive of the Hiphil.
2. The Hophal conjugations are distinguished by their prefix or preformative vowels — **Qibbuts or Qamets Hatuf**. Think of the Hophal prefix vowel in terms of vowel class: **u-class** (Qibbuts most frequent; Shureq also appears in many weak verbs) and **o-class** (Qamets Hatuf most frequent; Holem and Holem Waw also appear).
3. In the Perfect and Imperfect, the **stem vowel is Pathach**, except in forms with sufformatives that begin with or consist of a vowel. In the Participle, the **stem vowel is Qamets**, except in the feminine singular.

## 28.11 Additional Information: Verbal Hendiadys (§28.11)

Hebrew can express a single concept by using two independent words connected by the conjunction וְ (and). This coordination is called **hendiadys**. It is especially common with verbs.

In each example below, the verbs are not translated independently but are coordinated with an adverb (quickly, more, again). The second verb expresses the main idea; the first verb is translated adverbially. Verbs commonly used in this construction include:

| Verb | Stem | Adverbial Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| מָהַר | Piel | quickly, soon |
| יָסַף | Hiphil | again, increasingly |
| שׁוּב | Qal | again |
| שָׁכַם | Hiphil | early |
| רָבָה | Hiphil | many times |

**Examples:**

> אֶת־אָבִי הֵנָּה וּמִהַרְתֶּם וְהוֹרַדְתֶּם
> And you will quickly (soon) bring my father down here (Gen 45:13).

> אֹתוֹ שְׂנֹא עוֹד וַיּוֹסִפוּ
> And they hated him even more (Gen 37:5).

> אַנְשֵׁי־הָעִיר וַיְמַהֲרוּ וַיַּשְׁכִּימוּ וַיֵּצְאוּ לִקְרַאת־יִשְׂרָאֵל לַמִּלְחָמָה
> The men of the city went out quickly, early in the morning, to meet Israel in battle (Josh 8:14).

## 28.12 Something You Should Memorize: Deuteronomy 10:17 (§28.12)

> One of the great confessions of praise for ancient Israel's God is found in Deut 10:17. In the immediate context, the people are reminded that they are a "treasured possession," chosen by God because "Yahweh loves you and is keeping the promises that he swore to your fathers" (Deut 7:6–9; 10:12–15).
>
> יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵיכֶם הוּא אֱלֹהֵי הָאֱלֹהִים וַאֲדֹנֵי הָאֲדֹנִים הָאֵל הַגָּדֹל הַגִּבֹּר וְהַנּוֹרָא
> Yahweh your God is God of gods and Lord of lords, the great, the mighty, and the awesome God.
>
> Notice, not one verb — just the superlative proclamation of the one who is the God of the Bible. The 3ms independent personal pronoun (הוּא) is emphatic and serves to identify the verbless clause (§23.8.3).
>
> The predicate contains two construct chains, each with plural construct and plural absolute nouns: אֱלֹהֵי הָאֱלֹהִים (God of gods) and אֲדֹנֵי הָאֲדֹנִים (Lord of lords) — both expressing superlatives (§10.6.2). The declaration concludes with the definite article prefixed to אֵל, followed by two adjectives, גָּדֹל and גִּבֹּר, and the Niphal Participle ms of ירא.
>
> An attentive reader of Deut 7:21 and 10:17 cannot help but be drawn to Exod 3:13–15; 6:2–8 and 34:5–7, each of which rolls like thunder across the landscape of the Hebrew Bible. The great "I AM" extolled in Deut 10:17 is none other than the subject of the "I AM" sayings in John's Gospel (6:35, 48; 8:12; 10:7, 11, 14; 11:25; 14:6; 15:1, 5):
>
> I AM the bread of life. (John 6:35)
>
> I AM the good shepherd. (John 10:14)
>
> I AM the way, and the truth, and the life. (John 14:6)

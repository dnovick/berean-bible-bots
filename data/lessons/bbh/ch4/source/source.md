# BBH Chapter 4 — Hebrew Nouns

*Source: Pratico & Van Pelt, Basics of Biblical Hebrew Grammar (BBH), Chapter 4*

---

## 4.1 Introduction

Hebrew nouns function just like English nouns — they indicate a person, place, thing, or idea. Hebrew nouns can be singular, plural, or **dual** (indicating exactly two). Hebrew nouns also have **gender**: masculine or feminine.

Hebrew nouns pluralize by adding endings to the singular stem. The plural ending for a masculine noun differs from that of a feminine noun.

---

## 4.2 Plural and Dual Endings on Masculine and Feminine Nouns (§4.2)

| | Masculine | | Feminine | |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Singular** | סוּס | horse | תּוֹרָה | law |
| **Plural** | סוּסִים | horses | תּוֹרוֹת | laws |
| **Dual** | סוּסַ֫יִם | two horses | תּוֹרָתַ֫יִם | two laws |

## 4.3 Gender and Number (§4.3)

**Masculine endings:**

| Number | Ending | Example | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Singular | endingless | סוּס | horse |
| Plural | ִים | סוּסִים | horses |
| Dual | ַ֫יִם | סוּסַ֫יִם | two horses |

**Feminine singular endings:**

| Ending | Example | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| ָה (most common) | תּוֹרָה | law |
| ַת | בַּת | daughter |
| ֶת | תִּפְאֶ֫רֶת | glory |
| ִית | בְּרִית | covenant |
| וּת | מַלְכּוּת | kingdom |

**Feminine plural endings:**

| Number | Ending | Example | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plural | וֹת | מְלָכוֹת | queens |
| Dual | ַ֫יִם | יָדַ֫יִם | two hands |
| Dual | ָתַ֫יִם | תּוֹרָתַ֫יִם | two laws |

> Dual forms are most common with paired body parts (hands, feet, eyes, ears).

## 4.6 Lexical Form (§4.6)

The **lexical form** of any noun is its **singular form**. Look up סוּסִים or סוּסַ֫יִם under סוּס; look up תּוֹרוֹת under תּוֹרָה.

---

## 4.7 Exceptions to the Rules (§4.7)

1. **Endingless Feminine Nouns.** Some feminine singular nouns are endingless (e.g., אֶ֫רֶץ land, עִיר city, אֶ֫בֶן stone). Gender must be confirmed in the lexicon.
2. **Exception to Normal Pluralization.** A few nouns take plural endings of the opposite gender (e.g., אָב father → אָבוֹת; שָׁנָה year → שָׁנִים).
3. **Special Dual Nouns.** Three nouns are always dual in form: שָׁמַ֫יִם (heaven), מִצְרַ֫יִם (Egypt), מַ֫יִם (water).
4. **Irregular Stem Change.** Some nouns change their consonantal stem in the plural: אִישׁ → אֲנָשִׁים; אִשָּׁה → נָשִׁים.
5. **Defective Spelling of וֹת.** The feminine plural ending וֹת is occasionally spelled with Holem (not Holem Waw), e.g., עֵדֹת for עֵדוֹת.

---

## 4.8 Basic Patterns of Noun Pluralization (§4.8)

### Pattern 1 — No Change

Monosyllabic nouns with an unchangeable long vowel, and two-syllable nouns with Vocal Shewa in the first syllable and an unchangeable long vowel in the second syllable, pluralize without vowel change.

| Singular | Translation | Plural | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| שִׁיר | song | שִׁירִים | songs |
| אוֹת | sign | אוֹתוֹת | signs |
| רְחוֹב | street | רְחוֹבוֹת | streets |
| חֲלוֹם | dream | חֲלוֹמוֹת | dreams |

### Pattern 2 — Propretonic Reduction

Two-syllable nouns accented on the final syllable with a changeable long vowel (Qamets or Tsere) in the pretonic syllable undergo **propretonic reduction**: the changeable long vowel reduces to Shewa in the open propretonic position.

| Singular | Translation | Plural | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| דָּבָר | word | דְּבָרִים | words |
| לֵבָב | heart | לְבָבוֹת | hearts |

Initial guttural consonants prefer Hateph Pathach instead of Vocal Shewa:

| Singular | Translation | Plural | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| עָנָן | cloud | עֲנָנִים | clouds |
| חָצֵר | courtyard | חֲצֵרוֹת | courtyards |

Nouns with a vowel letter (unchangeable long vowel) in the first syllable do not undergo propretonic reduction:

| Singular | Translation | Plural | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| כּוֹכָב | star | כּוֹכָבִים | stars |

### Pattern 3 — Segholate Nouns

Two-syllable nouns accented on the **first** syllable are Segholate nouns (named for the Seghol vowel pattern). In the plural they all share the same pattern: Vocal Shewa (or Hateph vowel) under the first consonant and Qamets under the second.

| Singular | Translation | Plural | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| מֶ֫לֶךְ | king | מְלָכִים | kings |
| נֶ֫פֶשׁ | life | נְפָשׁוֹת | lives |
| חֶ֫רֶב | sword | חֲרָבוֹת | swords |
| סֵ֫פֶר | book | סְפָרִים | books |
| בֹּ֫קֶר | morning | בְּקָרִים | mornings |
| זֶ֫רַע | seed | זְרָעִים | seeds |
| נַ֫עַר | boy | נְעָרִים | boys |

### Pattern 4 — Geminate Nouns

Geminate nouns appear to have only two root consonants (the third was originally doubled). With the plural ending, the doubled consonant reappears as Daghesh Forte.

| Singular | Translation | Plural | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| עַם | people | עַמִּים | peoples (root עמם) |
| חֵץ | arrow | חִצִּים | arrows (root חצץ) |
| חֹק | statute | חֻקִּים | statutes (root חקק) |

### Pattern 5 — Masculine Nouns Ending in ֶה

Masculine nouns ending in ֶה (Seghol He) drop the ֶה before taking a plural ending. These nouns frequently take the feminine plural ending (וֹת).

| Singular | Translation | Plural | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| שָׂדֶה | field | שָׂדוֹת | fields |
| מַעֲשֶׂה | work | מַעֲשִׂים | works |
| מַחֲנֶה | camp | מַחֲנִים / מַחֲנוֹת | camps |

### Pattern 6 — Irregular Pluralization

| Singular | Translation | Plural | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| אִישׁ | man | אֲנָשִׁים | men |
| אִשָּׁה | woman | נָשִׁים | women |
| עִיר | city | עָרִים | cities |
| אָב | father | אָבוֹת | fathers |
| בַּ֫יִת | house | בָּֽתִּים | houses |
| בַּת | daughter | בָּנוֹת | daughters |
| בֵּן | son | בָּנִים | sons |
| יוֹם | day | יָמִים | days |

---

## 4.9 Summary (§4.9)

1. Hebrew nouns have **gender** (masculine or feminine) and **number** (singular, plural, dual).
2. Masculine singular nouns are endingless; mp = ִים; md = ַ֫יִם.
3. Most common feminine singular ending = ָה; other endings use ת. Feminine plural = וֹת; fd = ַ֫יִם or ָתַ֫יִם.
4. Exceptions: endingless feminines, cross-gender plurals, special duals, irregular stem changes, defective וֹת spelling.
5. Most nouns change vowel pattern when pluralized. Learn the six pluralization patterns.
6. The **lexical form** of a noun is its singular form.

## 4.11 Additional Information: Rules of Shewa (§4.11)

1. **First Rule of Shewa.** Hebrew does not permit two contiguous Vocal Shewas at the beginning of a word. The first Vocal Shewa becomes Hireq; the second becomes Silent Shewa. Example: לְ + נְבִיאִים → לִנְבִיאִים (for prophets).
2. **Exception to First Rule.** When a word begins with יְ, the preposition contracts with the יְ to form Hireq Yod: לְ + יְהוּדָה → לִיהוּדָה.
3. **Second Rule of Shewa.** A Vocal Shewa cannot precede a guttural with a reduced (Hateph) vowel. The preposition takes the corresponding short vowel: בְּ + חֲלוֹם → בַּחֲלוֹם.
4. **Exception to Second Rule.** When לְ is prefixed to אֱלֹהִים, the quiescing (silencing) of the א produces Tsere: לֵאלֹהִים.

## 4.12 Something You Should Consider: The Work of William Tyndale (§4.12)

William Tyndale (d. 1536) translated the New Testament into English and began the Old Testament before being imprisoned in Vilvorde, Belgium. The following is a letter written to his jailer during his imprisonment:

> I believe, right worshipful, that you are not unaware of what may have been determined concerning me. Wherefor I beg your lordship, and that by the Lord Jesus, that if I am to remain here through the winter, you will request the commissary to have the kindness to send me, from the goods of mine which he has, a warmer cap; for I suffer greatly from cold in the head … But most of all I beg and beseech your clemency to be urgent with the commissary, that he will kindly permit me to have the **Hebrew Bible, Hebrew grammar, and Hebrew dictionary**, that I may pass the time in that study.
>
> —W. Tindalus

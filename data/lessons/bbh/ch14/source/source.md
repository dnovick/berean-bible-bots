# BBH Chapter 14 — Qal Perfect Weak Verbs

*Source: Pratico & Van Pelt, Basics of Biblical Hebrew Grammar (BBH), Chapter 14*

---

## 14.1 Introduction (§14.1)

Weak verbs have one or more weak root consonants that cause changes in vocalization or spelling. Identifying weak verb forms requires comparing them to the strong verb. Weak verb forms are presented in four categories:

1. Triconsonantal verbs that **retain** all three root letters
2. Triconsonantal verbs that **drop** a root letter
3. Triconsonantal verbs that are **doubly weak**
4. **Biconsonantal** verbs

Mastery of the Qal Perfect strong verb paradigm (ch. 13) is prerequisite.

---

## 14.2 I-Guttural, II-Guttural, and III-ע/ח Verbs (§14.2)

These three classes share a common type of change: gutturals cannot take a Vocal Shewa and prefer **Hateph Pathach** instead. The five total changes are minor.

| PGN | I-Guttural (עָמַד) | II-Guttural (בָּחַר) | III-ע/ח (שָׁמַע) | Strong (קָטַל) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3ms | עָמַד | בָּחַר | שָׁמַע | קָטַל |
| 3fs | עָֽמְדָה | בָּֽחֲרָה | שָֽׁמְעָה | קָֽטְלָה |
| 2ms | עָמַ֫דְתָּ | בָּחַ֫רְתָּ | שָׁמַ֫עְתָּ | קָטַ֫לְתָּ |
| 2fs | עָמַדְתְּ | בָּחַרְתְּ | שָׁמַ֫עַתְּ | קָטַלְתְּ |
| 1cs | עָמַ֫דְתִּי | בָּחַ֫רְתִּי | שָׁמַ֫עְתִּי | קָטַ֫לְתִּי |
| 3cp | עָֽמְדוּ | בָּֽחֲרוּ | שָֽׁמְעוּ | קָֽטְלוּ |
| 2mp | עֲמַדְתֶּם | בְּחַרְתֶּם | שְׁמַעְתֶּם | קְטַלְתֶּם |
| 2fp | עֲמַדְתֶּן | בְּחַרְתֶּן | שְׁמַעְתֶּן | קְטַלְתֶּן |
| 1cp | עָמַ֫דְנוּ | בָּחַ֫רְנוּ | שָׁמַ֫עְנוּ | קָטַ֫לְנוּ |

---

## 14.3 Notes on I-Guttural, II-Guttural, and III-ע/ח (§14.3)

1. In I-Guttural and II-Guttural paradigms, Vocal Shewas change to **Hateph Pathach** because of the guttural (עֲמַדְתֶּם, בָּֽחֲרָה, בָּֽחֲרוּ). Memorize the rule; no need to memorize the full paradigm.
2. In the III-ע/ח paradigm, the only change is in the **2fs** form: the Silent Shewa under the guttural changes to Pathach (שָׁמַ֫עַתְּ). Gutturals prefer a-class vowels.

---

## 14.4 III-א Verbs (§14.4)

These verbs have א as the third root consonant (e.g., מָצָא "to find"). The א is **quiescent** (silent) in almost every form, causing significant changes compared to the strong verb.

| PGN | III-א (מָצָא) | Strong (קָטַל) |
|---|---|---|
| 3ms | מָצָא | קָטַל |
| 3fs | מָֽצְאָה | קָֽטְלָה |
| 2ms | מָצָ֫אתָ | קָטַ֫לְתָּ |
| 2fs | מָצָאת | קָטַלְתְּ |
| 1cs | מָצָ֫אתִי | קָטַ֫לְתִּי |
| 3cp | מָֽצְאוּ | קָֽטְלוּ |
| 2mp | מְצָאתֶם | קְטַלְתֶּם |
| 2fp | מְצָאתֶן | קְטַלְתֶּן |
| 1cp | מָצָ֫אנוּ | קָטַ֫לְנוּ |

---

## 14.5 III-א Notes (§14.5)

1. The **3ms lexical form** (מָצָא) has a distinctive vowel pattern shared by all III-א and III-ה weak verbs: Qamets under both the first and second root consonants.
2. The **stem vowel** changes from Pathach to Qamets in every form except the 3fs and 3cp.
3. The quiescent א eliminates the need for a Silent Shewa in many inflected forms.
4. The **Daghesh Lene** is absent from the initial ת of sufformatives (תִּי → תִי) because the quiescent א is preceded by a vowel sound, leaving the ת in an open syllable.

---

## 14.6 III-ה Verbs (§14.6)

These verbs have ה as the third root consonant (e.g., בָּנָה "to build"). The final ה root consonant is **lost in every form**.

| PGN | III-ה (בָּנָה) | Strong (קָטַל) |
|---|---|---|
| 3ms | בָּנָה | קָטַל |
| 3fs | בָּֽנְתָה | קָֽטְלָה |
| 2ms | בָּנִ֫יתָ | קָטַ֫לְתָּ |
| 2fs | בָּנִית | קָטַלְתְּ |
| 1cs | בָּנִ֫יתִי | קָטַ֫לְתִּי |
| 3cp | בָּנוּ | קָֽטְלוּ |
| 2mp | בְּנִיתֶם | קְטַלְתֶּם |
| 2fp | בְּנִיתֶן | קְטַלְתֶּן |
| 1cp | בָּנִ֫ינוּ | קָטַ֫לְנוּ |

---

## 14.7 III-ה Notes (§14.7)

1. The 3ms form (בָּנָה) shares the same vowel pattern as III-א (Qamets under both root consonants). The ה in 3ms is a vowel letter, not the root consonant.
2. The **3fs** form (בָּֽנְתָה) inserts ת to distinguish it from the 3ms.
3. All **second and first person** forms (both singular and plural) have the **diagnostic Hireq Yod stem vowel** (e.g., בָּנִ֫יתָ). III-ה verbs were originally III-י; the Hireq Yod is a remnant of the original third root consonant.
4. The **Daghesh Lene** is absent from the ת of sufformatives because the ת is preceded by the Hireq Yod vowel.

---

## 14.8 Geminate Verbs (§14.8)

Geminate verbs have identical second and third root consonants (e.g., סָבַב "to go around," תָּמַם "to be finished"). Two distinctive features:

1. In most forms, the first Geminate consonant **assimilates** into the second as Daghesh Forte.
2. A **Holem Waw (וֹ) connecting vowel** is inserted between the verbal root and the sufformative in all second and first person forms.

| PGN | Geminate Type 1 (סָבַב) | Geminate Type 2 (תַּם) | Strong (קָטַל) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3ms | סָבַב | תַּם | קָטַל |
| 3fs | סָֽבְבָה | תַּ֫מָּה | קָֽטְלָה |
| 2ms | סַבּ֫וֹתָ | תַּמּ֫וֹתָ | קָטַ֫לְתָּ |
| 2fs | סַבּוֹת | תַּמּוֹת | קָטַלְתְּ |
| 1cs | סַבּ֫וֹתִי | תַּמּ֫וֹתִי | קָטַ֫לְתִּי |
| 3cp | סָֽבְבוּ | תַּ֫מּוּ | קָֽטְלוּ |
| 2mp | סַבּוֹתֶם | תַּמּוֹתֶם | קְטַלְתֶּם |
| 2fp | סַבּוֹתֶן | תַּמּוֹתֶן | קְטַלְתֶּן |
| 1cp | סַבּ֫וֹנוּ | תַּמּ֫וֹנוּ | קָטַ֫לְנוּ |

---

## 14.9 Geminate Notes (§14.9)

1. In all second and first person forms, the first Geminate consonant assimilates into the second with Daghesh Forte (בב → בּ).
2. The **Holem Waw (וֹ) connecting vowel** is a diagnostic feature of the Qal Perfect Geminate verb.
3. The Daghesh Lene in the initial ת of sufformatives is absent when preceded by the Holem Waw vowel.
4. In Type 2 (תָּמַם), the first Geminate consonant has assimilated in all forms and remains as Daghesh Forte, including in the 3fs and 3cp.

---

## 14.10 Doubly Weak Verbs (§14.10)

Doubly weak verbs have more than one weak consonant. Most are both III-ה and either I-Guttural or II-Guttural. Two high-frequency examples:

| PGN | III-ה/II-Gutt (רָאָה) | III-ה/I-Gutt (עָלָה) | Strong (קָטַל) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3ms | רָאָה | עָלָה | קָטַל |
| 3fs | רָאֲתָה | עָֽלְתָה | קָֽטְלָה |
| 2ms | רָאִ֫יתָ | עָלִ֫יתָ | קָטַ֫לְתָּ |
| 2fs | רָאִית | עָלִית | קָטַלְתְּ |
| 1cs | רָאִ֫יתִי | עָלִ֫יתִי | קָטַ֫לְתִּי |
| 3cp | רָאוּ | עָלוּ | קָֽטְלוּ |
| 2mp | רְאִיתֶם | עֲלִיתֶם | קְטַלְתֶּם |
| 2fp | רְאִיתֶן | עֲלִיתֶן | קְטַלְתֶּן |
| 1cp | רָאִ֫ינוּ | עָלִ֫ינוּ | קָטַ֫לְנוּ |

---

## 14.11 Doubly Weak Notes (§14.11)

1. All III-ה changes are present: loss of final ה in every form; ת in the 3fs; diagnostic Hireq Yod in all second and first person forms; loss of Daghesh Lene in ת-sufformatives.
2. Guttural changes are also present: in רָאֲתָה, the expected Vocal Shewa under א changes to Hateph Pathach; the 2mp (עֲלִיתֶם) and 2fp (עֲלִיתֶן) have Hateph Pathach under the initial guttural.

---

## 14.12 Biconsonantal Verbs (§14.12)

Biconsonantal (Hollow / II-ו/י) verbs have only two root consonants (e.g., קָם "to rise," שָׂם "to set," בָּא "to enter"). Their **lexical form** is the **Infinitive Construct**, not the Qal Perfect 3ms: קוּם, שִׂים, בּוֹא.

| PGN | Strong Bicon. (קָם) | Strong Bicon. (שָׂם) | Weak Bicon. (בָּא) | Strong (קָטַל) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3ms | קָם | שָׂם | בָּא | קָטַל |
| 3fs | קָ֫מָה | שָׂ֫מָה | בָּ֫אָה | קָֽטְלָה |
| 2ms | קַ֫מְתָּ | שַׂ֫מְתָּ | בָּ֫אתָ | קָטַ֫לְתָּ |
| 2fs | קַמְתְּ | שַׂמְתְּ | בָּאת | קָטַלְתְּ |
| 1cs | קַ֫מְתִּי | שַׂ֫מְתִּי | בָּ֫אתִי | קָטַ֫לְתִּי |
| 3cp | קָ֫מוּ | שָׂ֫מוּ | בָּ֫אוּ | קָֽטְלוּ |
| 2mp | קַמְתֶּם | שַׂמְתֶּם | בָּאתֶם | קְטַלְתֶּם |
| 2fp | קַמְתֶּן | שַׂמְתֶּן | בָּאתֶן | קְטַלְתֶּן |
| 1cp | קַ֫מְנוּ | שַׂ֫מְנוּ | בָּ֫אנוּ | קָטַ֫לְנוּ |

---

## 14.13 Biconsonantal Notes (§14.13)

1. In strong Biconsonantal paradigms (קוּם, שִׂים): third person forms have **Qamets** under the first root consonant; all other forms have **Pathach**.
2. In the weak Biconsonantal paradigm (בּוֹא): all forms have Qamets under the first root consonant.
3. In the weak Biconsonantal, the quiescent א causes loss of Daghesh Lene in ת-sufformatives (same as III-א).

---

## 14.14 Summary (§14.14)

1. Memorize the strong verb paradigm and Perfect sufformatives — sufformatives do not change despite root weakness.
2. Gutturals cannot take Vocal Shewa; they prefer Hateph Pathach (I-Guttural, II-Guttural, doubly weak verbs).
3. With III-א: the א is quiescent, causing loss of Daghesh Lene in ת-sufformatives.
4. With III-ה: the final ה root consonant is lost in all forms; all second and first person forms have the diagnostic Hireq Yod stem vowel; Daghesh Lene lost in ת-sufformatives.
5. With Geminate verbs: the first Geminate consonant assimilates into the second with Daghesh Forte in all second and first person forms; Holem Waw (וֹ) connecting vowel is a diagnostic feature.
6. Strong Biconsonantal verbs: third person forms have Qamets; second and first person forms have Pathach.

---

## 14.17 Additional Information: Qal Perfect of נָתַן and הָיָה (§14.17)

Both נָתַן (to give) and הָיָה (to be) are high-frequency verbs with multiple weaknesses.

| PGN | נָתַן (to give) | הָיָה (to be) | Strong (קָטַל) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3ms | נָתַן | הָיָה | קָטַל |
| 3fs | נָֽתְנָה | הָֽיְתָה | קָֽטְלָה |
| 2ms | נָתַ֫תָּ | הָיִ֫יתָ | קָטַ֫לְתָּ |
| 2fs | נָתַ֫תְּ | הָיִית | קָטַלְתְּ |
| 1cs | נָתַ֫תִּי | הָיִ֫יתִי | קָטַ֫לְתִּי |
| 3cp | נָֽתְנוּ | הָיוּ | קָֽטְלוּ |
| 2mp | נְתַתֶּם | הֱיִיתֶם | קְטַלְתֶּם |
| 2fp | נְתַתֶּן | הֱיִיתֶן | קְטַלְתֶּן |
| 1cp | נָתַ֫נּוּ | הָיִ֫ינוּ | קָטַ֫לְנוּ |

**Notes:**

1. In נָתַן, the final נ of the verbal root assimilates into the initial consonant of the Perfect sufformative in all second and first person forms.
2. הָיָה inflects as a regular III-ה verb, with one deviation: the 2mp (הֱיִיתֶם) and 2fp (הֱיִיתֶן) have Hateph Seghol under the initial ה because a guttural cannot take Vocal Shewa.

## 14.19 Something You Should Consider: The Biblical Languages and Pastoral Ministry (§14.19)

> No second-hand knowledge of the revelation of God for the salvation of a ruined world can suffice the needs of a ministry whose function it is to convey this revelation to men, commend it to their acceptance and apply it in detail to their needs … Nothing will suffice for it but to know; to know the book; to know it at first hand; and to know it through and through. And what is required first of all for training men for such a ministry is that the book should be given them in its very words [Hebrew, Aramaic, and Greek] as it has come from God's hand and in the fullness of its meaning, as that meaning has been ascertained by the labors of generations of men of God who have brought to bear upon it all the resources of sanctified scholarship and consecrated thought.
>
> —B. B. Warfield

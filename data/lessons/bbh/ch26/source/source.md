# BBH Chapter 26 — The Hiphil Stem: Strong Verbs

*Source: Pratico & Van Pelt, Basics of Biblical Hebrew Grammar (BBH), Chapter 26*

---

## 26.1 Introduction (§26.1)

The Hiphil stem occurs more frequently than any other derived stem. The name "Hiphil" (הִפְעִיל) preserves certain diagnostic features of the Hiphil Perfect: the הִ prefix and the Hireq Yod stem vowel.

---

## 26.2 The Meaning of the Hiphil Stem (§26.2)

1. **Causative.** The Hiphil stem commonly expresses causative action with an active voice. The key words "caused" and "made" can express the causative nuance, though idiomatic translations are preferred in good English style.

| Qal | Translation | Hiphil | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| זָכַר | he remembered | הִזְכִּיר | he caused to remember / he reminded |
| שָׁמַע | he heard | הִשְׁמִיעַ | he caused to hear / he proclaimed |
| קָרַב | he approached | הִקְרִיב | he caused to approach / he presented |

2. **Declarative.** The Hiphil may carry a declarative nuance: the subject declares someone to be in a certain state. Example: רָשַׁע (Q: to be guilty) → Hiphil: to declare guilty, to pronounce guilty.

3. **Factitive.** The Hiphil may change an intransitive verb into a transitive verb. Example: גָּדַל (Q: to be great) → Hiphil: to make someone or something great.

4. **Simple Action.** Some Hiphil verbs preserve the simple action of the Qal, especially when the root is rare in the Qal.

| Qal | Hiphil | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| שָׁכַם | הִשְׁכִּים | he arose early |
| שָׁלַךְ | הִשְׁלִיךְ | he threw (down) |
| שָׁמַד | הִשְׁמִיד | he exterminated |

5. There are a few other Hiphil categories (denominative, permissive) but these are infrequent. In cases where meaning cannot be easily categorized, consult the lexicon.

---

## 26.3 Hiphil Perfect (§26.3)

The Hiphil Perfect is formed with the Hiphil prefix (הִ), the verbal root (with a stem vowel variable between Hireq Yod and Pathach), and the Perfect sufformatives.

| | **Qal** | **Hiphil** | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **3ms** | קָטַל | הִקְטִיל | he caused to kill |
| **3fs** | קָֽטְלָה | הִקְטִ֫ילָה | she caused to kill |
| **2ms** | קָטַ֫לְתָּ | הִקְטַ֫לְתָּ | you caused to kill |
| **2fs** | קָטַלְתְּ | הִקְטַלְתְּ | you caused to kill |
| **1cs** | קָטַ֫לְתִּי | הִקְטַ֫לְתִּי | I caused to kill |
| **3cp** | קָֽטְלוּ | הִקְטִ֫ילוּ | they caused to kill |
| **2mp** | קְטַלְתֶּם | הִקְטַלְתֶּם | you caused to kill |
| **2fp** | קְטַלְתֶּן | הִקְטַלְתֶּן | you caused to kill |
| **1cp** | קָטַ֫לְנוּ | הִקְטַ֫לְנוּ | we caused to kill |

---

## 26.4 Hiphil Perfect Diagnostics (§26.4)

1. **הִ prefix in every form.**

2. **Stem vowel variable between Hireq Yod and Pathach.** All third person forms have Hireq Yod (הִקְטִיל); all second and first person forms have Pathach (הִקְטַ֫לְתָּ).

3. Do not confuse the הִ prefix with the Niphal Imperative and Infinitive Construct (הִקָּטֵל). The vowel patterns are distinctive: note the Daghesh Forte in the Niphal form vs. the uninterrupted root consonants in the Hiphil.

**Hiphil Perfect diagnostic forms: הִקְטִיל (3rd person) / הִקְטַ֫לְתָּ (2nd/1st person)**

---

## 26.5 Hiphil Imperfect (§26.5)

The Hiphil Imperfect is formed with the Imperfect preformatives, the verbal root (with Hireq Yod stem vowel), and the Imperfect sufformatives.

| | **Qal** | **Hiphil** | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **3ms** | יִקְטֹל | יַקְטִיל | he will cause to kill |
| **3fs** | תִּקְטֹל | תַּקְטִיל | she will cause to kill |
| **2ms** | תִּקְטֹל | תַּקְטִיל | you will cause to kill |
| **2fs** | תִּקְטְלִי | תַּקְטִ֫ילִי | you will cause to kill |
| **1cs** | אֶקְטֹל | אַקְטִיל | I will cause to kill |
| **3mp** | יִקְטְלוּ | יַקְטִ֫ילוּ | they will cause to kill |
| **3fp** | תִּקְטֹ֫לְנָה | תַּקְטֵ֫לְנָה | they will cause to kill |
| **2mp** | תִּקְטְלוּ | תַּקְטִ֫ילוּ | you will cause to kill |
| **2fp** | תִּקְטֹ֫לְנָה | תַּקְטֵ֫לְנָה | you will cause to kill |
| **1cp** | נִקְטֹל | נַקְטִיל | we will cause to kill |

---

## 26.6 Hiphil Imperfect Diagnostics (§26.6)

1. **Pathach preformative vowel in every form.** This also occurs in I-Guttural Type 2 Qal Imperfect verbs (יַעֲמֹד); the stem vowel distinguishes them (Qal: Holem; Hiphil: Hireq Yod).

2. **Hireq Yod stem vowel** in every form, except the 3fp and 2fp (תַּקְטֵ֫לְנָה), which have Tsere.

3. **With Waw Consecutive**, the stem vowel is usually Tsere, not Hireq Yod (e.g., יַקְטִיל → וַיַּקְטֵל).

**Hiphil Imperfect diagnostic form: יַקְטִיל**

---

## 26.7 Hiphil Imperative (§26.7)

The Hiphil Imperative is formed with the Hiphil prefix (הַ), the verbal root (with a stem vowel variable between Tsere and Hireq Yod), and the Imperative sufformatives.

| | **Qal** | **Hiphil** | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **2ms** | קְטֹל | הַקְטֵל | cause to kill! |
| **2fs** | קִטְלִי | הַקְטִ֫ילִי | cause to kill! |
| **2mp** | קִטְלוּ | הַקְטִ֫ילוּ | cause to kill! |
| **2fp** | קְטֹ֫לְנָה | הַקְטֵ֫לְנָה | cause to kill! |

---

## 26.8 Hiphil Imperative Diagnostics (§26.8)

1. **הַ prefix in every form.** This same prefix is used with the Hiphil Infinitive Construct and Infinitive Absolute.

2. **Stem vowel variable:** Tsere in 2ms and 2fp; Hireq Yod in 2fs and 2mp (forms with sufformatives consisting of a vowel).

**Hiphil Imperative diagnostic forms: הַקְטֵל (2ms) / הַקְטִ֫ילִי (2fs)**

---

## 26.9 Hiphil Infinitive Construct (§26.9)

The Hiphil Infinitive Construct is formed with the Hiphil prefix (הַ) and the verbal root (with Hireq Yod stem vowel). There are no sufformatives, since the Infinitive Construct is uninflected (though it may take pronominal suffixes, as in הַקְטִילִי with a 1cs suffix).

| **Qal** | **Hiphil** | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| קְטֹל | הַקְטִיל | to cause to kill |

---

## 26.10 Hiphil Infinitive Construct Diagnostics (§26.10)

1. **הַ prefix.**

2. **Hireq Yod stem vowel.** This distinguishes the Infinitive Construct (הַקְטִיל) from the Hiphil Imperative 2ms (הַקְטֵל).

**Hiphil Infinitive Construct diagnostic form: הַקְטִיל**

---

## 26.11 Hiphil Infinitive Absolute (§26.11)

The Hiphil Infinitive Absolute is formed with the Hiphil prefix (הַ) and the verbal root (with Tsere stem vowel). The stem vowel distinguishes the Infinitive Absolute (הַקְטֵל) from the Infinitive Construct (הַקְטִיל).

---

## 26.12 Hiphil Infinitive Absolute Diagnostics (§26.12)

1. **הַ prefix.**

2. **Tsere stem vowel.** Note that the Imperative 2ms and Infinitive Absolute forms are identical; context will enable correct identification.

**Hiphil Infinitive Absolute diagnostic form: הַקְטֵל**

---

## 26.13 Hiphil Participle (§26.13)

The Hiphil Participle is formed with the Participle prefix (מַ), the verbal root (with Hireq Yod stem vowel), and the Participle inflectional endings.

| | **Qal** | **Hiphil** | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ms** | קֹטֵל | מַקְטִיל | causing to kill |
| **fs** | קֹטֶ֫לֶת | מַקְטֶ֫לֶת | causing to kill |
| **mp** | קֹטְלִים | מַקְטִילִים | causing to kill |
| **fp** | קֹטְלוֹת | מַקְטִילוֹת | causing to kill |

---

## 26.14 Hiphil Participle Diagnostics (§26.14)

1. **מַ prefix in every form.** The מ prefix also characterizes the Participle in the Hophal, Piel, and Pual stems.

2. **Hireq Yod stem vowel** throughout, except the feminine singular (מַקְטֶ֫לֶת).

**Hiphil Participle diagnostic form: מַקְטִיל**

---

## 26.15 Summary (§26.15)

1. The Hiphil stem most commonly expresses causative action with an active voice. It may also express declarative and factitive types of action, as well as simple Qal action.

2. Most important diagnostics:
   - **ה prefix** in all conjugations except the Imperfect and Participle
   - **Pathach** under all preformatives and prefixes, except in the Perfect where the prefix vowel is Hireq (הִ)
   - **Hireq Yod or Tsere stem vowel** in all forms, except second and first person Perfect forms, which have Pathach

**The Hiphil Stem: Strong Verb Diagnostic Chart**

| Conjugation | Diagnostic Form(s) | Key Features |
|---|---|---|
| **Perfect** | הִקְטִיל / הִקְטַ֫לְתָּ | הִ prefix; Hireq Yod (3rd person), Pathach (2nd/1st person) |
| **Imperfect** | יַקְטִיל | Pathach preformative; Hireq Yod stem vowel |
| **Imperative** | הַקְטֵל / הַקְטִ֫ילִי | הַ prefix; Tsere (2ms, 2fp), Hireq Yod (2fs, 2mp) |
| **Infinitive Construct** | הַקְטִיל | הַ prefix; Hireq Yod stem vowel |
| **Infinitive Absolute** | הַקְטֵל | הַ prefix; Tsere stem vowel |
| **Participle** | מַקְטִיל | מַ prefix; Hireq Yod stem vowel |

## 26.17 Something You Should Memorize: Deuteronomy 7:21 (§26.17)

> Deuteronomy is the fifth book of Moses (Hebrew: אֵלֶּה הַדְּבָרִים, "These are the words"). In it, Moses retells the events of Exodus, Leviticus, and Numbers, emphasizing the mighty acts of God, the character of Israel's covenant Lord, the covenant relationship, and God's dynamic presence to redeem, protect, and guide from slavery to the promised land. All of this wealth is summarized in Deut 7:21:
>
> יְהוָה אֱלֹהֶיךָ בְּקִרְבֶּךָ אֵל גָּדוֹל וְנוֹרָא
>
> Yahweh your God is in your midst, a great and awesome God.
>
> The declaration is a verbless clause. The subject is יְהוָה followed by אֱלֹהִים with a 2ms pronominal suffix. The predicate is a prepositional phrase (בְּ + קֶ֫רֶב + 2ms suffix). The second half is an appositional construction: the divine name אֵל modified by גָּדוֹל (great) and נוֹרָא (awesome — a Niphal Participle ms of ירא meaning "awe-inspiring").
>
> What a shame that we have lost the word "awesome" in contemporary culture. Its power and meaning have been eviscerated and diluted to a point of absurdity. At its heart, the word communicates benumbing silence, profound reverence, indescribable wonder, and holy fear. The God of the Bible is truly an awesome God.
>
> Gary D. Pratico
> Professor of Old Testament Emeritus

# BBH Chapter 10 — Hebrew Construct Chain

*Source: Pratico & Van Pelt, Basics of Biblical Hebrew Grammar (BBH), Chapter 10*

---

## 10.1 Introduction (§10.1)

There is no word for "of" in biblical Hebrew. Hebrew expresses the possessive relationship between two nouns by the **construct chain** — placing two or more nouns side by side. For those who have studied Greek, the construct chain is Hebrew's rough equivalent of the genitive case.

| Hebrew | Translation |
|---|---|
| קוֹל הָאִישׁ | the voice of the man / the man's voice |
| מֶ֫לֶךְ הָאָ֫רֶץ | the king of the land |

The study of the construct chain is divided into two main sections: **function** (grammar, §10.2) and **form** (spelling of construct nouns, §10.3).

---

## 10.2 Basic Grammar of the Hebrew Construct Chain (§10.2)

### 1 — Construct and Absolute States

In a construct chain, the **first** noun is the **construct noun** (construct state); the **last** noun is the **absolute noun** (absolute state). The absolute form is also the lexical form.

- There is only **one absolute noun** per chain, always last.
- **Multiple construct nouns** may precede it.
- No words or particles may separate the nouns of a chain (except the definite article on the absolute noun).

### 2 — Construct Chains Linked with Maqqef

The nouns of a construct chain are sometimes joined by Maqqef:

| Hebrew | Translation |
|---|---|
| בֶּן־דָּוִד | the son of David |
| כָּל־הָעָם | all of the people |
| עֵץ־הַגָּן | the tree of the garden |

### 3 — Definiteness and Indefiniteness

A construct chain is either **entirely definite** or **entirely indefinite**. The construct noun never takes the definite article. The definiteness of the chain is determined by the **absolute noun**.

**Indefinite chains (absolute noun = indefinite):**

| Hebrew | Translation |
|---|---|
| קוֹל אִישׁ | a voice of a man |
| מֶ֫לֶךְ אֶ֫רֶץ | a king of a land |
| עֶ֫בֶד מֶ֫לֶךְ | a servant of a king |

**Definite chains (absolute noun = definite):**

| Hebrew | Translation |
|---|---|
| קוֹל הָאִישׁ | the voice of the man |
| מֶ֫לֶךְ הָאָ֫רֶץ | the king of the land |
| הַמֶּ֫לֶךְ עֶ֫בֶד | the servant of the king |

Although the construct nouns lack the definite article, they are considered definite when the absolute noun is definite. A noun is definite: (1) with the definite article (הַמַּלְכָּה), (2) with a pronominal suffix (סוּסוֹ), or (3) as a proper noun (שְׁמוּאֵל):

| Hebrew | Translation |
|---|---|
| עֶ֫בֶד שְׁמוּאֵל | the servant of Samuel (proper noun) |
| בֵּית מַלְכִּי | the house of my king (pronominal suffix) |
| סֵ֫פֶר אָבִ֫יהוּ | the book of his father (pronominal suffix) |

### 4 — Multiple Construct Nouns

Multiple construct nouns may appear in a chain, each translated with "of":

| Hebrew | Translation |
|---|---|
| עֶ֫בֶד אֲחִי הַמֶּ֫לֶךְ | the servant of the brother of the king |
| מֶ֫לֶךְ כָּל־הָאָ֫רֶץ | the king of all of the earth |
| כָּל־מִשְׁפַּ֫חַת בֵּית־אֲבִי אִמּוֹ | all of the family of the house of the father of his mother (Judg 9:1) |

### 5 — Construct Nouns with Inseparable Prepositions

Although a construct noun cannot take the definite article, it may appear with an inseparable preposition (בְּ, כְּ, לְ):

| Hebrew | Translation |
|---|---|
| בְּבֵית דָּוִד | in the house of David |
| כְּכָל־הַגּוֹיִם | like all of the nations |
| לְעַם־הָאָרֶץ | to the people of the land |

Note: the Daghesh Lene in the second ב of בְּבֵית and the second כ of כְּכָל is lost because these begadkephat consonants are now preceded by an open syllable.

### 6 — Construct Chains with Attributive Adjectives

Because no word may separate the nouns of a construct chain, an attributive adjective must **follow the entire chain**. It agrees with the noun it modifies in gender, number, and definiteness.

| Hebrew | Translation |
|---|---|
| דְּבַר הַמַּלְכָּה הַטּוֹב | the good word of the queen (adjective ms modifies construct noun) |
| דְּבַר הַמַּלְכָּה הַטּוֹבָה | the word of the good queen (adjective fs modifies absolute noun) |

In ambiguous constructions (same gender and number for both nouns), context must be the guide:

| Hebrew | Translation |
|---|---|
| דְּבַר הַמֶּ֫לֶךְ הַטּוֹב | the good word of the king **or** the word of the good king |
| דִּבְרֵי הַמְּלָכִים הַטּוֹבִים | the good words of the kings **or** the words of the good kings |

### 7 — Construct Chains with Demonstrative Adjectives

Like attributive adjectives, demonstrative adjectives must follow the construct chain:

| Hebrew | Translation |
|---|---|
| אֵ֫שֶׁת הָאִישׁ הַזֶּה | the wife of this man |
| אֵ֫שֶׁת הָאִישׁ הַטּוֹב הַזֶּה | the wife of this good man |
| דִּבְרֵי הַנָּבִיא הַזֶּה | the words of this prophet |
| דִּבְרֵי הַנְּבִיאִים הָאֵ֫לֶּה | these words of the prophets **or** the words of these prophets |

### 8 — Adjectives in the Construct State

A substantive adjective (used as a noun) may appear in the construct state:

| Hebrew | Translation | Note |
|---|---|---|
| יְפַת מַרְאֶה | beautiful of appearance | יְפַת = fs construct of יָפֶה |
| חֲכַם־לֵב | wise of heart | חֲכַם = ms construct of חָכָם |
| קְדוֹשׁ יִשְׂרָאֵל | the Holy One of Israel | קְדוֹשׁ = ms construct of קָדוֹשׁ |

## 10.3 The Spelling of Nouns in the Construct State (§10.3)

When a noun enters the construct state, it surrenders its primary accent to the absolute noun; the entire chain is treated as a single accented unit. This often causes vowel changes. Two basic categories of change: **(1) vowel reduction** and **(2) a change in the noun's ending**.

### 1 — Vowel Reduction

**a. Vowel Reduction in Closed Syllables**

Changeable long vowels in a final closed syllable reduce to a short vowel when the syllable becomes unaccented:

| Absolute | → | Construct | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| מִשְׁפָּט | → | מִשְׁפַּט | judgment of |
| מִדְבָּר | → | מִדְבַּר | wilderness of |
| בֵּן | → | בֶּן | son of |
| כֹּל | → | כָּל | all of (Qamets Hatuf) |

With a few words, Tsere reduces to Pathach: מִזְבֵּחַ → מִזְבַּח (altar of).

**b. Vowel Reduction in Open Syllables**

Changeable long vowels in an open syllable reduce to Vocal Shewa (or Hateph Pathach with gutturals) as the syllable moves to the propretonic position:

| Absolute | → | Construct | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| נָבִיא | → | נְבִיא | prophet of |
| הָמוֹן | → | הֲמוֹן | multitude of |
| דָּבָר | → | דְּבַר | word of |

In דָּבָר → דְּבַר, both long vowels reduce: Qamets → Vocal Shewa in the open syllable (דְּ), Qamets → Pathach in the closed syllable (בַּר).

**c. Vowel Reduction and the First Rule of Shewa**

In plural nouns like בְּרָכוֹת, the Qamets under the second root letter reduces to Vocal Shewa in the construct state (בְּרְכוֹת). Then the First Rule of Shewa (§4.11.1) converts the two initial Vocal Shewas to Hireq + Silent Shewa:

| Absolute | → | Construct | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| בְּרָכוֹת | → | בִּרְכוֹת | blessings of |
| דְּבָרִים | → | דִּבְרֵי | words of |
| צְבָאוֹת | → | צִבְאוֹת | armies of |

### 2 — Changes with the Endings of Construct Nouns

**a. Masculine Plural and Dual Endings → ֵי (Tsere Yod)**

| Absolute | → | Construct | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| אֱלֹהִים | → | אֱלֹהֵי | God of |
| בָּנִים | → | בְּנֵי | sons of |
| עֵינַ֫יִם | → | עֵינֵי | (two) eyes of |

**b. Feminine Singular Ending ָה → ַת (Pathach Taw)**

| Absolute | → | Construct | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| תּוֹרָה | → | תּוֹרַת | law of |
| מַלְכָּה | → | מַלְכַּת | queen of |
| שָׁנָה | → | שְׁנַת | year of |

**c. Feminine Plural Ending וֹת — No Change**

| Absolute | → | Construct | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| מַמְלָכוֹת | → | מַמְלְכוֹת | kingdoms of |
| מִשְׁפָּחוֹת | → | מִשְׁפְּחוֹת | families of |
| בָּנוֹת | → | בְּנוֹת | daughters of |

**d. Masculine Nouns Ending in ֶה → ֵה (Tsere He)**

| Absolute | → | Construct | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| שָׂדֶה | → | שְׂדֵה | field of |
| מַחֲנֶה | → | מַחֲנֵה | camp of |
| מַטֶּה | → | מַטֵּה | tribe of |

**e. Monosyllabic Nouns — Hireq Yod Added to Stem**

Certain (not all) singular monosyllabic nouns add Hireq Yod to their stem in the construct state (cf. pronominal suffixes, §9.9). Note the difference: construct forms have a reduced vowel (Hateph Pathach) under the first root letter; forms with a pronominal suffix maintain the long Qamets.

| Absolute | → | Construct | Pronominal Suffix |
|---|---|---|---|
| אָב | → | אֲבִי (father of) | אָבִי (my father) |
| אָח | → | אֲחִי (brother of) | אָחִי (my brother) |
| פֶּה | → | פִּי (mouth of) | פִּי (my mouth) |

### 3 — Other Spelling Features

**a. Plural Segholate Nouns**

In the construct state, plural Segholate nouns begin with a short vowel in a closed syllable:

| Absolute | → | Construct | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| עֲבָדִים | → | עַבְדֵי | servants of |
| שְׁבָטִים | → | שִׁבְטֵי | tribes of |
| חֳדָשִׁים | → | חָדְשֵׁי | months of |

**b. Diphthongs**

The AY diphthong changes to Tsere Yod; the AW diphthong changes to Holem Waw:

| Absolute | → | Construct | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| בַּ֫יִת | → | בֵּית | house of |
| מָ֫וֶת | → | מוֹת | death of |

**c. No Change in Spelling**

Monosyllabic nouns with an unchangeable long vowel and singular Segholate nouns exhibit **no spelling changes** in the construct state. Their construct form must be identified from context.

| Absolute | → | Construct | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| קוֹל | → | קוֹל | voice of |
| שִׁיר | → | שִׁיר | song of |
| מֶ֫לֶךְ | → | מֶ֫לֶךְ | king of |

## 10.4 Summary (§10.4)

1. The construct chain expresses the "of" relationship by placing nouns side by side. There is one absolute noun (always last); multiple construct nouns may precede it.
2. **Basic grammar:**
   - Nouns may be linked by Maqqef (בֶּן־דָּוִד).
   - The chain is entirely definite or indefinite; the construct noun never takes the definite article.
   - A construct noun may appear with an inseparable preposition (בְּבֵית דָּוִד).
   - Attributive and demonstrative adjectives must follow the entire chain.
3. **Vowel reduction:**
   - Changeable long vowels in a final closed syllable reduce to a short vowel (בֵּן → בֶּן).
   - Changeable long vowels in an open syllable reduce to Vocal Shewa (נָבִיא → נְבִיא) or Hateph Pathach with gutturals.
4. **Ending changes:**
   - Masculine plural and dual endings → ֵי (אֱלֹהִים → אֱלֹהֵי).
   - Feminine singular ending ָה → ַת (תּוֹרָה → תּוֹרַת).
   - Feminine plural ending וֹת — no change.
   - Masculine singular nouns ending in ֶה → ֵה (שָׂדֶה → שְׂדֵה).
   - Certain monosyllabic nouns add Hireq Yod (אָב → אֲבִי).
5. **Other features:**
   - Plural Segholate nouns begin with a short vowel in a closed syllable (עֲבָדִים → עַבְדֵי).
   - AY diphthong → Tsere Yod (בַּ֫יִת → בֵּית); AW diphthong → Holem Waw (מָ֫וֶת → מוֹת).
   - Monosyllabic nouns with unchangeable long vowels and singular Segholate nouns show no spelling change.

## 10.6 Additional Information: The Superlative (§10.6)

In §6.6.2 we learned that מִן with כֹּל forms a superlative (מִכֹּל). Two additional superlative constructions:

**1. A definite adjective** (made definite by the article, a pronominal suffix, or the construct state) may express the superlative. Context will suggest the possibility.

| Hebrew | Literal | Superlative |
|---|---|---|
| מִן־הַקָּטֹן וְעַד־הַגָּדוֹל | from the small one to the great one | from the smallest to the greatest (1 Sam 30:19) |
| הַקָּטֹן אֶת־אָבִינוּ הַיּוֹם | the young one is with our father today | the youngest is with our father today (Gen 42:13) |
| הַיָּפָה בַּנָּשִׁים | the beautiful one among women | the most beautiful among women (Song 1:8) |
| בְּנוֹ הַקָּטָן | his young son | his youngest son (Gen 9:24) |

**2. A construct chain** in which the construct noun is singular and the absolute noun is plural and definite — and both nouns are the same:

| Hebrew | Superlative Translation | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| קֹ֫דֶשׁ הַקֳּדָשִׁים | the most holy place ("the holy of holies") | Exod 26:33 |
| אֲדֹנֵי הָאֲדֹנִים | the greatest Lord ("the Lord of lords") | Deut 10:17 |
| שִׁיר הַשִּׁירִים | the greatest song ("the song of songs") | Song 1:1 |

## 10.7 Something You Should Know: The Covenant Donkey (§10.7)

Genesis 49:10–12 contains a messianic prophecy that touches on both suffering and glory. In Gen 49:11, two terms describe the animal:

> אֹסְרִי לַגֶּפֶן עִירוֹ
> He ties to the vine his foal,
>
> וְלַשּׂרֵקָה בְּנִי אֲתֹנוֹ
> and to the choice vine the son of his female donkey.

The terms עַ֫יִר and בְּנִי אֲתֹנוֹ are picked up in Zech 9 — the primary OT background for the Gospel accounts of Jesus' triumphal entry (Matt 21:1–9; Mark 11:1–10; Luke 19:28–38; John 12:12–16).

A Mari text (ca. 18th century BC) describes a covenant ratification ceremony in which a specific breed of donkey (cognate to Hebrew עַ֫יִר בֶּן אָתוֹן) was slaughtered to seal the covenant — symbolizing the curse on anyone who broke it. This was the **covenant donkey**. The shedding of its blood ratified the covenant.

Against this background, Messiah's association with the covenant donkey hints that the path to glory passes through the shedding of covenant blood.

Zechariah 9:9bc picks up the same language:

> הִנֵּה מַלְכֵּךְ יָבוֹא לָךְ
> Behold, your king will come to you.
>
> צַדִּיק וְנוֹשָׁע הוּא
> He is righteous and vindicated,
>
> עָנִי וְרֹכֵב עַל־חֲמוֹר
> afflicted and riding on a donkey,
>
> וְעַל־עַיִר בֶּן־אֲתֹנוֹת
> and on a foal, son of a female donkey.

The coming king is righteous and victorious (Messiah's glory) but also afflicted and riding on the covenant donkey (Messiah's suffering). By riding into Jerusalem on this animal, Jesus signals that he comes as the one who sheds the blood of the covenant — the primary purpose of his triumphal entry.

# BBH Chapter 8 — Hebrew Pronouns

*Source: Pratico & Van Pelt, Basics of Biblical Hebrew Grammar (BBH), Chapter 8*

---

## 8.1 Introduction (§8.1)

A pronoun is a word that replaces a noun. The noun that a pronoun refers back to is called the **antecedent**. In Hebrew, there are personal, demonstrative, relative, and interrogative pronouns. Hebrew pronouns function much like their English counterparts.

---

## 8.2 Independent Personal Pronouns (§8.2)

The independent personal pronoun is **independent** because it stands alone and is not prefixed or suffixed to another word. Personal pronouns can be third (he, she, it, they), second (you), or first (I, we) person and either singular or plural. Second and third person pronouns have masculine and feminine forms. First person pronouns are **common** — they may refer to either masculine or feminine nouns (not inflected for gender).

Hebrew independent personal pronouns are **subjective** — used as the subject of a verb or as the subject of a verbless clause. They are never used as the object of the verb.

---

---

## 8.4 The Use of the Independent Personal Pronoun (§8.4)

The independent personal pronouns must be memorized as vocabulary. They may be used in a predicative relationship with nouns or adjectives in **verbless clauses**. A form of the verb "to be" in the present tense must be supplied in translation. In normal word order, the pronoun may precede or follow the noun or adjective.

| Hebrew | Translation |
|---|---|
| אֲנִי יְהוָה | I am Yahweh. |
| הוּא נָבִיא צַדִּיק | He is a righteous prophet. |
| אַתָּה מֶ֫לֶךְ טוֹב | You (2ms) are a good king. |
| אַחִים אֲנַ֫חְנוּ | We are brothers. |
| הִיא אִשָּׁה חֲכָמָה | She is a wise woman. |
| אַתֶּם בָּעִיר הַגְּדוֹלָה | You (2mp) are in the great city. |

---

## 8.5 Demonstrative Pronouns and Adjectives (§8.5)

Hebrew demonstratives correspond to English "this," "these," "that," and "those." They may function as either adjectives (this man, those women) or pronouns (this is the man, those are the women). The level of specificity increases: "a man" → "the man" → "this man."

---

## 8.6 Demonstrative Paradigm (§8.6)

**Near demonstratives (this / these):**

| | Masculine | Feminine |
|---|---|---|
| **Singular** | זֶה | זֹאת |
| **Plural** | אֵ֫לֶּה | אֵ֫לֶּה |

**Far demonstratives (that / those):**

| | Masculine | Feminine |
|---|---|---|
| **Singular** | הוּא | הִיא |
| **Plural** | הֵ֫מָּה, הֵם | הֵ֫נָּה, הֵן |

**Notes:**

1. The masculine and feminine singular forms הוּא and הִיא are identical to the third person masculine and feminine independent personal pronouns. Context usually clarifies the usage.

   | Hebrew | Possible Translations |
   |---|---|
   | הוּא הָאִישׁ | He is the man. (personal pronoun) / That is the man. (demonstrative) |
   | הִיא הָאִשָּׁה | She is the woman. (personal pronoun) / That is the woman. (demonstrative) |

2. The demonstrative אֵ֫לֶּה (these) is **common** — not inflected for gender; it can modify either masculine or feminine nouns.
3. Rare variant forms (no need to memorize): זֶה → הַלָּז / הַלָּזֶה; זֹאת → זֹה / זוֹ / הַלֵּ֫זוּ; אֵ֫לֶּה → אֵל.

---

## 8.7 The Use of the Hebrew Demonstratives (§8.7)

### 1 — Demonstrative Adjectives

When functioning as an **adjective**, the demonstrative **follows** the noun and **agrees in gender, number, and definiteness** (cf. attributive adjective, §7.5.1).

| Hebrew | Translation |
|---|---|
| הָאִישׁ הַזֶּה | this man |
| הָאִשָּׁה הַזֹּאת | this woman |
| הָאִישׁ הַהוּא | that man |
| הָאִשָּׁה הַהִיא | that woman |
| הָאֲנָשִׁים הָאֵ֫לֶּה | these men |
| הַנָּשִׁים הָאֵ֫לֶּה | these women |
| הָאֲנָשִׁים הָהֵם | those men |
| הַנָּשִׁים הָהֵ֫נָּה | those women |

When an attributive adjective is added, it is placed **between** the noun and the demonstrative:

| Hebrew | Translation |
|---|---|
| הָאִישׁ הַטּוֹב הַזֶּה | this good man |
| הָאִשָּׁה הַטּוֹבָה הַזֹּאת | this good woman |
| הָאִישׁ הַטּוֹב הַהוּא | that good man |
| הָאִשָּׁה הַטּוֹבָה הַהִיא | that good woman |
| הָאֲנָשִׁים הַטּוֹבִים הָאֵ֫לֶּה | these good men |
| הַנָּשִׁים הַטּוֹבוֹת הָהֵ֫נָּה | those good women |

### 2 — Demonstrative Pronouns

When functioning as a **pronoun**, the demonstrative **precedes** the noun and **agrees in gender and number but not definiteness** (cf. predicative adjective, §7.5.2).

| Hebrew | Translation |
|---|---|
| זֶה הָאִישׁ | This is the man. |
| זֹאת הָאִשָּׁה | This is the woman. |
| הוּא הָאִישׁ | That is the man. |
| הִיא הָאִשָּׁה | That is the woman. |
| אֵ֫לֶּה הָאֲנָשִׁים | These are the men. |
| אֵ֫לֶּה הַנָּשִׁים | These are the women. |
| הֵם הָאֲנָשִׁים | Those are the men. |
| הֵ֫נָּה הַנָּשִׁים | Those are the women. |

An attributive adjective follows the noun and agrees in gender, number, and definiteness:

| Hebrew | Translation |
|---|---|
| זֶה הָאִישׁ הַטּוֹב | This is the good man. |
| אֵ֫לֶּה הָאֲנָשִׁים הַטּוֹבִים | These are the good men. |
| הִיא הָאִשָּׁה הַטּוֹבָה | That is the good woman. |
| הֵ֫נָּה הַנָּשִׁים הַטּוֹבוֹת | Those are the good women. |

---

## 8.8 Relative Pronoun (§8.8)

The Hebrew word **אֲשֶׁר** functions as the relative pronoun (who, whom, whose, which, that). The form **never changes** — it does not inflect for gender or number. It immediately follows the noun it modifies.

| Hebrew | Translation |
|---|---|
| הָעֵץ אֲשֶׁר בְּתוֹךְ־הַגָּן | the tree that (is) in the middle of the garden |
| הֶהָרִים אֲשֶׁר־תַּ֫חַת הַשָּׁמַ֫יִם | the mountains that (are) under the heavens |
| הַמֶּ֫לֶךְ אֲשֶׁר בְּחַרְתֶּם | the king whom you chose |
| דָּוִד אֲשֶׁר בְּבֵית יְהוָה | David, who (is) in the house of Yahweh |

---

## 8.9 Interrogative Pronouns (§8.9)

The main interrogative pronouns are **מִי** (who? whom?) and **מָה** (what?). These do not inflect and can appear with or without Maqqef. מָה also occurs as מַה (usually with Daghesh Forte in the following word) and מֶה.

| Hebrew | Translation |
|---|---|
| מַה־שְּׁמוֹ | What (is) his name? |
| מָה הַחֲלוֹם הַזֶּה | What (is) this dream? |
| מֶה־עָשִׂ֫יתָ | What have you done? |
| מִי־אַתָּה | Who (are) you? |
| מִי־הָאִישׁ הַזֶּה | Who (is) this man? |
| מִי הָאֲנָשִׁים הָאֵ֫לֶּה | Who (are) these men? |

---

## 8.10 The Interrogative Particle הֲ (§8.10)

In Hebrew, questions are not formed with punctuation but with the **interrogative particle הֲ** prefixed to the first word of the sentence.

| Declarative | Interrogative |
|---|---|
| שָׁלַח הַמֶּ֫לֶךְ אֶת־הַנָּבִיא | הֲשָׁלַח הַמֶּ֫לֶךְ אֶת־הַנָּבִיא |
| The king sent the prophet. | Did the king send the prophet? |

**Spelling rules for the interrogative particle:**

| Condition | Form |
|---|---|
| Before most consonants | הֲ |
| Before a guttural consonant or consonant with Shewa | הַ |
| Before a guttural consonant followed by Qamets | הֶ |

**Distinguishing the interrogative particle from the definite article:**

1. The interrogative particle normally does **not** have an associated Daghesh Forte.
2. The interrogative particle may be prefixed to verbal forms or other particles — which cannot take the definite article.
3. The Hateph Pathach spelling (הֲ) is not a form of the definite article.

---

## 8.11 Summary (§8.11)

1. A pronoun replaces a noun; the noun it refers back to is the **antecedent**.
2. Independent personal pronouns are **subjective** — used as the subject of a verb or verbless clause. The paradigm in §8.3 must be memorized.
3. Demonstratives may be used as:
   - **Adjectives** — follow the noun; agree in gender, number, and definiteness: הָאִישׁ הַזֶּה (this man).
   - **Pronouns** — precede the noun; agree in gender and number but not definiteness: זֶה הָאִישׁ (this is the man).
4. **אֲשֶׁר** (who, that, which) is the Hebrew relative pronoun. Its form never changes.
5. The main interrogative pronouns are **מִי** (who?) and **מָה** (what?). They do not inflect.
6. The **interrogative particle הֲ** is prefixed to the first word of an interrogative clause. It occurs as הֲ (most cases), הַ (before gutturals and Shewa), or הֶ (before guttural + Qamets).

## 8.13 Additional Information: More Interrogative Pronouns (§8.13)

**1. לָמָה or לָ֫מָּה (why?)**

> עֲזַבְתָּנִי לָמָה אֵלִי אֵלִי
> My God, my God, why have you forsaken me? (Ps 22:2 [English 22:1])

> לָ֫מָּה תַעֲשֶׂה כֹה לַעֲבָדֶיךָ
> Why are you doing thus to your servants? (Exod 5:15)

**2. אֵיךְ or אֵיכָה (how?)**

> וְאֵיךְ אֶעֱשֶׂה הָרָעָה הַגְּדֹלָה הַזֹּאת וְחָטָאתִי לֵאלֹהִים
> How could I do this great evil and sin against God? (Gen 39:9)

> אֵיכָה נֵדַע אֶת־הַדָּבָר
> How may we know the word? (Deut 18:21)

**3. אֵי or אַיֵּה (where?)**

> וַיֹּאמֶר יְהוָה אֶל־קַיִן אֵי הֶבֶל אָחִיךָ
> And Yahweh said to Cain, "Where is Abel your brother?" (Gen 4:9)

> אַיֵּה שָׂרָה אִשְׁתֶּךָ
> Where is Sarah your wife? (Gen 18:9)

## 8.14 Something You Should Know: Who is Like You? Part 1 (§8.14)

The interrogative pronouns מִי and מָה have wide ranges of use. As a general rule, מִי is used for persons and מָה for things. Both appear in **rhetorical questions** — questions posed for effect that neither demand nor expect an answer. In certain circumstances, a rhetorical question can function as a negation.

> מִי יֵלֶךְ־לָנוּ אֶשְׁלַח וּמִי
> Whom shall I send and who will go for us? (Isa 6:8)

> מִי זֶה מֶלֶךְ הַכָּבוֹד
> Who is this king of glory? (Ps 24:8)

> מִי כָמֹכָה בָּאֵלִם יְהוָה מִי כָּמֹכָה נֶאְדָּר בַּקֹּדֶשׁ נוֹרָא תְהִלֹּת עֹשֵׂה פֶלֶא
> Who is like you, Yahweh, among the gods? Who is like you, majestic in holiness, awesome in glorious deeds, doing wonders? (Exod 15:11)

> מִי־אֵל גָּדוֹל כֵּאלֹהִים אֱלֹהִים בַּקֹּדֶשׁ דַּרְכֶּךָ
> Your way, O God, is holy. What god is great like our God? (Ps 77:14–15 [13–14])

> יְהוָה כָּל עַצְמוֹתַי תֹּאמַרְנָה מִי כָמוֹךָ
> All my bones say, Yahweh, who is like you? (Ps 35:10)

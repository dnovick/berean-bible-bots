§2 — ענה (I-guttural + III-ה)  to afflict, humble, oppress

I-guttural note: ע (R1) is a guttural. In the Piel Perfect the i-class vowel (Hireq) still appears under ע — no composite Shewa required since ע can take a simple vowel. In the Imperfect, ע takes pathach (the normal a-class position for R1). No dagesh forte is possible in ע, but the pattern is otherwise the same as §1.

§3 — הלל (Geminate)  to praise, boast

Geminate note: In a geminate root R2 = R3. The Piel treats R2 as the doubled radical and R3 as the repeated final consonant. Perfect 3ms: הִ (i-class) + לּ (R2 with dagesh forte) + ֵ + ל (R3) = הִלֵּל. Before consonantal suffixes the inter-radical vowel reduces: הִלַּלְתָּ. Before vowel suffixes the vowel under R2 becomes Shewa: הִלְּלוּ.

§4 — בּאר (II-Guttural / II-aleph)  to explain, expound

II-Guttural note (BBH Ch31 priority class): R2 = א (aleph) refuses Dagesh Forte. Three simultaneous changes from strong Piel: (1) No Dagesh in R2; (2) Compensatory lengthening — Short Hireq → Long Tsere under R1 in the Perfect; Short Pathach → Long Qamets under R1 in the Imperfect / Imperative / Participle; (3) Alef takes Pathach [ַ] (a-class) instead of Tsere, and Hateph-Pathach [ֲ] instead of simple Shewa before vowel suffixes. These three changes together make this the most visually distinct Piel weak class. Attested: בֵּאַר (Deut 1:5, "Moses began to expound this law").

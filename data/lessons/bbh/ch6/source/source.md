# BBH Chapter 6 — Hebrew Prepositions

*Source: Pratico & Van Pelt, Basics of Biblical Hebrew Grammar (BBH), Chapter 6*

---

## 6.1 Introduction (§6.1)

Hebrew prepositions function like English prepositions, introducing prepositional phrases that describe relationships between words — spatial (upon the altar, before Yahweh) or temporal (until the day, after the flood).

There are three types of Hebrew prepositions, classified by their form and relationship to their object:

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| **Independent** | Stand alone, just like English prepositions |
| **Maqqef** | Joined to their object by a raised horizontal stroke (Maqqef) |
| **Inseparable** | Prefixed directly to their objects; never occur independently |

---

## 6.2 Independent Prepositions (§6.2)

Independent prepositions stand alone before their objects.

| Hebrew | Translation |
|---|---|
| לִפְנֵי הַמֶּ֫לֶךְ | before the king |
| אַחַר הַמַּבּוּל | after the flood |

---

## 6.3 Maqqef Prepositions (§6.3)

These prepositions are joined to their objects by Maqqef (־). Common examples: עַל־ (on, upon), אֶל־ (to, toward), עַד־ (until), מִן־ (from).

| Hebrew | Translation |
|---|---|
| עַל־הָאֶ֫בֶן | upon the stone |
| מִן־הָאָ֫רֶץ | from the land |

---

## 6.4 Inseparable Prepositions (§6.4)

The three inseparable prepositions — **בְּ** (in, by, with), **כְּ** (like, as), **לְ** (to, for) — are always prefixed to their objects.

### Before Most Consonants — Vocal Shewa

| Hebrew | Translation |
|---|---|
| בְּשָׂדֶה | in a field |
| כְּמֶ֫לֶךְ | like a king |
| לְנַ֫עַר | for a young man |

### Before Hateph Vowels — Corresponding Short Vowel

| Hebrew | Translation |
|---|---|
| בֶּאֱמֶת | in truth |
| כַּאֲנָשִׁים | like men |
| לָחֳלִי | for sickness |

### Before Consonants with Vocal Shewa — Hireq (Rule of Shewa, §4.11.1)

| Hebrew | Translation |
|---|---|
| בִּזְרוֹעַ | with an arm |
| כִּשְׁאוֹל | like Sheol |
| לִבְרִית | for a covenant |

### With the Definite Article

The preposition replaces the ה of the definite article; the vowel and Daghesh Forte of the article are retained.

| Indefinite | Definite | With Preposition | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| מִדְבָּר | הַמִּדְבָּר | בַּמִּדְבָּר | in the wilderness |
| מֶ֫לֶךְ | הַמֶּ֫לֶךְ | כַּמֶּ֫לֶךְ | like the king |
| אִשָּׁה | הָאִשָּׁה | לָאִשָּׁה | to the woman |

---

## 6.5 The Form of the Preposition מִן (§6.5)

מִן occurs both as a Maqqef preposition and as an inseparable preposition.

**As a Maqqef preposition:** מִן־הַבַּ֫יִת (from the house).

**As an inseparable preposition:** the נ assimilates into the following consonant and appears as Daghesh Forte.

| Maqqef Form | → | Inseparable Form | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| מִן־מֶ֫לֶךְ | → | מִמֶּ֫לֶךְ | from a king |

**With begadkephat:** the Daghesh Lene becomes Daghesh Forte: מִבַּ֫יִת (from a house).

**With gutturals (א, ע, ר):** the guttural rejects the Daghesh Forte; Hireq lengthens to Tsere (**compensatory lengthening**): מֵאִישׁ (from a man), מֵעִיר (from a city), מֵהָאָ֫רֶץ (from the land).

**With ח:** usually virtual doubling (no lengthening): מִחוּץ (from outside). Occasionally compensatory lengthening occurs: מֵחֶ֫רֶב (from a sword).

---

## 6.6 The Use of the Preposition מִן (§6.6)

מִן occurs 7,592 times and has three notable special uses:

### 1 — Comparative Use ("better than")

| Hebrew | Literal | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| טוֹבָה חָכְמָה מִזָּהָב | Wisdom is good from gold | Wisdom is better than gold |
| קָשָׁה הָעֲבוֹדָה מֵהָאֲנָשִׁים | The work is difficult from the men | The work is too difficult for the men |

### 2 — Superlative Use (with כֹּל)

מִכֹּל ("from every") forms the superlative:

| Hebrew | Literal | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| עָרוּם מִכֹּל חַיַּת הַשָּׂדֶה | clever from every living thing of the field | the most clever living thing of the field |

### 3 — Partitive Use ("some of")

| Hebrew | Literal | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| מִפְּרִי | from a fruit | some fruit |
| מֵהָאֲנָשִׁים | from the men | some (of the) men |

---

## 6.7 The Definite Direct Object Marker (§6.7)

In Hebrew prose, **definite direct objects** are usually marked with **אֵת / אֶת־** (the accusative marker). The marker is never translated — it is a grammatical function word only.

A direct object is definite when it has the definite article, is a proper noun, or has a pronominal suffix.

| Hebrew | Translation |
|---|---|
| אֶת־הַדְּבָרִים בַּסֵּ֫פֶר כָּתַב | He wrote the words in the book. |
| הַשָּׁמַ֫יִם אֵת בָּרָא אֱלֹהִים | God created the heavens. |
| אֶת־הַסֵּ֫פֶר לַמֶּ֫לֶךְ נָתַן הַנָּבִיא | The prophet gave the book to the king. |
| אֶת־הַהֵיכָל הַגָּדוֹל בָּנָה הַמֶּ֫לֶךְ | The king built the great temple. |
| אֶת־יְהוֹנָתָן אָהַב דָּוִד | David loved Jonathan. |

**Indefinite direct objects are NOT marked:**

| Hebrew | Translation |
|---|---|
| בָּנָה הַמֶּ֫לֶךְ הֵיכָל גָּדוֹל | The king built a great temple. |
| בָּנָה הַנַּ֫עַר בַּ֫יִת קָטֹן בָּעִיר | The young man built a small house in the city. |

---

## 6.8 Summary (§6.8)

1. Three types of Hebrew prepositions: independent, Maqqef, and inseparable.
2. Inseparable prepositions בְּ, כְּ, לְ are prefixed directly to their objects:
   - Before most consonants: Vocal Shewa (בְּ, כְּ, לְ)
   - Before Hateph vowel: corresponding short vowel
   - Before Vocal Shewa: Hireq (Rule of Shewa)
   - With definite article: ה drops; article's vowel and Daghesh Forte are retained
3. מִן occurs as both Maqqef and inseparable. When inseparable, נ assimilates as Daghesh Forte (rejected by gutturals → compensatory lengthening or virtual doubling).
4. Special uses of מִן: comparative (better than), superlative with כֹּל, partitive (some of).
5. Definite direct objects are marked with אֵת / אֶת־ (never translated).

## 6.10 Additional Information: Compound Prepositions (§6.10)

**Prepositions from פָּנִים (face):**

| Compound | Meaning | Components |
|---|---|---|
| לִפְנֵי | before, in the presence of | לְ + פָּנִים |
| מִפְּנֵי | away from, on account of | מִן + פָּנִים |
| מִלִּפְנֵי | away from, from the presence of | מִן + לְ + פָּנִים |
| עַל־פְּנֵי | in front of, opposite to | עַל + פָּנִים |

**Prepositions from two prepositions:**

| Compound | Meaning | Components |
|---|---|---|
| מֵעַל | from upon | מִן + עַל |
| מִתַּ֫חַת | from under | מִן + תַּ֫חַת |
| מֵאֵת | from with | מִן + אֵת |

**Prepositions from a noun + preposition:**

| Compound | Meaning | Components |
|---|---|---|
| עַל־דְּבַר | on account of | עַל + דָּבָר |

| מִתּוֹךְ | from the midst of | מִן + תָּ֫וֶךְ |
| בְּקֶ֫רֶב | in the midst of | בְּ + קֶ֫רֶב |

## 6.11 Something You Should Consider: The Humanity of Christ and the Grammar of Scripture (§6.11)

> An evangelical believes that God humbled himself not only in the incarnation of the Son, but also in the inspiration of the Scriptures. The manger and the cross were not sensational. Neither are grammar and syntax. But that is how God chose to reveal himself. A poor Jewish peasant and a prepositional phrase have this in common — they are both human and both ordinary. That the poor peasant was God and the prepositional phrase is the Word of God does not change this fact. Therefore, if God humbled himself to take on human flesh and to speak human language, woe to us if we arrogantly presume to ignore the humanity of Christ and the grammar of Scripture.
>
> —John Piper, Chancellor of Bethlehem College & Seminary, Minneapolis, Minnesota

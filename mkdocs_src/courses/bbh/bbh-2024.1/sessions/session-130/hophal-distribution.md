# Hophal — OT Distribution by Book

The Hophal stem is the **passive of the Hiphil** (causative-passive). With only ~408 tokens across the entire Hebrew OT,
it is one of the rarest verbal stems — roughly 1 Hophal for every 750 Hebrew words.

## Key Observations

1. **Torah dominates — but unevenly.** Exodus (3.23/1k) and Leviticus (3.43/1k) are the highest in the entire OT. Legal-causal vocabulary is dense there: tabernacle construction, sacrificial procedure, and purity laws generate many causative → passive Hophal forms. **Deuteronomy is strikingly lower (0.63/1k)** — it retells the same legal material but as direct address, so the passive framing drops sharply.

2. **Major Prophets cluster around 2/1k.** Isaiah (2.19), Ezekiel (1.87), and Jeremiah (1.33) all use the Hophal regularly, reflecting the prophetic rhetoric of divine judgment carried out on passive objects (nations, cities, peoples).

3. **Malachi is surprisingly high (3.42/1k).** A tiny book of only 876 words, but three Hophal tokens — mostly in the covenant-lawsuit passages where sacrificial and priestly concepts appear.

4. **1 Chronicles is the lowest non-zero book (0.28/1k).** Chronicles tends to narrate the same events as Samuel–Kings but through a priestly, genealogical lens; it favors direct narration over passive constructions.

5. **Eight books have zero attested Hophal** (Nehemiah, Ecclesiastes, Obadiah, Jonah, Habakkuk, Zephaniah, Haggai, plus Nahum pending indexing). These tend to be shorter books with wisdom, prophetic, or narrative genres that do not require the causative-passive frame.

6. **OT average: 1.34 per 1,000 words.** As a rough benchmark: any book above ~2/1k is genuinely Hophal-rich; below ~0.5/1k is essentially Hophal-sparse.

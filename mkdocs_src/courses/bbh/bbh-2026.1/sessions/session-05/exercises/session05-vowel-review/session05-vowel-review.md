## Notes

### The Qamets / Pathach / Qamets Hatuf Distinction (items 1–3)

This is the most important contextual judgment in reading Hebrew vowels:

- **Qamets** (בָּ, item 1) and **Qamets Hatuf** (בָּ, item 3) are graphically identical — the same T-bar sign. To tell them apart you need two pieces of information: (1) the syllable type (open vs. closed) and (2) the accent. Qamets is a long A-class vowel found in open or accented syllables. Qamets Hatuf is a short O-class vowel found only in closed, unaccented syllables, and it often occurs before a hateph qamets.
- **Pathach** (בַּ, item 2) is visually similar — a plain horizontal bar — but shorter in appearance than the T-bar and always short A-class.

### Class and Quantity System

| Class | Long | Short | Reduced (hateph) |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Qamets | Pathach | Hateph Pathach |
| E | Tsere, Tsere Yod | Seghol | Hateph Seghol |
| I | Hireq Yod | Hireq | — |
| O | Holem, Holem Waw | Qamets Hatuf | Hateph Qamets |
| U | Shureq | Qibbuts | — |
| Reduced | Vocal Shewa | — | — |

Reduced vowels (vocal shewa and the three hateph shewas) do not receive full syllable weight. They are used under gutturals (for hateph forms) and at the beginning of words (vocal shewa).

# BBH Chapter 16 — Qal Imperfect Weak Verbs

*Source: Pratico & Van Pelt, Basics of Biblical Hebrew Grammar (BBH), Chapter 16*

---

## 16.1 Introduction (§16.1)

Compared to weak verbs in the Perfect, the spelling of weak Imperfect verbs exhibits far more variation and irregularity. The addition of both preformatives and sufformatives to weak verbal roots increases the likelihood of spelling variation. Regardless of a verb's weakness, however, the preformative consonants and the sufformatives of the Imperfect conjugation do not change. For this reason, it is necessary to have first memorized the Imperfect forms of the strong verb before working through this chapter. In this chapter, you will not be required to memorize weak verb paradigms. Rather, you must study and understand the types of changes that occur in each of the various weak verb classes.

---

## 16.2 I-Guttural Verbs (§16.2)

There are three types of I-Guttural weak verbs in the Imperfect, each classified by its preformative vowel:
- **Type 1** — Seghol preformative vowel (יֶחֱזַק)
- **Type 2** — Pathach preformative vowel (יַעֲמֹד)
- **Type 3** — Holem preformative vowel (יֹאמַר)

| | **I-Guttural Type 1** | **I-Guttural Type 2** | **I-Guttural Type 3** | **Strong** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **3ms** | יֶחֱזַק | יַעֲמֹד | יֹאמַר | יִקְטֹל |
| **3fs** | תֶּחֱזַק | תַּעֲמֹד | תֹּאמַר | תִּקְטֹל |
| **2ms** | תֶּחֱזַק | תַּעֲמֹד | תֹּאמַר | תִּקְטֹל |
| **2fs** | תֶּחֶזְקִי | תַּעַמְדִי | תֹּאמְרִי | תִּקְטְלִי |
| **1cs** | אֶחֱזַק | אֶעֱמֹד | אֹמַר | אֶקְטֹל |
| **3mp** | יֶחֶזְקוּ | יַעַמְדוּ | יֹאמְרוּ | יִקְטְלוּ |
| **3fp** | תֶּחֱזַ֫קְנָה | תַּעֲמֹ֫דְנָה | תֹּאמַ֫רְנָה | תִּקְטֹ֫לְנָה |
| **2mp** | תֶּחֶזְקוּ | תַּעַמְדוּ | תֹּאמְרוּ | תִּקְטְלוּ |
| **2fp** | תֶּחֱזַ֫קְנָה | תַּעֲמֹ֫דְנָה | תֹּאמַ֫רְנָה | תִּקְטֹ֫לְנָה |

## 16.3 I-Guttural Notes (§16.3)

1. **Type 1** verbs exhibit a Seghol preformative vowel followed by a Hateph Seghol under the guttural in first root position (יֶחֱזַק). In forms with a sufformative consisting of a vowel (2fs, 3mp, 2mp), the vowel under the guttural is Seghol in a closed syllable (תֶּחֶזְקוּ).
2. **Type 2** verbs exhibit a Pathach preformative vowel and a Hateph Pathach under the guttural in first root position (יַעֲמֹד). The 1cs form (אֶעֱמֹד) has Seghol under the preformative and Hateph Seghol under the guttural. In forms with a sufformative consisting of a vowel (2fs, 3mp, 2mp), the vowel under the guttural is Pathach in a closed syllable (תַּעַמְדוּ).
3. **Type 3** verbs have א for the first root letter (אָמַר). The preformative vowel is Holem and the א is quiescent (יֹאמַר). Note the unusual 1cs form אֹמַר — the א is written only once rather than twice.
4. Not all I-א verbs follow the Type 3 pattern. Several I-א verbs follow the Type 1 or Type 2 pattern, including אָסַף (to gather), אָהַב (to love), and אָסַר (to tie, bind).

## 16.4 II-Guttural, III-ע/ח, and III-א Verbs (§16.4)

Verbs in these categories differ from the strong verb primarily in the stem vowel. Strong verbs have a Holem stem vowel (יִקְטֹל), but these weak verbs prefer an a-class stem vowel in selected forms: Pathach (יִבְחַר) or Qamets (יִמְצָא).

| | **II-Guttural** | **III-ע/ח** | **III-א** | **Strong** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **3ms** | יִבְחַר | יִשְׁלַח | יִמְצָא | יִקְטֹל |
| **3fs** | תִּבְחַר | תִּשְׁלַח | תִּמְצָא | תִּקְטֹל |
| **2ms** | תִּבְחַר | תִּשְׁלַח | תִּמְצָא | תִּקְטֹל |
| **2fs** | תִּבְחֲרִי | תִּשְׁלְחִי | תִּמְצְאִי | תִּקְטְלִי |
| **1cs** | אֶבְחַר | אֶשְׁלַח | אֶמְצָא | אֶקְטֹל |
| **3mp** | יִבְחֲרוּ | יִשְׁלְחוּ | יִמְצְאוּ | יִקְטְלוּ |
| **3fp** | תִּבְחַ֫רְנָה | תִּשְׁלַ֫חְנָה | תִּמְצֶ֫אנָה | תִּקְטֹ֫לְנָה |
| **2mp** | תִּבְחֲרוּ | תִּשְׁלְחוּ | תִּמְצְאוּ | תִּקְטְלוּ |
| **2fp** | תִּבְחַ֫רְנָה | תִּשְׁלַ֫חְנָה | תִּמְצֶ֫אנָה | תִּקְטֹ֫לְנָה |

## 16.5 II-Guttural, III-ע/ח, and III-א Notes (§16.5)

1. In the **II-Guttural** class, note the Hateph Pathach under the guttural in the 2fs (תִּבְחֲרִי), 3mp (יִבְחֲרוּ), and 2mp (תִּבְחֲרוּ) forms, since the guttural in second root position cannot take the expected Vocal Shewa.
2. Like the Qal Imperfect stative verbs (15.6), **II-Guttural** and **III-ע/ח** verbs take a Pathach stem vowel because guttural consonants prefer a-type vowels. The vocalization of the Qal Imperfect stative is identical to the vocalization of the Qal Imperfect III-ע/ח verb.
3. In the **III-א** class, the stem vowel is Qamets (יִמְצָא) in forms without a sufformative. Forms with sufformatives consisting of a vowel (2fs, 3mp, 2mp) are identical to their strong verb counterparts. The 3fp and 2fp forms (תִּמְצֶ֫אנָה) exhibit an unexpected Seghol stem vowel.

## 16.6 I-י Verbs (§16.6)

There are two types of I-י verbs. With **Type 1**, the first consonant of the verbal root drops off in every form. With **Type 2**, the first consonant is preserved as a vowel letter. The verb הָלַךְ (to walk) inflects like I-י Type 1 weak verbs.

| | **I-י Type 1** | **הָלַךְ** | **I-י Type 2** | **Strong** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **3ms** | יֵשֵׁב | יֵלֵךְ | יִירַשׁ | יִקְטֹל |
| **3fs** | תֵּשֵׁב | תֵּלֵךְ | תִּירַשׁ | תִּקְטֹל |
| **2ms** | תֵּשֵׁב | תֵּלֵךְ | תִּירַשׁ | תִּקְטֹל |
| **2fs** | תֵּשְׁבִי | תֵּלְכִי | תִּירְשִׁי | תִּקְטְלִי |
| **1cs** | אֵשֵׁב | אֵלֵךְ | אִירַשׁ | אֶקְטֹל |
| **3mp** | יֵשְׁבוּ | יֵלְכוּ | יִירְשׁוּ | יִקְטְלוּ |
| **3fp** | תֵּשַׁ֫בְנָה | תֵּלַ֫כְנָה | תִּירַ֫שְׁנָה | תִּקְטֹ֫לְנָה |
| **2mp** | תֵּשְׁבוּ | תֵּלְכוּ | תִּירְשׁוּ | תִּקְטְלוּ |
| **2fp** | תֵּשַׁ֫בְנָה | תֵּלַ֫כְנָה | תִּירַ֫שְׁנָה | תִּקְטֹ֫לְנָה |
| **1cp** | נֵשֵׁב | נֵלֵךְ | נִירַשׁ | נִקְטֹל |

## 16.7 I-י Notes (§16.7)

1. In every form of the **Type 1** paradigm, the initial י root consonant has dropped off and the Imperfect preformative vowel is Tsere. Note that the י in יֵשֵׁב and יֵשְׁבוּ is the Imperfect preformative, not a root consonant. The stem vowel is Tsere in all forms without a sufformative. The **Tsere preformative vowel** is diagnostic of I-י Type 1 verbs.
2. The verb **הָלַךְ** inflects like Type 1 with loss of the initial root consonant.
3. In the **Type 2** paradigm, the initial י is seemingly preserved as the vowel letter **Hireq Yod** (יִירַשׁ). The stem vowel is Pathach except in forms with a sufformative consisting of a vowel. The **Hireq Yod preformative vowel** is diagnostic of this class.

## 16.8 I-נ Verbs (§16.8)

With most I-נ verbs, the initial root consonant (נ) assimilates into the second consonant of the verbal root and is represented by a Daghesh Forte (יִפֹּל). The verb לָקַח (to take) also inflects like a I-נ verb. An additional doubly weak verb נָחַל (I-נ and II-Guttural) is included for study.

| | **I-נ** | **לָקַח** | **Doubly Weak (נָחַל)** | **Strong** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **3ms** | יִפֹּל | יִקַּח | יִנְחַל | יִקְטֹל |
| **3fs** | תִּפֹּל | תִּקַּח | תִּנְחַל | תִּקְטֹל |
| **2ms** | תִּפֹּל | תִּקַּח | תִּנְחַל | תִּקְטֹל |
| **2fs** | תִּפְּלִי | תִּקְחִי | תִּנְחֲלִי | תִּקְטְלִי |
| **1cs** | אֶפֹּל | אֶקַּח | אֶנְחַל | אֶקְטֹל |
| **3mp** | יִפְּלוּ | יִקְחוּ | יִנְחֲלוּ | יִקְטְלוּ |
| **3fp** | תִּפֹּ֫לְנָה | תִּקַּ֫חְנָה | תִּנְחַ֫לְנָה | תִּקְטֹ֫לְנָה |
| **2mp** | תִּפְּלוּ | תִּקְחוּ | תִּנְחֲלוּ | תִּקְטְלוּ |
| **2fp** | תִּפֹּ֫לְנָה | תִּקַּ֫חְנָה | תִּנְחַ֫לְנָה | תִּקְטֹ֫לְנָה |
| **1cp** | נִפֹּל | נִקַּח | נִנְחַל | נִקְטֹל |

---

## 16.9 I-נ Notes (§16.9)

1. With **I-נ verbs**, the initial root consonant (נ) has assimilated into the second consonant of the verbal root as Daghesh Forte. In other words, יִנְפֹּל has become יִפֹּל. The presence of this Daghesh Forte enables identification of the I-נ verbal root.
2. The verb **לָקַח** inflects like I-נ with assimilation of the first root consonant (ל) into the second root consonant (ק) as Daghesh Forte (יִקַּח). The Daghesh Forte is given up in the 2fs, 3mp, and 2mp forms due to the sqnmlwy rule (5.5).
3. In the doubly weak paradigm (I-נ and II-Guttural), the נ does not assimilate into the second root consonant because the guttural rejects the assimilation (it cannot take a Daghesh Forte).

## 16.11 III-ה Notes (§16.11)

1. Imperfect forms without a sufformative have an **ֶה ending**. The final ה in these forms is not the original third root consonant but a vowel letter. Even in the derived stems, the ֶה ending is diagnostic of most III-ה Imperfect forms.
2. Imperfect forms with a sufformative do not take the ֶה ending (יִבְנוּ). Such a form exhibits only two of the three original root consonants.
3. In the 3fp and 2fp forms (תִּבְנֶ֫ינָה), the stem vowel is **Seghol Yod**. This long stem vowel parallels the Hireq Yod stem vowel found in the Perfect conjugation of this weak verb class (compare the Perfect 2ms form בָּנִ֫יתָ).

## 16.12 Geminate Verbs (§16.12)

A Geminate verb is one in which the second and third root consonants are identical, as in סָבַב (to go around). Three different patterns of inflection occur, distinguished by their preformative vowel:
- **Type 1** — Qamets preformative vowel (יָסֹב)
- **Type 2** — Hireq preformative vowel (יִסֹּב)
- **Type 3** — Tsere preformative vowel (יֵתַם)

| | **Geminate Type 1** | **Geminate Type 2** | **Geminate Type 3** | **Strong** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **3ms** | יָסֹב | יִסֹּב | יֵתַם | יִקְטֹל |
| **3fs** | תָּסֹב | תִּסֹּב | תֵּתַם | תִּקְטֹל |
| **2ms** | תָּסֹב | תִּסֹּב | תֵּתַם | תִּקְטֹל |
| **2fs** | תָּסֹ֫בִּי | תִּסֹּ֫בִּי | תֵּתַ֫מִּי | תִּקְטְלִי |
| **1cs** | אָסֹב | אֶסֹּב | אֵתַם | אֶקְטֹל |
| **3mp** | יָסֹ֫בּוּ | יִסֹּ֫בּוּ | יֵתַ֫מּוּ | יִקְטְלוּ |
| **3fp** | תְּסֻבֶּ֫ינָה | תְּסֻבֶּ֫ינָה | תְּתַמֶּ֫ינָה | תִּקְטֹ֫לְנָה |
| **2mp** | תָּסֹ֫בּוּ | תִּסֹּ֫בּוּ | תֵּתַ֫מּוּ | תִּקְטְלוּ |
| **2fp** | תְּסֻבֶּ֫ינָה | תְּסֻבֶּ֫ינָה | תְּתַמֶּ֫ינָה | תִּקְטֹ֫לְנָה |

## 16.13 Geminate Notes (§16.13)

1. The three Geminate types are distinguished by their preformative vowel in most forms: Type 1 (Qamets יָסֹב), Type 2 (Hireq יִסֹּב, with an unexpected Daghesh Forte in the first root consonant), and Type 3 (Tsere יֵתַם).
2. In every form of the Geminate paradigms, only one of the Geminate consonants remains. In forms with a sufformative, a Daghesh Forte in the remaining Geminate consonant aids identification. In forms without a sufformative, there is no Daghesh Forte.

## 16.15 Doubly Weak Notes (§16.15)

1. In the three III-ה paradigms (עָשָׂה, רָאָה, and הָיָה), note the ֶה ending except in forms with sufformatives. The ֶה ending is diagnostic of III-ה Imperfect verbs in both the Qal and derived stems.
2. The verb **עָשָׂה** is both III-ה and I-Guttural. Note the Pathach under the preformative and Hateph Pathach under the guttural in first root position (Type 2 I-Guttural pattern). The Seghol/Hateph Seghol pattern is preserved in the 1cs form (אֶעֱשֶׂה).
3. Study the Imperfect forms of **הָיָה** carefully. This doubly weak verb inflects exactly like the Imperfect of בָּנָה (16.10), though forms like תִּהְיִי (2fs), יִהְיוּ (3mp), and תִּהְיֶ֫ינָה (3fp/2fp) may appear irregular due to the י in second root position.
4. In the Imperfect of **נָשָׂא**, most forms preserve a Daghesh Forte in the second consonant (יִשָּׂא), representing the assimilated נ. The Daghesh Forte is given up in the 2fs, 3mp, and 2mp forms (sqnmlwy rule, 5.5).

## 16.16 Reconstructing Verbal Roots (§16.16)

With the Imperfect, four triconsonantal weak verb classes appear with only two root letters: I-י, I-נ, III-ה, and Geminate. Memorize the clues that distinguish each class:

1. **I-י Type 1** — the initial root consonant drops off; diagnostic: Tsere preformative vowel (יֵשֵׁב).
2. **I-נ (and לָקַח)** — the נ (or ל) assimilates into the second root consonant; diagnostic: Daghesh Forte in second root position (יִפֹּל).
3. **III-ה** — the final ה root consonant is lost; in forms without a sufformative, the vowel letter ה remains (יִבְנֶה). In forms with a sufformative (יִבְנוּ), use process of elimination: no Daghesh Forte rules out I-נ; non-Tsere preformative rules out I-י; and Geminate is less likely (only 163 occurrences vs. 3,500+ for III-ה).
4. **Geminate** — only one Geminate consonant remains; a Daghesh Forte in the remaining consonant (יָסֹ֫בּוּ) identifies the class; otherwise use process of elimination.

## 16.18 Biconsonantal Notes (§16.18)

1. In all forms (except 3fp and 2fp) and in every class, the Imperfect preformative vowel is Qamets.
2. The stem vowel of every form is determined by the Biconsonantal sub-class: Shureq (יָקוּם), Hireq Yod (יָשִׂים), or Holem Waw (יָבוֹא).
3. Some mixing of the diagnostic stem vowel does occur (e.g., יָשִׂים is sometimes spelled יָשׂוּם in Exod 4:11). The stem vowel may also be written defectively (e.g., יָקוּם may appear as יָקֻם in Gen 27:31).

## 16.19 Summary (§16.19)

1. In this chapter, no weak verb paradigms are required to be memorized. Rather, you must study and understand the types of changes that occur in each weak verb class.
2. Four triconsonantal weak verb classes appear with only two root letters in the Qal Imperfect (I-י, I-נ, III-ה, and Geminate).

4. **I-נ verbs** assimilate the נ into the second root consonant; the resulting Daghesh Forte identifies the missing consonant. The verb לָקַח also inflects like I-נ with assimilation of the ל.
5. **III-ה verbs** drop their final root consonant; identifiable by the distinctive ֶה ending in forms without a sufformative.
6. **Geminate verbs** retain only one Geminate consonant; in some forms identifiable by a Daghesh Forte in the remaining consonant; in others by a Qamets or Tsere preformative vowel.

## 16.22 Something You Should Consider: An Address to the Clergy (§16.22)

> "Do I understand Greek and Hebrew? Otherwise, how can I undertake, as every Minister does, not only to explain books which are written therein but to defend them against all opponents? Am I not at the mercy of everyone who does understand, or even pretends to understand, the original? For which way can I confute his pretense? Do I understand the language of the Old Testament? critically? at all? Can I read into English one of David's Psalms, or even the first chapter of Genesis? Do I understand the language of the New Testament? Am I a critical master of it? Have I enough of it even to read into English the first chapter of St. Luke? If not, how many years did I spend at school? How many at the University? And what was I doing all those years? Ought not shame to cover my face?"
>
> "The main point is, with all and above all, study the Greek and Hebrew Bible, and the love of Christ."
>
> John Wesley

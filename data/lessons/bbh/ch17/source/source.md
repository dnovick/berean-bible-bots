# BBH Chapter 17 — Waw Consecutive

*Source: Pratico & Van Pelt, Basics of Biblical Hebrew Grammar (BBH), Chapter 17*

---

## 17.1 Introduction (§17.1)

For the Qal stem, you have now completed the study of the Perfect and Imperfect verbal conjugations. You know that the Perfect conjugation may be used to express a completed action and is usually translated by the English past tense (12.11.1; 13.3). You also know that the Imperfect conjugation may be used to express incomplete action and is usually translated by the English future or present tense (12.11.2; 15.2).

In this chapter, you will study a special and complementary use of the Perfect and Imperfect conjugations. When used in the context of biblical narrative (and sometimes in poetry), an Imperfect verb may be prefixed with a distinctive form of the conjunction וְ and translated with the values of the Perfect. This special form of the conjunction is called Waw Consecutive. When prefixed with Waw Consecutive, the Imperfect verb may be called a "consecutive" Imperfect. Similarly, when a Waw Consecutive is prefixed to a Perfect verb, it may be called a "consecutive" Perfect.

The Waw Consecutive and the consecutive verbal forms are used primarily in narrative sequences to denote consecutive actions, that is, actions occurring in sequence.

---

## 17.2 Form and Translation of the Consecutive Imperfect (§17.2)

When an Imperfect verb is prefixed with Waw Consecutive, it will be translated with the values of a Perfect verb:

| | Qal Imperfect | Qal Imperfect with Waw Consecutive |
|---|---|---|
| | יִשְׁמֹר | וַיִּשְׁמֹר |
| | he will observe | and he observed |
| | יִזְכֹּר | וַיִּזְכֹּר |
| | he will remember | and he remembered |

The consecutive Imperfect is commonly translated with the English past tense (he killed) or present perfect tense (he has killed). With the Imperfect, the Waw Consecutive is spelled with the conjunction ו with Pathach and a Daghesh Forte in the Imperfect preformative:

**Imperfect with Waw Consecutive:** וַיִּקְטֹל

The spelling of the Waw Consecutive with the Imperfect should remind you of the definite article (הַ ּ). Full Qal Imperfect paradigm of the strong verb with Waw Consecutive:

| | Imperfect with Waw Consecutive | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| **3ms** | וַיִּקְטֹל | and he killed |
| **3fs** | וַתִּקְטֹל | and she killed |
| **2ms** | וַתִּקְטֹל | and you killed |
| **2fs** | וַתִּקְטְלִי | and you killed |
| **1cs** | וָאֶקְטֹל | and I killed |
| **3mp** | וַיִּקְטְלוּ | and they killed |
| **3fp** | וַתִּקְטֹ֫לְנָה | and they killed |
| **2mp** | וַתִּקְטְלוּ | and you killed |
| **2fp** | וַתִּקְטֹ֫לְנָה | and you killed |
| **1cp** | וַנִּקְטֹל | and we killed |

**Notes:**

1. With the prefixing of the Waw Consecutive, the spelling of the Imperfect verb remains the same. Each form is Imperfect in form but Perfect in translation value.
2. Note the spelling of the Waw Consecutive in the 1cs וָאֶקְטֹל (and I killed). Because the א preformative is a guttural, it rejects the Daghesh Forte and the Pathach under the Waw becomes Qamets as a result of compensatory lengthening.
3. Imperfect verbs may also appear with the regular conjunction וְ as in וְיִכְתֹּב (and he will write). With the regular conjunction, the Imperfect retains its normal translation values. Compare below:

| | Waw Consecutive | | Regular Conjunction | |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **3ms** | וַיִּקְטֹל | and he killed | וְיִקְטֹל | and he will kill |
| **3fs** | וַתִּקְטֹל | and she killed | וְתִקְטֹל | and she will kill |
| **2ms** | וַתִּקְטֹל | and you killed | וְתִקְטֹל | and you will kill |
| **2fs** | וַתִּקְטְלִי | and you killed | וְתִקְטְלִי | and you will kill |
| **1cs** | וָאֶקְטֹל | and I killed | וְאֶקְטֹל | and I will kill |
| **3mp** | וַיִּקְטְלוּ | and they killed | וְיִקְטְלוּ | and they will kill |
| **3fp** | וַתִּקְטֹ֫לְנָה | and they killed | וְתִקְטֹ֫לְנָה | and they will kill |
| **2mp** | וַתִּקְטְלוּ | and you killed | וְתִקְטְלוּ | and you will kill |
| **2fp** | וַתִּקְטֹ֫לְנָה | and you killed | וְתִקְטֹ֫לְנָה | and you will kill |
| **1cp** | וַנִּקְטֹל | and we killed | וְנִקְטֹל | and we will kill |

## 17.3 Changes in the Spelling of the Consecutive Imperfect Verb (§17.3)

In most verb classes, the prefixing of the Waw Consecutive does not change the spelling of the Imperfect verb itself. Some weak verbs, however, do experience minor changes.

1. With I-א verbs like אָמַר and certain I-י verbs (including הָלַךְ, which behaves like a I-י verb), the stem vowel normally changes to Seghol:

| | Imperfect | | Consecutive Imperfect | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| | יֹאמַר | ➣ | וַיֹּ֫אמֶר | and he said |
| | יֵשֵׁב | ➣ | וַיֵּ֫שֶׁב | and he dwelt |
| | יֵלֵךְ | ➣ | וַיֵּ֫לֶךְ | and he went |

2. Biconsonantal and III-ה verbs are usually "shortened" in those Imperfect forms without a sufformative. In Biconsonantal verbs, the medial vowel letter is lost; in III-ה verbs, the final ֶה drops off:

| | Imperfect | | Consecutive Imperfect | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| | יָקוּם | ➣ | וַיָּ֫קָם | and he rose up |
| | יָשִׂים | ➣ | וַיָּ֫שֶׂם | and he set |
| | יִבְנֶה | ➣ | וַיִּ֫בֶן | and he built |
| | יִגְלֶה | ➣ | וַיִּ֫גֶל | and he revealed |

## 17.4 The Use of the Consecutive Imperfect (§17.4)

In the context of Hebrew narrative, the consecutive Imperfect is normally used for the past tense narrative sequence. A past tense narrative sequence may begin in the following ways:

1. Frequently, a past tense narrative sequence begins with a Perfect verb followed by any number of consecutive Imperfect verbs. The Perfect verb signals the past tense narration.

> וְהָאָדָם יָדַע אֶת־חַוָּה אִשְׁתּוֹ וַתַּהַר וַתֵּלֶד אֶת־קַיִן
>
> And Adam knew Eve his wife, and then she conceived, and then she bore Cain (Gen 4:1).

> וּמֹשֶׁה עָלָה אֶל־הָאֱלֹהִים וַיִּקְרָא אֵלָיו יְהוָה מִן־הָהָר
>
> And Moses went up to God, and then Yahweh called to him from the mountain (Exod 19:3).

2. It is not uncommon for a past tense narrative sequence to begin with the consecutive Imperfect (and not with a Perfect).

> וַיִּקְרָא אֶל־מֹשֶׁה וַיְדַבֵּר יְהוָה אֵלָיו מֵאֹהֶל מוֹעֵד
>
> And he called to Moses and Yahweh spoke to him from the tent of meeting (Lev 1:1).

> וַיִּפְשַׁע מוֹאָב בְּיִשְׂרָאֵל אַחֲרֵי מוֹת אַחְאָב
>
> Moab rebelled against Israel after the death of Ahab (2 Kgs 1:1).

3. A past tense narrative sequence may begin with the temporal modifier וַיְהִי (Qal Imperfect 3ms of הָיָה with Waw Consecutive) followed by Imperfect verbs with Waw Consecutive.

> וַיְהִי אַחֲרֵי מוֹת אַבְרָהָם וַיְבָרֶךְ אֱלֹהִים אֶת־יִצְחָק בְּנוֹ
>
> And after the death of Abraham God blessed Isaac his son (Gen 25:11).

4. Occasionally, the sequence of consecutive Imperfect verbs may be interrupted by a Perfect in the place of an expected Imperfect with Waw Consecutive. This can happen when a negative clause appears in the sequence — the negated verb appears as a Perfect following the negative particle (often with וְלֹא). After the interruption, the sequence may return to consecutive Imperfect verbs.

> וַיֶּחֱזַק לֵב־פַּרְעֹה וְלֹא־שָׁמַע אֲלֵהֶם כַּאֲשֶׁר דִּבֶּר יְהוָה וַיִּפֶן פַּרְעֹה וַיָּבֹא אֶל־בֵּיתוֹ
>
> And Pharaoh's heart became hard and he did not listen to them just as Yahweh had said. And Pharaoh turned and went to his house (Exod 7:22–23).

## 17.5 Form and Translation of the Consecutive Perfect (§17.5)

When a Perfect verb is prefixed with Waw Consecutive, it will be translated with the values of the Imperfect:

| | Qal Perfect | Qal Perfect with Waw Consecutive |
|---|---|---|
| | שָׁמַר | וְשָׁמַר |
| | he observed | and he will observe |
| | זָכַר | וְזָכַר |
| | he remembered | and he will remember |

Note that the Waw Consecutive on the Perfect is spelled differently than on the Imperfect:

| | Form |
|---|---|
| **Perfect with Waw Consecutive** | וְקָטַל |
| **Imperfect with Waw Consecutive** | וַיִּקְטֹל |

With the Perfect, there is no spelling difference between the Waw Consecutive and the regular conjunction וְ. Full paradigm of the Perfect with Waw Consecutive:

| | Perfect with Waw Consecutive | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| **3ms** | וְקָטַל | and he will kill |
| **3fs** | וְקָֽטְלָה | and she will kill |
| **2ms** | וְקָטַלְתָּ֫ | and you will kill |
| **2fs** | וְקָטַלְתְּ | and you will kill |
| **1cs** | וְקָטַלְתִּ֫י | and I will kill |
| **3cp** | וְקָֽטְלוּ | and they will kill |
| **2mp** | וּקְטַלְתֶּם | and you will kill |
| **2fp** | וּקְטַלְתֶּן | and you will kill |
| **1cp** | וְקָטַ֫לְנוּ | and we will kill |

**Notes:**

1. The spelling of the Perfect verb is not changed by the prefixing of the Waw Consecutive.
2. In the 2mp (וּקְטַלְתֶּם) and 2fp (וּקְטַלְתֶּן), the Waw Consecutive is spelled as Shureq (וּ) because it occurs before a consonant with Vocal Shewa (§5.7).
3. In the Perfect with Waw Consecutive, the accent normally shifts to the final syllable in the 2ms and 1cs forms. For example, קָטַ֫לְתִּי (I killed) is accented on the second syllable, but וְקָטַלְתִּ֫י (and I will kill) is usually accented on the final syllable. This accent shift does not occur with the regular conjunction: וְקָטַ֫לְתִּי (and I killed). The shift can help distinguish Waw Consecutive from the regular conjunction in the 2ms and 1cs forms. Gen 9:15 reads וְזָכַרְתִּ֫י אֶת־בְּרִיתִי — because the accent falls on the final syllable, this is Waw Consecutive: "I will remember my covenant."

## 17.6 The Use of the Consecutive Perfect (§17.6)

The consecutive Perfect is used for the future tense narrative sequence (prophetic revelations, legal instruction).

1. Normally, the future tense narrative sequence begins with an Imperfect verb followed by any number of consecutive Perfect verbs. The Imperfect signals the future tense narration.

> שֵׁשֶׁת יָמִים תַּעֲבֹד וְעָשִׂיתָ כָּל־מְלַאכְתֶּךָ
>
> Six days you will labor and (you will) do all your work (Deut 5:13).

> לְמַעַן תִּזְכְּרוּ וַעֲשִׂיתֶם אֶת־כָּל־מִצְוֹתָי וִהְיִיתֶם קְדֹשִׁים לֵאלֹהֵיכֶם
>
> In order that you may remember and (you may) do all my commandments and (you may) be holy to your God (Num 15:40).

2. A future tense narrative sequence may begin with the temporal modifier וְהָיָה (Qal Perfect 3ms of הָיָה with Waw Consecutive) followed by Perfect verbs with Waw Consecutive.

> אֶת־קֶשֶׁת יִשְׂרָאֵל וְשָׁבַרְתִּי בַּיּוֹם הַהוּא וְהָיָה
>
> And in that day I will break the bow of Israel (Hos 1:5).

3. The consecutive Perfect may follow an Imperative verb and be translated with the full force of the initial Imperative.

> שִׁמְעוּ דְבַר־יְהוָה וְאָמַרְתָּ שָּׁם אֶת־הַדָּבָר הַזֶּה וְקָרָאתָ בְּשַׁעַר בֵּית יְהוָה עֲמֹד
>
> Stand (Imperative) in the gate of Yahweh's house and proclaim there this word and say, "Hear the word of Yahweh" (Jer 7:2).

> אוֹתָם וַעֲשִׂיתֶם אֶת־דִּבְרֵי הַבְּרִית הַזֹּאת שִׁמְעוּ
>
> Hear (Imperative) the words of this covenant and do them (Jer 11:6).

4. Infrequently, the Perfect may appear with the regular conjunction וְ rather than with Waw Consecutive. In these instances, translate with "and" plus the past (or present) tense.

    a. The Perfect with the regular conjunction may occur in a short sequence of Perfect verbs. The Perfect verbs that follow carry the temporal value of the first Perfect.

> מִשְׁפָּט אָכַל וְשָׁתָה וְעָשָׂה אָבִיךָ הֲלוֹא
>
> As for your father, did he not eat and drink and do justice? (Jer 22:15)

> זָקַנְתִּי וָשַׂבְתִּי וַאֲנִי
>
> But as for me, I am old and I am gray (1 Sam 12:2).

    b. The Perfect with the regular conjunction may occur in a series of syntactically parallel clauses where both clauses contain a Perfect verb.

> עֲוֹנֶךָ וְסָר זֶה עַל־שְׂפָתֶיךָ נָגַע הִנְּה
>
> Behold, he touched this upon your lips and he removed your iniquity (Isa 6:7).

> מִיַּד חָזָק מִמֶּנּוּ וּגְאָלוֹ יְהוָה אֶת־יַעֲקֹב כִּי־פָדָה
>
> For Yahweh ransomed Jacob and he redeemed him from a hand of one stronger than he (Jer 31:11).

## 17.7 Translating Temporal Modifiers (§17.7)

Because of their frequency in narrative, the temporal modifiers וַיְהִי and וְהָיָה are best not translated (though the conjunction may still be translated as "and"). Some translate וַיְהִי as "and it came to pass that" and וְהָיָה as "and it will be that," but in the interest of good English style it is preferable to avoid these translations.

## 17.8 Parsing (§17.8)

The parsing of consecutive verbal forms is just like the parsing of regular Perfect or Imperfect verbs, except that the presence of the Waw Consecutive must be identified:

| Form | Stem | Conjugation | PGN | Root | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| וַיִּשְׁמֹר | Qal | Imperfect | 3ms | שָׁמַר | with Waw Consecutive |
| וְזָכַר | Qal | Perfect | 3ms | זָכַר | with Waw Consecutive |

## 17.9 Summary (§17.9)

1. Perfect and Imperfect verbs with Waw Consecutive are used primarily in narrative sequences to denote consecutive actions occurring in logical or temporal sequence.
2. When an Imperfect verb is prefixed with Waw Consecutive, it is translated with the values of the Perfect. The Waw Consecutive on the Imperfect consists of ו with Pathach and Daghesh Forte in the preformative (וַיִּקְטֹל); in the 1cs, compensatory lengthening produces וָאֶקְטֹל.
3. Some weak verbs experience minor spelling changes: I-א verbs and certain I-י verbs (and הָלַךְ) change the stem vowel to Seghol; Biconsonantal and III-ה consecutive verbs are usually "shortened" in forms without a sufformative.
4. The consecutive Imperfect is commonly used for the past tense narrative sequence, beginning with a Perfect verb followed by any number of consecutive Imperfects.
5. When a Perfect verb is prefixed with Waw Consecutive, it is translated with the values of the Imperfect. The Waw Consecutive on the Perfect is spelled like the regular conjunction וְ (וְקָטַל).
6. The consecutive Perfect is commonly used for the future tense narrative sequence, beginning with an Imperfect verb followed by any number of consecutive Perfects.
7. Infrequently, the Perfect may appear with the regular conjunction וְ (not Waw Consecutive), translated with "and" plus the past tense. This occurs: (1) in a short sequence of Perfect verbs; (2) in syntactically parallel clauses with Perfect verbs.
8. A past tense narrative sequence may begin with the temporal modifier וַיְהִי (Qal Imperfect 3ms of הָיָה with Waw Consecutive); a future tense narrative sequence may begin with the temporal modifier וְהָיָה (Qal Perfect 3ms of הָיָה with Waw Consecutive).

## 17.11 Additional Information: Waw Consecutive (§17.11)

Hebrew grammars use a number of different names for Perfect and Imperfect verbs prefixed with the Waw Consecutive:

1. **Waw Consecutive.** Descriptive of the function — this special form of the conjunction marks consecutive actions in logical or temporal sequence. This is the terminology employed in this grammar.
2. **Waw Conversive.** Descriptive of the apparent "conversion" of verbal values — the Imperfect takes Perfect values, and the Perfect takes Imperfect values. Forms used with it are often called "converted" verbal forms.
3. **Waw Relative.** Descriptive of how the conjunction "relates" the verb to a previous verb, especially in terms of temporal succession.
4. **Wayyiqtol and Weqatal.** English transliterations of וַיִּקְטֹל (wayyiqtol) and וְקָטַל (weqatal). Descriptive of the verbal construction without reference to translation value or function.
5. **Preterite.** A grammatical term describing a verb that refers to a simple action in the past — the primary value of the Imperfect with Waw Consecutive. The term "preterite" does not apply to the consecutive Perfect (weqatal).

## 17.12 Something You Should Know: A Word Is Worth a Thousand Pictures (§17.12)

> One of the great benefits of learning to read biblical Hebrew is that you will be able to do "word studies" in the original language in order to determine precisely what a given term means in a particular passage. Although many wonderful English translations are available, doing your own word studies on key terms in a passage often provides you with tremendous insight into a text's meaning that cannot be gained from the English alone. In the context of Ps 137:1–9, let's look at verses 5–6 as an example.
>
> If I forget you, O Jerusalem, may my right hand wither.
> May my tongue cling to the roof of my mouth,
> if I do not remember you,
> if I do not exalt Jerusalem above my chief joy.
>
> What does it mean to "forget" Jerusalem? Why do many English translations render verse 5b as "may my right hand forget its skill" and why does the author refer to malfunctioning body parts in verses 5–6? A word study on שָׁכַח (to forget) reveals that while this term can mean simply a temporary or permanent lapse of memory, it can also refer to abandonment. Biblically, to forget God is to abandon him (Isa 49:14), to pervert one's ways (Jer 3:21), and to commit idolatry (Deut 4:23; Judg 3:7; Ps 44:17). In essence, to forget God is to break the covenant (see Deut 8:2). This is precisely what is meant in verse 5. To forget Jerusalem would be to assimilate into Babylonian culture and adopt her pagan religion.
>
> Second, why do many English translations render verse 5b as "may my right hand forget its skill" rather than "may my right hand wither"? The text of verse 5b reads תִּשְׁכַּח יְמִינִי with the verb שָׁכַח (to forget). Some argue that a scribal mistake known as "graphic confusion" occurred, where כָּחַשׁ (to grow lean, wither) was mistaken for שָׁכַח, and that the original text actually read תִּכְחַשׁ יְמִינִי ("may my right hand wither"). The Greek version of the Old Testament, the Septuagint, also attests to the confusion by rendering the active Hebrew verb with a passive Greek verb.
>
> One possible interpretation, followed here, is based on the context of Ps 137:5–6 and the author's reference to a malfunctioning tongue as a consequence of "forgetting" Jerusalem. A word study on body parts in the Old Testament demonstrates that mute tongues, dim eyes, deaf ears, and withering hands are often linked to idol worship. This is the message of Ps 115:4–8:
>
> Their idols are silver and gold, the work of man's hands.
> They have mouths, but they cannot speak; they have eyes, but they cannot see;
> they have ears, but they cannot hear; they have noses, but they cannot smell;
> they have hands, but they cannot feel; they have feet, but they cannot walk;
> they cannot make a sound with their throat.
> Those who make them will become like them, everyone who trusts in them. (NASB)
>
> The operative principle is that you are what you worship! Thus "may my right hand wither" in Ps 137:5 better fits the context of idolatry and stands in parallel with "may my tongue cling to the roof of my mouth." The psalmist is saying, "may I become like an immobile and mute idol if I worship one ever again!"

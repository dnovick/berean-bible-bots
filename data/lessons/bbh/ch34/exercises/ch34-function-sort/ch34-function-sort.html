Classify each Hithpael verb as IR (Intensive-Reflexive), RC (Reciprocal), IT (Iterative), or SA (Simple Action) — the four Hithpael functions from §34.2. Type your answer in the Function column, then click ▶ Answer to check.

CodeFunctionDiagnostic question
IRIntensive-ReflexiveIs the subject doing the action to or for itself? (Subject = object) — the most common Hithpael function.
RCReciprocalAre multiple subjects acting on one another mutually?
ITIterativeIs the action repeated or habitual?
SASimple ActionIs the Hithpael translated like a plain Qal simple action, with no reflexive or reciprocal force?
Tip: Subject acts on itself → Intensive-Reflexive. Subjects act on one another → Reciprocal. Action repeated/habitual → Iterative. No reflexive/reciprocal/repetitive force — reads like a Qal → Simple Action.

Discussion Questions

  1. The Hithpael of הָלַךְ appears five times (items 1, 3, 6, 8, 13). Why is this classified as Iterative rather than Intensive-Reflexive? What does the "walking about" context add that a single walk would not?
  2. Item 12 (הִתְבָּרֲכוּ, Gen 22:18): this form can be translated "bless themselves" (Intensive-Reflexive) or "be blessed" (passive-like). The same tension appears in Gen 12:3. What grammatical evidence would help decide between these interpretations?
  3. Items 15 (הִתְעַשְּׂקוּ, quarreled) and 17 (וַיִּתְנַכְּלוּ, plotted): both involve conflict between parties. What makes one Reciprocal and the other Intensive-Reflexive rather than Reciprocal? Does the context of Gen 26:20 (mutual quarrel between two herdsmen) vs. Gen 37:18 (the brothers acting together against Joseph, not against one another) help?
  4. Items 23 and 25 both come from חָזַק, and item 24 from הָלַל. In what way does the Hithpael's reflexive coloring ("strengthen YOURSELF," "praise/exalt ONESELF") add nuance beyond a plain Qal or Piel form of the same root? Why might Joshua 1:6 reach for the Hithpael imperative rather than a simple command?

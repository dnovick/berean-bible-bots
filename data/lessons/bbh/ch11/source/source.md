# BBH Chapter 11 — Hebrew Numbers

*Source: Pratico & Van Pelt, Basics of Biblical Hebrew Grammar (BBH), Chapter 11*

---

## 11.1 Introduction (§11.1)

Biblical Hebrew does not have separate numerical symbols (1, 2, 3, etc.). Numbers are written out in full. Hebrew has both **cardinal** numbers (counting: one, two, three) and **ordinal** numbers (position: first, second, third).

---

## 11.2 Cardinal Numbers: One through Ten (§11.2)

Hebrew cardinal numbers have masculine and feminine forms and occur in both absolute and construct states. Memorize only the **masculine absolute** forms; note the patterns of change in the other forms.

| | Masc. Absolute | Masc. Construct | Fem. Absolute | Fem. Construct |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **One** | אֶחָד | אַחַד | אַחַת | אַחַת |
| **Two** | שְׁנַ֫יִם | שְׁנֵי | שְׁתַּ֫יִם | שְׁתֵּי |
| **Three** | שָׁלֹשׁ | שְׁלֹשׁ | שְׁלֹשָׁה | שְׁלֹ֫שֶׁת |
| **Four** | אַרְבַּע | אַרְבַּע | אַרְבָּעָה | אַרְבַּ֫עַת |
| **Five** | חָמֵשׁ | חֲמֵשׁ | חֲמִשָּׁה | חֲמֵ֫שֶׁת |
| **Six** | שֵׁשׁ | שֵׁשׁ | שִׁשָּׁה | שֵׁ֫שֶׁת |
| **Seven** | שֶׁ֫בַע | שְׁבַע | שִׁבְעָה | שִׁבְעַת |
| **Eight** | שְׁמֹנֶה | שְׁמֹנֶה | שְׁמֹנָה | שְׁמֹנַת |
| **Nine** | תֵּ֫שַׁע | תְּשַׁע | תִּשְׁעָה | תִּשְׁעַת |
| **Ten** | עֶ֫שֶׂר | עֶ֫שֶׂר | עֲשָׂרָה | עֲשֶׂ֫רֶת |

### Notes

1. **Number one** — masculine spelled with ד (אֶחָד), feminine with ת (אַחַת). Functions like an **adjective**: follows the noun, agrees in gender and definiteness. In construct, precedes a plural absolute noun.

| Hebrew | Translation |
|---|---|
| בֵּן אֶחָד | one son |
| בַּת אַחַת | one daughter |
| הַבֵּן הָאֶחָד | the one son |
| אַחַד הַבָּנִים | one of the sons |
| אַחַת הַבָּנוֹת | one of the daughters |

2. **Number two** — masculine spelled with נ (שְׁנַ֫יִם), feminine with ת (שְׁתַּ֫יִם). Both forms are **dual**. Functions as a **noun**; agrees in gender with its associated noun. Absolute and construct are interchangeable.

| Hebrew | Translation |
|---|---|
| בָּנִים שְׁנַ֫יִם | two sons |
| בָּנוֹת שְׁתַּ֫יִם | two daughters |
| שְׁנֵי בָּנִים | two sons |
| שְׁתֵּי בָּנוֹת | two daughters |

3. **Numbers three through ten** — function as nouns but **do not agree in gender** with their associated nouns (masculine numbers with feminine nouns and vice versa). The nouns they modify are **plural**.

| Hebrew | Translation |
|---|---|
| שְׁלֹשָׁה בָּנִים | three sons |
| שָׁלוֹשׁ בָּנוֹת | three daughters |

4. **Numbers two through ten** may take pronominal suffixes on their construct forms.

| Hebrew | Translation |
|---|---|
| שְׁנֵיהֶם | two of them (m) |
| שְׁתֵּיהֶן | two of them (f) |
| שְׁנֵיכֶם | two of you (m) |
| שְׁנֵינוּ | two of us |

## 11.3 Cardinal Numbers: Eleven through Nineteen (§11.3)

Numbers eleven through nineteen combine the number ten with one through nine.

| | With Masculine Nouns | With Feminine Nouns |
|---|---|---|
| **Eleven** | אַחַד עָשָׂר / עַשְׁתֵּי עָשָׂר | אַחַת עֶשְׂרֵה / עַשְׁתֵּי עֶשְׂרֵה |
| **Twelve** | שְׁנֵי עָשָׂר / שְׁנֵים עָשָׂר | שְׁתֵּי עֶשְׂרֵה / שְׁתֵּים עֶשְׂרֵה |
| **Thirteen** | שְׁלֹשָׁה עָשָׂר | שְׁלֹשׁ עֶשְׂרֵה |
| **Fourteen** | אַרְבָּעָה עָשָׂר | אַרְבַּע עֶשְׂרֵה |
| **Fifteen** | חֲמִשָּׁה עָשָׂר | חֲמֵשׁ עֶשְׂרֵה |
| **Sixteen** | שִׁשָּׁה עָשָׂר | שֵׁשׁ עֶשְׂרֵה |
| **Seventeen** | שִׁבְעָה עָשָׂר | שְׁבַע עֶשְׂרֵה |
| **Eighteen** | שְׁמֹנָה עָשָׂר | שְׁמֹנֶה עֶשְׂרֵה |
| **Nineteen** | תִּשְׁעָה עָשָׂר | תְּשַׁע עֶשְׂרֵה |

### Notes

1. Eleven and twelve have alternate forms. The alternate forms for eleven (with עַשְׁתֵּי) occur only 19 times in the Hebrew Bible.
2. Most occur with plural nouns. A few nouns appear regularly in the singular with these numbers and are translated as plural.

| Hebrew | Translation |
|---|---|
| שְׁנֵי עָשָׂר אִישׁ | twelve men (Josh 3:12) |
| שְׁלֹשׁ עֶשְׂרֵה שָׁנָה | thirteen years (1 Kgs 7:1) |

## 11.4 Cardinal Numbers: Twenty through Ninety-Nine (§11.4)

| Number | Hebrew |
|---|---|
| Twenty | עֶשְׂרִים |
| Thirty | שְׁלֹשִׁים |
| Forty | אַרְבָּעִים |
| Fifty | חֲמִשִּׁים |
| Sixty | שִׁשִּׁים |
| Seventy | שִׁבְעִים |
| Eighty | שְׁמֹנִים |
| Ninety | תִּשְׁעִים |

### Notes

1. עֶשְׂרִים (twenty) is the masculine plural form of עֶשֶׂר (ten). Thirty through ninety are masculine plural forms of three through nine.
2. Compound numbers occur as "twenty and one" or less frequently as "one and twenty."

| Hebrew | Translation |
|---|---|
| עֶשְׂרִים וְאַחַת | twenty-one |
| שִׁבְעִים וְשִׁבְעָה | seventy-seven |
| תִּשְׁעִים וָתֵשַׁע | ninety-nine |
| שְׁנַיִם וּשְׁלֹשִׁים | thirty-two |

## 11.6 Ordinal Numbers (§11.6)

Ordinal numbers express position in a series. They have both masculine and feminine forms.

| | Masculine | Feminine |
|---|---|---|
| **First** | רִאשׁוֹן | רִאשׁוֹנָה |
| **Second** | שֵׁנִי | שֵׁנִית |
| **Third** | שְׁלִישִׁי | שְׁלִישִׁית |
| **Fourth** | רְבִיעִי | רְבִיעִית |
| **Fifth** | חֲמִישִׁי | חֲמִישִׁית |
| **Sixth** | שִׁשִּׁי | שִׁשִּׁית |
| **Seventh** | שְׁבִיעִי | שְׁבִיעִית |
| **Eighth** | שְׁמִינִי | שְׁמִינִית |
| **Ninth** | תְּשִׁיעִי | תְּשִׁיעִית |
| **Tenth** | עֲשִׂירִי | עֲשִׂירִית |

### Notes

1. רִאשׁוֹן (first) derives from the noun רֹאשׁ (head, beginning). The remaining ordinals share a root with their corresponding cardinal.
2. Masculine ordinals end in ִי; feminine ordinals end in ִית.
3. Ordinals are adjectives. When attributive, they follow the noun and agree in gender and definiteness.

| Hebrew | Translation |
|---|---|
| בַּיּוֹם הָרִאשׁוֹן | on the first day (Lev 23:7) |
| בַּיּוֹם הַשְּׁבִיעִי | on the seventh day (Gen 2:2) |
| יוֹם הַשִּׁשִּׁי | the sixth day (Gen 1:31) |

4. For ordinals greater than tenth, cardinal numbers are used.

---

## 11.7 Summary (§11.7)

1. Cardinal numbers are used for counting; ordinal numbers indicate position in a series.
2. Cardinal numbers have masculine and feminine forms and occur in absolute and construct states. Memorize the masculine absolute forms.
3. Cardinal number one functions like an adjective; numbers two and higher function like nouns. Numbers three through ten do not agree in gender with their associated nouns.
4. Numbers eleven through nineteen are formed by combining ten with one through nine.
5. Compound numbers (21–99) occur as "twenty and one" or "one and twenty."
6. Ordinal numbers are adjectives with masculine (ending ִי) and feminine (ending ִית) forms.

## 11.9 Something You Should Consider: Martin Luther on the Importance of the Biblical Languages (§11.9)

> And let us be sure of this: we will not long preserve the gospel without the languages. The languages are the sheath in which this sword of the Spirit is contained; they are the casket in which this jewel is enshrined; they are the vessel in which this wine is held; they are the larder in which this food is stored; and, as the gospel itself points out, they are the baskets in which are kept these loaves and fishes and fragments. If through our neglect we let the languages go (which God forbid!), we shall … lose the gospel.
>
> Experience too has proved this and still gives evidence of it. For as soon as the languages declined to the vanishing point, after the apostolic age, the gospel and faith and Christianity itself declined more and more … On the other hand, now that the languages have been revived, they are bringing with them so bright a light and accomplishing such great things that the whole world stands amazed and has to acknowledge that we have the gospel just as pure and undefiled as the apostles had it, that it has been wholly restored to its original purity, far beyond what it was in the days of St. Jerome and St. Augustine.
>
> —Martin Luther (1483–1546)

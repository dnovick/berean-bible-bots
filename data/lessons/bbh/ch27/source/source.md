# BBH Chapter 27 — Hiphil Weak Verbs

*Source: Pratico & Van Pelt, Basics of Biblical Hebrew Grammar (BBH), Chapter 27*

---

## 27.1 Introduction (§27.1)

This chapter studies weak verbs in the Hiphil stem. Before working through these paradigms, master the strong verb diagnostics:

1. **ה prefix** in all conjugations except the Imperfect and Participle
2. **Pathach** under all preformatives and prefixes — except in the Perfect, where it is **Hireq**
3. **Hireq Yod or Tsere stem vowel** in all forms — except the second and first person forms of the Perfect, where it is **Pathach**

## 27.3 I-Guttural Notes (§27.3)

1. In every conjugation except the Perfect, the strong verb diagnostics of the Hiphil stem are fully preserved.
2. The Perfect has a **Seghol prefix vowel** (הֶעֱמִיד) rather than the Hireq of the strong verb (הִקְטִיל). This same Seghol-Hateph Seghol pattern also appears in the Qal Imperfect (יֶחֱזַק) and Niphal Perfect (נֶעֱזַב) of I-Guttural verbs (§§16.2–3; 25.6–7).
3. The guttural in first root position takes a reduced vowel in every conjugation — Hateph Seghol in the Perfect, Hateph Pathach in all other conjugations.

## 27.5 III-ע/ח Notes (§27.5)

1. Most Hiphil strong verb diagnostics are preserved. Variation occurs only in the stem vowel of a few forms.
2. In the Imperfect 3fp and 2fp (תַּשְׁלַ֫חְנָה), the stem vowel is **Pathach** rather than Tsere. Gutturals prefer a-class vowels.
3. In the Imperative 2ms (הַשְׁלַח) and 2fp (הַשְׁלַ֫חְנָה), the stem vowel is also **Pathach** rather than Tsere.

## 27.7 III-א Notes (§27.7)

1. All prefix and preformative consonants and vowels follow the pattern of the Hiphil strong verb. Stem vowel variations characteristic of III-א verbs do occur.
2. In all second and first person forms of the Perfect, the stem vowel is **Tsere** (הִמְצֵ֫אתָ) rather than the Pathach of the strong verb (הִקְטַ֫לְתָּ).
3. In the Imperfect 3fp and 2fp, the stem vowel is **Seghol** (תַּמְצֶ֫אנָה) rather than the Tsere of the strong verb (תַּקְטֵ֫לְנָה). This same variation appears in the Imperative 2fp (הַמְצֶ֫אנָה).

## 27.9 III-ה Notes (§27.9)

1. All prefix and preformative consonants and vowels retain the strong verb diagnostics. Variation from the strong verb occurs mainly in the stem vowels.
2. Note the III-ה diagnostic endings that appear on all III-ה verbs in the Qal and derived stems. Review the "Diagnostic Endings for III-ה Verbs in All Stems" chart (§25.5.3).
3. In the Perfect, the Hireq Yod stem vowel may also appear as **Tsere Yod** (הִגְלֵ֫יתָ).
4. In the Imperfect, the Hireq Yod stem vowel of the Hiphil strong verb does not occur in any form; the **ֶה ending** is characteristic of Imperfect III-ה forms without a sufformative.
5. The Infinitive Construct has the **וֹת ending**, as expected in III-ה verbs.

## 27.11 I-נ Notes (§27.11)

1. All Hiphil strong verb diagnostics are preserved.
2. The challenge with I-נ Hiphil verbs is **root identification**: in every form, the נ in first root position assimilates into the second root consonant (צ) and appears as **Daghesh Forte** (צּ). Recognize the Daghesh Forte as the assimilated נ.

## 27.13 I-י Notes (§27.13)

Most I-י verbs were originally I-ו. In the Niphal, Hiphil, and Hophal stems, the original ו reappears as either a vowel letter or a consonant.

1. In every form above, the י in first root position is replaced by **Holem Waw** (וֹ). This Holem Waw follows the prefix or preformative consonant and represents the reappearance of the original ו. Knowing this identifies the I-י verb class for root reconstruction.
2. Apart from the Holem Waw, all other Hiphil strong verb diagnostics are preserved — preformative and prefix consonants are the same, and stem vowels are as expected.

## 27.15 Biconsonantal Notes (§27.15)

1. There are three Biconsonantal verb classes (קוּם, שִׂים, בּוֹא). In the Hiphil stem, all three exhibit the **Hireq Yod stem vowel** regardless of their original medial vowel. The diagnostics for קוּם therefore suffice for all three classes.
2. In the Perfect, the prefix vowel is **Tsere in third person forms** and **Hateph Pathach in second and first person forms**. A Holem Waw connecting vowel appears between the verbal root and Perfect sufformatives in all second and first person forms (הֲקִימ֫וֹתָ). This connecting vowel may also appear as Holem (הֲקִימֹתִי).
3. In the Imperfect, Imperative, Infinitive Construct, and Infinitive Absolute, the prefix and preformative vowels are **Qamets** rather than the Pathach of the strong verb. The Participle prefix vowel is variable between Tsere (ms) and Vocal Shewa.

## 27.18 Additional Information: Hiphil of יָדָה (§27.18)

The verb יָדָה (to thank, praise) is well attested in the Hiphil (100×, especially Psalms 67×). As a doubly weak verb (I-י and III-ה), certain forms merit special attention. As a I-י verb in the Hiphil, the original ו reappears as Holem Waw, replacing the י root consonant. III-ה verbs also have their own distinctive endings.

**Selected Hiphil forms of יָדָה:**

| Conjugation | Form | PGN | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Perfect | הוֹדִינוּ | 1cp | we gave thanks |
| Perfect | הוֹדוּ | 3cp | they gave thanks |
| Imperfect | אוֹדֶה | 1cs | I will give thanks |
| Imperfect | יוֹדוּ | 3mp | they will give thanks |
| Imperative | הוֹדוּ | 2mp | (you) give thanks! |
| Infinitive Construct | הוֹדוֹת | — | to give thanks |
| Participle | מוֹדֶה | ms | giving thanks |

**Biblical examples:**

## 27.19 Something You Should Know: Exodus 34 and the Character of God (§27.19)

> Moses was truly a remarkable figure in Old Testament history. Between the Nile and Nebo, he wore many mantles, but perhaps none was more important for the people of Israel than that of intercessor (Ps 106:23). It is in the context of Israel's faithlessness and Moses's intercession on behalf of God's delivered people that we encounter one of the essential texts for understanding the meaning of the name יהוה and the character behind it (Exod 34:5–7).
>
> Even God's "signs and wonders" in Egypt were not enough to ensure Israel's faithfulness in the wilderness of Sinai. While still on the sacred mountain, Moses receives this instruction from God (Exod 32:7).
>
> וַיְדַבֵּר יְהוָה אֶל־מֹשֶׁה לֶךְ־רֵד כִּי שִׁחֵת עַמְּךָ אֲשֶׁר הֶעֱלֵיתָ מֵאֶרֶץ מִצְרָיִם
> Yahweh said to Moses, "Go down because your people whom you brought up out of the land of Egypt have become corrupt."
>
> God then declares his intention to destroy this stiff-necked people for their unpardonable idolatry (32:7–10). Through the intercession of Moses, the people are spared but at a very heavy cost (Exod 32:11–14, 25–35).
>
> It is in this dark hour that we encounter one of the greatest moments of God's self-revelation in the Hebrew Bible. In Exod 34:5–6, God declares his intention to proclaim his name יהוה in the presence of Moses.
>
> וַיֵּרֶד יְהוָה בֶּעָנָן וַיִּתְיַצֵּב עִמּוֹ שָׁם וַיִּקְרָא בְשֵׁם יְהוָה וַיַּעֲבֹר יְהוָה עַל־פָּנָיו וַיִּקְרָא יְהוָה יְהוָה אֵל רַחוּם וְחַנּוּן אֶרֶךְ אַפַּיִם וְרַב־חֶסֶד וֶאֱמֶת
> Yahweh descended in the cloud and he stood with him there and he proclaimed his name יהוה. And Yahweh passed before Moses and he proclaimed, "יהוה יהוה, a God merciful and gracious, slow to anger, and abounding in steadfast love and faithfulness."
>
> With rare exception, the word רַחוּם (merciful) is used only of God and refers to his deep mercy and compassion. The word חַנּוּן (gracious) is used only with reference to God, and in most occurrences it appears in combination with רַחוּם. The construct אֶרֶךְ אַפַּיִם (literally, "long of nose") refers to God's seemingly inexhaustible patience.
>
> God's חֶסֶד (steadfast love) is a divine love that is forgiving, redeeming, and relentless in its pursuit of those to whom it is to be given. God's faithfulness (אֶמֶת) expresses the certainty and assurance that the attributes of the divine self-revelation will never fail.
>
> The response of Moses to God's self-revelation is worship (Exod 34:8). In response, יהוה promises to do wonders on their behalf, wonders never done before in any nation in all of the world (Exod 34:8–10). It is little wonder that the Lord's own revelation of his name and character would echo throughout later Old Testament history (Neh 9:17; Pss 103:8; 145:8; Joel 2:13; Jonah 4:2).
>
> The Word became flesh and dwelt among us, and we have seen his glory, glory as of the only Son from the Father, full of grace and truth. (John 1:14)
>

### III. Grammatical Commentary

**Psalm 96:1**

**שִׁירוּ** — Biconsonantal Imperatives in the Qal stem are generally easy to recognize (18.7). They normally appear with the medial vowel letter of their lexical form and the Imperative sufformatives that occur with the 2fs, 2mp, and 2fp.

**שִׁיר חָדָשׁ** — The masculine singular adjective חָדָשׁ (new) follows the masculine singular noun שִׁיר (song). Since they both agree in gender, number, and (in)definiteness, we know that this is the attributive use of the adjective (7.5.1), "a new song." The lexical forms of the Biconsonantal verb שִׁיר (to sing) and the noun שִׁיר (song) are identical.

**כָּל־הָאָרֶץ** — This construction is a vocative. The term vocative is used to indicate direct address, that is, it explicitly identifies the person to whom the speech is being addressed. In this verse, the psalmist invokes the whole earth to sing a new song.

**Psalm 96:2**

**בָּרֲכוּ** — The verbal stem of this Imperative 2mp verb is Piel. Because the guttural in second root position rejects the Daghesh Forte of the Piel stem, the expected Pathach (short a) under the preformative has lengthened to Qamets (long a). This is called compensatory lengthening (31.9–10). The verbal root ברך appears frequently in the Piel stem (233 times).

**יְשׁוּעָתוֹ** — When a feminine singular noun ending in ָה (as in יְשׁוּעָה) receives a pronominal suffix, the ה is replaced by ת with the addition of the suffix (9.7.1). In this verse, יְשׁוּעָתוֹ identifies the content of the news that is to be declared, "bring news about his salvation!"

**Psalm 96:3**

**סַפְּרוּ** — The Pathach under the first root consonant and the Daghesh Forte in the second root consonant clearly identify this Imperative 2mp verb as Piel. The use of this verb in verse 3 is a good example of verbal ellipsis in Hebrew poetry. There are two lines in this verse: סַפְּרוּ בַגּוֹיִם כְּבוֹדוֹ and בְּכָל־הָעַמִּים נִפְלְאוֹתָיו. The verb appears only in the first line, but is understood to govern the second as well, "make known his wonders among all the people."

**נִפְלְאוֹתָיו** — The נִ prefix on this form is diagnostic of the Niphal stem. The וֹת ending is the feminine plural inflectional ending of the Participle. The verbal root is פלא (to be wonderful) and this feminine plural Niphal Participle appears with a 3ms (Type 2) pronominal suffix. This verb occurs primarily in the Niphal stem (never in the Qal) and the Niphal Participle normally functions substantively, "his wonders."

**Psalm 96:4**

**כִּי** — The Imperative summons to praise Yahweh in verses 1–3 is now grounded in verses 4–5. Both verses begin with כִּי as the means by which the author introduces the reasons for praise.

**גָדוֹל יְהוָה** — In this verbless clause the predicate adjective (גָדוֹל) precedes the subject (יְהוָה). This order is common with verbless clauses of classification in biblical Hebrew, "great is Yahweh" or "Yahweh is great."

**מְהֻלָּל** — The three diagnostic features of the Pual Participle include the מְ prefix, Qibbuts under the first root consonant (הֻ), and Daghesh Forte in the second root consonant (לּ). This Participle is functioning predicatively with יְהוָה, "Yahweh is great and most worthy of praise."

**נוֹרָא הוּא** — The נ of נוֹרָא is diagnostic of the Niphal stem. The Holem Waw is diagnostic of the I-י weak verb class in the Niphal, Hiphil, and Hophal stems. This form could be either Niphal Perfect 3ms or Niphal Participle ms; the context of this verse suggests the Participle. The 3ms independent personal pronoun (הוּא) functions as the subject and the Participle functions as the predicate adjective, "he is the one who is to be feared."

**Psalm 96:5**

**אֱלִילִים** — Verse 5 begins with a verbless clause of identification: subject-predicate word order. The subject is the construct chain כָּל־אֱלֹהֵי הָעַמִּים and the predicate nominative is the masculine plural noun אֱלִילִים (idols), "all of the gods of the people are [identified] as idols." The use of this particular noun for idols may be motivated by its similarity in sound and form to אֱלֹהִים in the previous verse.

**וַיהוָה** — The word order for the final clause in verse 5 is subject-object-verb, "But Yahweh made [the] heavens." The conjunction וְ functions contrastively here, setting off the true God from the false gods of verse 5a (23.9.3).

# BBH Chapter 20 — Qal Infinitive Construct

*Source: Pratico & Van Pelt, Basics of Biblical Hebrew Grammar (BBH), Chapter 20*

---

## 20.1 Introduction (§20.1)

In Hebrew, there are two Infinitive forms, the Infinitive Construct and the Infinitive Absolute. Infinitives are verbal nouns and, as such, have features in common with both verbs and nouns. The Infinitive Construct can function much like the English Infinitive, commonly translated with the preposition "to" plus a verb as in "to study" or "to learn." In this chapter, we will study both the form and function of the Qal Infinitive Construct. In the next chapter, we will study the Qal Infinitive Absolute.

---

## 20.2 Qal Infinitive Construct of the Strong Verb (§20.2)

The Infinitive Construct form is not inflected for person, gender, or number. There is only one basic form and, therefore, no paradigm to memorize.

**Qal Infinitive Construct (Strong Verb):** קְטֹל

Note that the Infinitive Construct form of the strong verb is identical to the Qal Imperative 2ms (also קְטֹל). There are therefore two possible identifications for קְטֹל; context will likely resolve this ambiguity.

The vowel pattern for the Qal Infinitive Construct is consistent for all strong verbs and even for most weak verbs. Study the following comparison of Imperative 2ms and Infinitive Construct forms, noting especially the weak verbs:

| Type | Imperative 2ms | Infinitive Construct | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strong | כְּתֹב | כְּתֹב | to write, writing |
| Strong | זְכֹר | זְכֹר | to remember, remembering |
| Strong | שְׁמֹר | שְׁמֹר | to keep, keeping |
| I-Guttural | עֲזֹב | עֲזֹב | to abandon, abandoning |
| I-א | אֱמֹר | אֱמֹר | to say, saying |
| II-Guttural | בְּחַר | בְּחֹר | to choose, choosing |
| III-ח | שְׁלַח | שְׁלֹחַ | to send, sending |
| III-ע | שְׁמַע | שְׁמֹעַ | to hear, hearing |
| III-א | קְרָא | קְרֹא | to call, calling |

---

## 20.3 Notes on the Qal Infinitive Construct of the Strong Verb (§20.3)

1. Imperfect verbs with a-class stem vowels (II-Guttural, III-ע/ח, and III-א) preserve that stem vowel in the Imperative but not in the Infinitive Construct. For example, the Imperative 2ms of the II-Guttural verb בָּחַר is בְּחַר (choose!), preserving the Pathach stem vowel of the Imperfect. The Infinitive Construct form (בְּחֹר) follows the standard Shewa-Holem vowel pattern instead. The same applies to III-ע/ח and III-א weak verbs.
2. I-Guttural and I-א verbal roots have a reduced vowel under the guttural in first root position (אֱמֹר and עֲזֹב). Guttural consonants cannot take Vocal Shewa.
3. Note the Furtive Pathach in שְׁלֹחַ and שְׁמֹעַ (III-ע/ח verbs). The stem vowel in these forms is still Holem.

---

## 20.4 III-ה Verbs (§20.4)

The Infinitive Construct form of III-ה verbs ends in וֹת. Summary of III-ה verbal endings:

| Conjugation | Ending | Example | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Imperfect | ֶה | יִבְנֶה | he will build |
| Imperative | ֵה | בְּנֵה | you (2ms) build! |
| Infinitive Construct | וֹת | בְּנוֹת | to build, building |

Every Infinitive Construct of III-ה verbs begins with a Vocal Shewa (or Hateph vowel with gutturals) under the first root consonant and ends in וֹת:

| Lexical Form | Infinitive Construct | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| בָּנָה | בְּנוֹת | to build, building |
| בָּכָה | בְּכוֹת | to weep, weeping |
| עָלָה | עֲלוֹת | to go up, going up |
| עָנָה | עֲנוֹת | to answer, answering |
| עָשָׂה | עֲשׂוֹת | to do, doing |
| הָיָה | הֱיוֹת | to be, being |
| רָאָה | רְאוֹת | to see, seeing |

## 20.5 I-נ Verbs (§20.5)

Many I-נ verbs have Infinitive Constructs that conform to the strong verb pattern with preservation of the נ (as in נְפֹל or נְסֹעַ). A number of I-נ verbs, however, have an alternate Infinitive Construct form in which the initial נ is lost and a ת is added to the end. Recognizing the diagnostic final ת on these verbal forms is essential for identification and root reconstruction.

| Verbal Root | Infinitive Construct with נ | Alternate Form with ת |
|---|---|---|
| נָסַע | נְסֹעַ | סַ֫עַת |
| נָגַע | נְגֹעַ | גַּ֫עַת |
| נָטַע | נְטֹעַ | טַ֫עַת |
| נָשָׂא | נְשֹׂא | שְׂאֵת (שֵׂאת) |
| נָגַשׁ | — | גֶּ֫שֶׁת |
| נָתַן | נְתֹן | תֵּת |

---

## 20.6 I-י Verbs (§20.6)

A few I-י verbs preserve the standard vowel pattern (יְשֹׁן from יָשַׁן). Most I-י verbs, however, drop the initial י and add a final ת, similar to the alternate form of I-נ verbs. The vowel pattern of these Infinitives with ת is generally reminiscent of Segholate nouns (accent on the first syllable).

| Lexical Form | Infinitive Construct |
|---|---|
| יָשַׁב | שֶׁ֫בֶת |
| יָרַשׁ | רֶ֫שֶׁת |
| יָדַע | דַּ֫עַת |
| יָצָא | צֵאת |
| הָלַךְ | לֶ֫כֶת |

---

## 20.7 Biconsonantal Verbs (§20.7)

Biconsonantal verbs are divided into three classes based on the medial vowel letter: קוּם with Shureq, בּוֹא with Holem Waw, and שִׂים with Hireq Yod. The lexical forms you have been memorizing are in fact the Infinitive Construct forms, which are also identical to the Biconsonantal Imperative 2ms. Context enables you to distinguish between the Imperative and Infinitive Construct.

| Lexical Form | Infinitive Construct |
|---|---|
| שׁוּב | שׁוּב |
| מוּת | מוּת |
| בּוֹא | בּוֹא |
| בּוֹשׁ | בּוֹשׁ |
| דִּין | דִּין |

---

## 20.8 Parsing the Infinitive Construct (§20.8)

The Infinitive Construct form is not inflected for person, gender, or number. When parsing, identify only stem, conjugation, and lexical form or verbal root:

כְּתֹב → Qal Infinitive Construct, כָּתַב

אֱמֹר → Qal Infinitive Construct, אָמַר

---

## 20.9 The Infinitive Construct with Pronominal Suffixes (§20.9)

The Infinitive Construct frequently appears with a pronominal suffix that can function as either the subject or object of the verbal idea. The initial closed syllable with Qamets Hatuf is diagnostic of the strong verb Infinitive Construct with pronominal suffixes.

| PGN | Suffix Form | As Subject | As Object |
|---|---|---|---|
| **3ms** | קָטְלוֹ | his killing | to kill/killing him |
| **3fs** | קָטְלָהּ | her killing | to kill/killing her |
| **2ms** | קָטְלְךָ | your killing | to kill/killing you |
| **2fs** | קָטְלֵךְ | your killing | to kill/killing you |
| **1cs** | קָטְלִי | my killing | to kill/killing me |
| **3mp** | קָטְלָם | their killing | to kill/killing them |
| **3fp** | קָטְלָן | their killing | to kill/killing them |
| **2mp** | קָטְלְכֶם | your killing | to kill/killing you |
| **2fp** | קָטְלְכֶן | your killing | to kill/killing you |
| **1cp** | קָטְלֵ֫נוּ | our killing | to kill/killing us |

## 20.10 The Infinitive Construct with Inseparable Prepositions (§20.10)

The inseparable prepositions בְּ, כְּ, and לְ may be prefixed to the Infinitive Construct:

- **לְ** is used to express purpose or result: לִזְכֹּר (in order to remember)
- **בְּ and כְּ** are used temporally and translate "when" or "while": בִּשְׁמֹר or כִּשְׁמֹר (when or while keeping)

Of all the verbal conjugations studied so far, only the Infinitive Construct takes a prepositional prefix — whenever you encounter a verbal form with a prepositional prefix, you can be confident it is an Infinitive Construct. The Infinitive Construct with a prefixed preposition can also take a pronominal suffix, as in כְּשָׁמְעוֹ, parsed as: Qal Infinitive Construct of שָׁמַע with preposition כְּ and 3ms pronominal suffix.

---

## 20.11 Negation of the Infinitive Construct (§20.11)

The negative particles לֹא and אַל are not used to negate the Infinitive Construct. Instead, the Hebrew Infinitive is negated with בִּלְתִּי ("not" or "in order not"). It also occurs as לְבִלְתִּי, with no difference in meaning. Both בִּלְתִּי קְטֹל and לְבִלְתִּי קְטֹל mean the same thing: "in order not to kill" or "not to kill."

---

## 20.12 The Use of the Infinitive Construct (§20.12)

Five common uses of the Infinitive Construct:

**1. Purpose, Intention, or Result.** When prefixed with לְ, the Infinitive Construct may denote purpose, intention, or result. Translate with "to" or "in order to."

> אֶת־בְּנוֹ לִשְׁחֹט וַיִּקַּח אֶת־הַמַּאֲכֶלֶת
>
> And he took the knife (in order) to slaughter his son (Gen 22:10).

> וְלֹא־נָתַן יְהוָה לָכֶם לֵב לָדַעַת וְעֵינַיִם לִרְאוֹת וְאָזְנַיִם לִשְׁמֹעַ עַד הַיּוֹם הַזֶּה
>
> But Yahweh has not given to you a heart to know or eyes to see or ears to hear until this day (Deut 29:3 [English 29:4]).

**2. Inceptive.** When prefixed with לְ, the Infinitive Construct may denote an action about to take place. Frequently occurs with the temporal modifiers וַיְהִי or וְהָיָה. Translate with "about to."

> לָבוֹא וַיְהִי הַשֶּׁמֶשׁ
>
> The sun was about to set (Gen 15:12).

> לִסְגּוֹר וַיְהִי הַשַּׁעַר
>
> The gate was about to shut (Josh 2:5).

**3. Verbal Noun.** The Infinitive Construct may function like a noun as the subject or object of the verbal idea. It may or may not be prefixed with לְ.

> מִזֶּבַח טוֹב שְׁמֹעַ הִנֵּה
>
> Behold, to obey (obeying) is better than sacrifice (1 Sam 15:22).

> בָּאָדָם מִבְּטֹחַ בַּיהוָה לַחֲסוֹת טוֹב
>
> To take refuge in Yahweh is better than trusting in man (Ps 118:8).

**4. Complementary.** The Infinitive Construct may explain, clarify, or complement a preceding action or statement. It may or may not be prefixed with לְ. Translate with "by" plus the "-ing" form of the verb.

> וְשָׂמְרוּ בְנֵי־יִשְׂרָאֵל אֶת־הַשַּׁבָּת לַעֲשׂוֹת אֶת־הַשַּׁבָּת לְדֹרֹתָם בְּרִית עוֹלָם
>
> The people of Israel shall observe the Sabbath by doing the Sabbath throughout their generations as an everlasting covenant (Exod 31:16).

> בִּדְרָכָיו לָלֶכֶת וְשָׁמַרְתָּ אֶת־מִצְוֹת יְהוָה אֱלֹהֶיךָ
>
> And you shall observe the commandments of Yahweh your God by walking in his ways (Deut 8:6).

**5. Temporal.** When prefixed with בְּ or כְּ, the Infinitive Construct may be used in a temporal clause. The prepositions translate "when" or "while." Frequently the Infinitive Construct in this construction also has a pronominal suffix.

Given that the Infinitive Construct has no tense, the temporal value must be taken from context. The form כְּשָׁמְעוֹ may be translated "when he heard" or "when he hears/will hear." The temporal modifiers וַיְהִי (past tense narration) and וְהָיָה (future tense narration) provide context for determining temporal value.

> יִשְׂרָאֵל בָּאָרֶץ הַהִוא בִּשְׁכֹּן וַיְהִי
>
> When (while) Israel dwelt in that land … (Gen 35:22)

> וְהָיָה כְּצֵאת מֹשֶׁה אֶל־הָאֹהֶל יָקוּמוּ כָּל־הָעָם וְנִצְּבוּ אִישׁ פֶּתַח אָהֳלֹו
>
> Whenever Moses went out to the tent, all the people rose up and every man stood at the door of his tent (Exod 33:8).

> אֵלֶּה הָעֵדֹת וְהַחֻקִּים וְהַמִּשְׁפָּטִים אֲשֶׁר דִּבֶּר מֹשֶׁה אֶל־בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל בְּצֵאתָם מִמִּצְרָיִם
>
> These are the testimonies and the statutes and the judgments which Moses spoke to the people of Israel when they came out from Egypt (Deut 4:45).

## 20.13 Summary (§20.13)

1. The Infinitive Construct is not inflected for person, gender, or number. In strong and many weak verbal roots, its basic form is identical to the Qal Imperative 2ms (קְטֹל).
2. Three weak verb classes have Infinitive Construct forms that vary significantly from the strong verb vowel pattern:
    a. III-ה verbs drop the final ה and add וֹת: בְּנוֹת from בָּנָה.
    b. Some I-נ verbs drop the initial נ and add ת: סַ֫עַת from נָסַע.
    c. Some I-י verbs drop the initial י and add ת: שֶׁ֫בֶת from יָשַׁב.
3. The Infinitive Construct may occur with prepositional prefixes, pronominal suffixes, or both:

| Form | Description |
|---|---|
| קְטֹל | basic form |
| קָטְלִי | basic form with pronominal suffix (1cs) |
| לִקְטֹל | basic form with prepositional prefix (לְ) |
| כְּקָטְלוֹ | basic form with prepositional prefix (כְּ) and pronominal suffix (3ms) |

4. The Infinitive Construct may occur with a pronominal suffix that can function as either the subject or object of the verbal idea.
5. The Infinitive Construct is negated with בִּלְתִּי or לְבִלְתִּי ("not" or "in order not").
6. Five common uses of the Infinitive Construct:
    a. **Purpose.** May appear with לְ to express purpose, intention, or result.
    b. **Inceptive.** May appear with לְ to denote an action about to take place.
    c. **Verbal Noun.** May appear with or without לְ as a verbal noun.
    d. **Complementary.** May appear with or without לְ to explain, clarify, or complement a preceding action or statement.
    e. **Temporal.** May appear with בְּ or כְּ in a temporal clause with or without pronominal suffixes.

## 20.15 Something You Should Know: A Life Centered on Torah (§20.15)

> The priest/scribe Ezra was a man whose life provided a model of godly leadership to a people in desperate need of hearing God's Word and seeing God's will lived out in practical ways.
>
> You have learned that the Infinitive Construct with the preposition לְ often complements the main verb by expressing the purpose, goal, or result to which the main verb points. In Ezra 7:10, there are three Infinitive Construct forms that are used in this way in a verse that highlights Ezra's personal commitments.
>
> The narrator has already introduced Ezra as a minister with the right pedigree (7:1–5) and the right professional abilities: he was a טֹ֫פֵר, a "scribe," "skilled in the Torah of Moses" (7:6; cf. 7:11). Ezra's every request was granted him by the Persian king and his ministry flourished "because the (good) hand of Yahweh his God was on him" (7:6, 9). Ezra 7:10 then provides the reason for this divine favor.
>
> כִּי עֶזְרָא הֵכִין לְבָבוֹ לִדְרוֹשׁ אֶת־תּוֹרַת יְהוָה וְלַעֲשֹׂת וּלְלַמֵּד בְּיִשְׂרָאֵל חֹק וּמִשְׁפָּט
>
> For Ezra set his heart to study the Torah of Yahweh, and to practice (it), and to teach (its) statutes and ordinances in Israel.
>
> Here we find the main verb הֵכִין (to set, establish) followed by three Infinitive Construct verbs with the preposition לְ (לִדְרוֹשׁ, לַעֲשֹׂת, לְלַמֵּד) each of which clarifies the purpose to which Ezra committed himself: study ➣ practice ➣ teach. This progression of action, grounded in God's Word, characterized Ezra's life and ministry. Sound study of the Scriptures must give rise to personal practice. Only then can we have a basis for effective teaching. But far too often preachers and teachers either lose the centrality of the Word in their ministry, or confuse the order of Ezra's resolve — some focus on teaching technique at the expense of quality time in the Word, while others proclaim God's truth but are slow to apply it to their own lives, resulting in hypocritical leaders who have forgotten that only the pure in heart will see God (Matt 5:8; cf. Ps 24:3–5; Heb 12:13). Still others apply before having studied, allowing their own definitions of right and wrong to guide conduct rather than the revealed divine will in the Scriptures.
>
> Ezra was serious about understanding God's Word, applying it, and proclaiming it — in that order! This personal commitment generated a ministry blessed by God. "The hand of our God works for good on all who seek him, but his powerful wrath is against all who forsake him" (Ezra 8:22).
>
> —Jason S. DeRouchie, Professor of Old Testament and Biblical Theology, Bethlehem College and Seminary
